Frame 1: HOW TO READ A RESEARCH PAPER (white Newsreader on a black box, over her)

1. Hook [20.10-24.48] + [35.20-44.85] (one idea: papers are not read front to back)
   "Research papers are not written to be read from start to finish, like a novel."
   "Most research papers are built from the same parts. And you need to know what each part is for, so that you can stop
   reading it like one big block of text."

2. The idea [124.27-131.70] + [135.50-140.70]
   "One of the best known methods for reading research paper comes from a two-page paper called How to Read a Paper. It was
   written by S. Keshav." "In his word, you should read the paper in up to three passes, and each pass has a different job."

Card: PASS ONE (5-10 minutes)
3. [156.95-165.40] "Pass one of Keshav's three-pass method takes about five to ten minutes and it also gives you the order to
   read the paper in."
4. [232.88-241.95] "A lot of time after those five, ten minutes you would find that maybe the paper is not relevant to what
   you're doing. At this point you stop, save yourself a whole evening."

Card: PASS TWO (up to an hour)
5. [256.88-262.86] "Now pass two, the second pass of Keshav's method for reading research paper takes up to an hour."
6. [309.25-317.40] "So by the time you're at the end of the second pass, you should be able to explain the main point of the
   paper and the evidence for it to someone else."

Card: PASS THREE (in depth)
7. [325.95-334.52] "The third pass of Keshav's method for reading research papers is for papers that you need to understand
   completely. The ones that, for example, your thesis is built on."
8. [372.63-383.50, cut 376.65-378.15] "And the third part takes time. According to the person that started this method, it
