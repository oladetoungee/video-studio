HTTP doesn't remember you
Request 1: log in
Request 2: My Orders
Server: who is this?

Logging in and clicking My Orders are two separate requests. So the browser has to remind the server it's still you.

02 / 10
Cookies
A cookie works like a cloakroom ticket
Cookie
117

The staff don't remember your face, they use your ticket number. Your browser shows its cookie on every request to that site the same way.

03 / 10
Cookies are tiny
4 KB
per cookie, and it goes with every request to that site

Browsers must allow at least 4,096 bytes per cookie. Source: RFC 6265, section 6.1.

04 / 10
Your login cookie usually holds just an ID
Cookie: session_id=8f3a1c77

The server keeps the real details about you and uses that ID to look them up, the same way the cloakroom uses the number to find your coat.

05 / 10
localStorage
localStorage is why your cart is still there tomorrow
Still there
Browser closed

localStorage stays until your code removes it or you clear your browser data. Closing the tab, or the whole browser, doesn't clear it.

06 / 10
localStorage holds a lot more than a cookie
5 MB
per site

It varies by browser. localStorage only stores strings, and it never goes to the server unless your code sends it. Source: MDN, Storage quotas.
