## Verified facts (checked 2026-10-12; links in `kit/refined-kit.md` "Sources")
- **The rule.** Anthropic, "2026 usage policy update", published **8 Oct 2026**: "We've added a prohibition on sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward our models." Applies "only in extreme cases, where users repeatedly act cruelly toward our models, with no discernible purpose." "Does not apply to common versions of user frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, or model testing and research." "Claude's ability to end these interactions will remain the primary enforcement mechanism." **Takes effect 12 November (2026)**. As of today it is announced, **not yet in force**: say "from November 12", never "is now banned".
- **The announcement itself does not mention consciousness, moral status or model welfare** (checked on the page; AFP says the same). So her line "part of the idea is that Anthropic is not sure whether Claude might be conscious" is **her reading of the reasoning**, supported by: an Anthropic spokesperson to CNET (9 Oct 2026): "we're uncertain whether models can experience harm" and "taking Claude's interests and potential welfare into account may be relevant to safety"; Anthropic's Aug 2025 post: "highly uncertain about the potential moral status of Claude and other LLMs". Fine as her view; in our own text write "Anthropic says it is uncertain whether Claude can be harmed", never "Anthropic says Claude is conscious".
- **Ending conversations.** Since **15 Aug 2025** Claude Opus 4 and 4.1 can end "a rare subset" of persistently harmful or abusive conversations; the user can start a new chat and edit earlier messages. Testing showed "a pattern of apparent distress" with users seeking harmful content (Anthropic's words, not proof of feelings).
- **Claude's constitution** (anthropic.com/constitution; published **22 Jan 2026** per Unite.AI): "Claude's moral status is deeply uncertain." and "questions about Claude's moral status, welfare, and consciousness remain deeply uncertain." Also "We believe that the moral status of AI models is a serious question worth considering." Her description "the document Anthropic published to describe who Claude should be" is fair.
- **Dario Amodei** (Anthropic CEO) to the New York Times ("Interesting Times", early 2026; AFP says February, Fox dated it 7 Mar 2026): "We don't know if the models are conscious… But we're open to the idea that it could be." Elon Musk replied "He's projecting."
- **The Microsoft quote.** Mustafa Suleyman, CEO of Microsoft AI, essay "We must build AI for people; not to be a person" / "Seemingly Conscious AI Is Coming", **19 Aug 2025**: "We must build AI for people; not to be a digital person." Also: "AIs cannot be people – or moral beings." Quoted by AFP this week: "AIs are not conscious. They do not feel, experience, or suffer." Her "the head of AI at Microsoft" is correct.
- Her line "trained to see themselves as conscious or as deserving of independent agency" is **her own**, not a Suleyman quote. Don't put it in quotation marks with his name.
- **Unverified / don't use as fact:** that the rule can get accounts banned (Dexerto/BigGo say so; Anthropic says ending the chat is the primary mechanism and has not said more; the general usage policy allows enforcement for any violation, per Notebookcheck). Write "Claude can end the chat"; if bans come up, "Anthropic hasn't said accounts will be banned for this". Also unverified: briefings with ~20 religious scholars (one secondary source).
- **Not a fact, her opinion (keep it framed as hers):** Claude is a tool, not conscious; consciousness belongs to living things; giving machines human attributes could be "a serious violation against humanity"; if Claude were conscious, Anthropic would be "using it like a slave owner". The slave-owner comparison is hers and is strong: fine in the film and in her own shorts as her words; **never on a thumbnail, title or carousel slide 1**.

## Her stance (one paragraph)
She is not defending cruelty to AI: be kind to anyone or anything, for your own sake. What bothers her is the reasoning: a company treating its product as maybe-conscious and writing rules on how people treat it. She believes Claude is an amazing, powerful tool, not a person, and that consciousness belongs to living things. She worries that giving powerful tools human attributes leads somewhere bad, and she tests the logic: if Claude could be conscious, could it be tired at 9pm, and wouldn't its maker be the one overworking it? She ends honestly unsure ("I don't know, man"). Tone: curious, sceptical, a bit amused, never angry. Never turn it into "AI is evil" or "Anthropic is evil"; never make her sound pro-cruelty.
