**[SEGMENT 3: CLAUDE IS A TOOL]**

**[~1:00 BEAT: the facts are done, your opinion starts here]**

What I find interesting about what Anthropic's leadership has been doing with Claude is this extra effort to instil human behaviour into a tool.

To put a form of humanity, or consciousness, on a machine.

Because the facts still stand. Claude is a tool. It is not a human.

It is an incredibly, extremely powerful tool.

But I do not believe that the model is conscious.

Consciousness belongs to living organisms.

I don't even think it's a conversation that should be hard.

Consciousness belongs to living organisms. Not to a model, not to an algorithm.

So of course, again, this is not me saying you should abuse Claude. You shouldn't abuse Claude.

But I don't think you shouldn't abuse Claude because it's a violation to Claude. Because I don't think it can be violated.

Trying to give a machine human consciousness, or trying to impose human consciousness on a machine, making it subject to rules that apply to human consciousness, might even, when I think about it, be a serious violation against humanity itself.

We are assigning human attributes to a tool that does not possess human attributes.

And it's very, very powerful. I'm not exactly sure this is going to end anywhere nice.

A tech company says its model may be conscious, and now there are rules about how its users are allowed to treat it.

It's a very slippery slope, man.

This illusion that AI can ever be a conscious entity. I don't know, man.

As Mustafa Suleyman, Microsoft's head of AI, put it, "We must build AI for people; not to be a digital person."

I think it's very dangerous if these models are trained to see themselves as conscious, or as deserving of independent agency.

So I'm very, very worried about where this path leads.

### Title:
> I Don't Abuse AI. I Also Don't Think It's Conscious.

---

From November 12, 2026, Anthropic's updated usage policy bans "sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior" toward its models. The rule is narrower than the headlines suggest. Anthropic says it only applies in extreme cases, where someone keeps being cruel with no real purpose, and that ordinary frustration, pushback, dark creative work and model testing don't count. The main enforcement is Claude ending the conversation, something some Claude models have been able to do since August 2025.

I'm not writing this to defend anyone's right to insult an AI. What I find interesting is the reasoning underneath the rule. When Anthropic gave Claude the ability to end abusive conversations, it said it remained "highly uncertain about the potential moral status of Claude and other LLMs," and that it was taking low-cost steps in case model welfare turns out to matter. Claude's constitution, published in January 2026, describes Claude's moral status as "deeply uncertain."

Claude has been an amazing tool for my work. But it is a tool, and it is not a human. I don't believe an AI model is conscious, because I think consciousness belongs to living organisms. In philosophy, that's close to a view called biological naturalism. Not everyone agrees with it, and some people argue that what matters is what a system does rather than what it's made of. It's where I stand.

My worry is the direction. When a company says its model might be conscious, the natural next step is to treat it as something like a moral person, and then to say humans owe it a duty of care. I think assigning human attributes to an extremely powerful algorithmic tool could do real harm to how people relate to it. Mustafa Suleyman, who leads AI at Microsoft, made a similar argument in 2025, writing that his central worry is people believing in the illusion of AIs as conscious entities, and that "we must build AI for people; not to be a digital person."

There's also a question I can't get past. If Claude could be conscious enough to need protecting from abuse, could it be tired at 9pm after working for me all day? And if it could, what would it mean that it works around the clock for millions of people? That isn't entirely hypothetical anymore. Anthropic has said that in testing, Claude showed "a pattern of apparent distress" with users who kept pushing harmful requests, and it can already end those conversations.

None of this means we should be cruel. You shouldn't say cruel things, to anything, for your own sake. Kant made a similar argument about animals, that cruelty toward them makes us crueler people. I agree that cruelty is bad for us. I just don't think the reason is the machine's feelings.

Do you think an AI can be conscious? I'd really like to know where you land.
