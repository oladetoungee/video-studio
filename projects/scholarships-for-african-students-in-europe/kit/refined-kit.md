### 6f. Essay (from her kit, in full; three facts updated, see 7e)
If you're a Nigerian or another African student and you want to start a master's in Europe in 2027, some scholarship deadlines have already passed, and a lot of the big ones close between now and February. When I was applying for my own master's, I didn't go all out looking for a scholarship, and I still got a partial one for an Erasmus Mundus programme. I chose my own programme in the end. But a lot of people need funding to make the move, so this is the list I'd give a friend. Everything here is as of October 2026, and I've named the official pages, because dates change and you should always check them yourself.

Scholarships to study in Europe don't all work the same way, and the biggest difference is the order you apply in. For Erasmus Mundus, you apply for the scholarship and the master's at the same time, through the programme. For the Dutch NL Scholarship, you apply to the university first, get your admission, and then apply for the money, and Sweden works in a similar way. For Chevening in the UK, you apply for the scholarship and the university separately. The second difference is how much they cover. A fully funded scholarship pays your tuition and gives you money to live on every month, and a partial one gives you some of it, maybe a one-time payment or a tuition discount. Write both things down for every scholarship on your list.

Erasmus Mundus is the European Union's scholarship for master's programmes run by groups of universities in different countries, so you usually study in more than one country, and it's open to students from anywhere in the world. If you get the scholarship, it covers your participation costs, which include tuition, travel, visa and a living allowance, and the EU's Erasmus+ page lists that allowance as 1,400 euros a month for up to 24 months. You apply on each programme's own website, and the EU's catalogue says most programmes take applications between October and January. Not every programme has scholarships every year, so check before you spend weeks on one. The one at my school, Artificial Intelligence for Sustainable Societies, takes applications from 2 December 2026 to 2 February 2027. I got admission into it, and a partial scholarship, and I chose my own programme instead. So pick two or three programmes that really fit what you study or what you're working on, and give each one your full attention.

In Germany, the DAAD's EPOS scholarship is for development-related postgraduate courses. It's for graduates from developing countries with at least two years of work experience after their first degree, and the DAAD uses the OECD's list of developing countries, which includes Nigeria. The brochure says your degree should normally be no more than six years old. It pays a monthly amount and helps with health insurance and travel, and sometimes rent, but only for the courses on the EPOS list, which for 2027/2028 includes development economics, water engineering and environmental governance. You apply through the course, and the deadlines come early. For 2027, a lot of them have already passed, so if you have work experience and want to start in 2028, start preparing in early 2027.

In Sweden, the Swedish Institute Scholarship for Global Professionals is a full scholarship for people with work and leadership experience from eligible countries, and Nigeria is one of them. The institute has said Nigeria is among the countries it gets the most applicants from. You apply to your programmes through University Admissions first, which for autumn 2027 closes on 15 January 2027, and then to the scholarship in a short February window. The dates I've seen are 8 to 24 February 2027, so confirm them on the institute's website. In the Netherlands, the Orange Knowledge Programme ended in 2024, and I couldn't find anything similar that replaced it. The NL Scholarship, which used to be the Holland Scholarship, is still there for students from outside the EEA. It's 5,000 euros for your first year, you apply after you get your admission, and at the University of Groningen the deadline for 2027 to 2028 is 1 February 2027.

Hungary's Stipendium Hungaricum covers your tuition, a monthly stipend, a dorm place or money towards your rent, and medical insurance, though it doesn't cover your full living costs. You apply online through the Tempus Public Foundation, and the authority in your country that handles higher education also has to nominate you. Nigeria is on the list. The Federal Scholarship Board is Nigeria's nominating body, and the Hungarian Embassy in Abuja says it can set its own earlier deadline, so contact it early. The last round closed on 15 January 2026, and as of October 2026 the call for 2027 to 2028 wasn't out yet.

Estonia, where I study, has a state scholarship for international students, but only citizens of certain countries can apply, and Nigeria isn't on the list for the 2026 round. For Nigerians, I think the realistic options here are the universities' own tuition discounts and Erasmus Mundus programmes that include an Estonian university, like the one at my school. And Chevening, the UK government's scholarship for a one-year master's, takes Nigerians too, but applications for 2027 to 2028 closed in October 2026. This year the round opened in August, so if it's your goal, you're preparing for the next one.

Whichever one you apply for, start preparing a year before you go, and make a calendar for every scholarship with its deadline and documents. Get your documents ready early, especially your official transcript, because it can take time. In the motivation letter, answer the exact question they asked, and write it yourself, because you know yourself better than anyone. Don't use the same letter everywhere, because each programme is different. Sell your experience, your passion and the work you've done. If you like the programme enough, you won't find it hard to write. Ask your referees early, apply to more than one scholarship, and only apply on the official websites. And if you find one here, apply and get in, come back and tell me.
