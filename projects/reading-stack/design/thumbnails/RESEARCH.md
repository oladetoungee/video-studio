## 2. Platform facts (YouTube, official)

- **Test & Compare** tests up to **3 thumbnails, 3 titles, or 3 title+thumbnail pairs**. It is in desktop Studio only, needs advanced features, and isn't available for Shorts, made-for-kids or private videos.
  - YouTube: "We optimize tests for overall **watch time** over other metrics, like click-through-rate." A test takes "a few days or up to 2 weeks".
  - Outcomes are *Winner*, *Performed same* or *Inconclusive*.
  - Sources: [support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714), verified; also [answer/16391400](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400).
  - Title testing rolled out globally in Dec 2025 ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-title-a-b-testing-rolls-out-globally-to-creators/562571/)).
  - What this means: a thumbnail that wins clicks but misrepresents the video loses the test.
- **Specs:** 16:9. Upload limit **2 MB** on mobile and 50 MB on desktop. [answer/72431](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431)
- **Policy:** a thumbnail that implies content the video doesn't contain breaks the deceptive-practices policy. [answer/7002331](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7002331)
- **Display:** the duration badge sits bottom-right, and a red progress bar runs along the bottom of partly watched videos. Desktop sidebar thumbnails are about **168x94** ([postfa.st](https://postfa.st/sizes/youtube/thumbnail)).

## 3. Practitioner consensus (opinion)

- **Paddy Galloway:**
  - Pass the "glance test", and judge title and thumbnail as one unit: do they "compliment or repeat?" ([X post](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1811083499044044951))
  - At most 3 focus areas and under 5 words; make 3 or more thumbnails ([summary](https://www.marketingexamined.com/blog/paddy-galloway-youtube-guide)).
- **1of10:** in education, "it's the packaging and text that drive the click, not the face." ([blog](https://1of10.com/blog/should-you-use-your-face-in-your-youtube-thumbnails/))
- **YouTube's own tips:** use colour and composition "but too much can overwhelm", and readable fonts. ([answer/12340300](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300))

## 6. Rules applied to these thumbnails

1. **Text length: 2-5 words**, three lines at most, set at 110-170 px on a 1280 canvas. Every word must read at 168x94. Test with the sidebar render, not the full size.
2. **Face:** when used, it fills **35-45% of the frame height**, sits on the right, and the expression matches the hook. Use calm eye contact, mild concern, or a real smile. No manufactured shock, because the evidence says shock faces are rare and a closed mouth often wins. Her face stays **mirrored** as filmed. Only book-cover text is un-mirrored, block by block (`tools/prep.py`).
3. **Contrast:** use one flat ground from her palette and one accent colour per thumbnail. No gradients, glows or outlines.
4. **Focal point:** one dominant subject (face, a contents page, or a giant number) plus one text block. That's at most 3 focus areas.
5. **Curiosity gap:** the thumbnail states the counter-intuitive *move* ("Read the contents first", "Don't start on page 1") or the *problem* ("You only remember the vibe"). **The title states the outcome** ("How I remember every non-fiction book I read").
6. **Complement, don't repeat:** no thumbnail shares more than one content word with its paired title.
7. **Keep the bottom-right corner (about 150x70 px) and the bottom edge clear** of words and faces, because of the duration badge and progress bar.
8. **No jargon in titles.** "Priming" and "inspectional reading" stay inside the video; at most "1940" appears in a thumbnail as a curiosity hook.
9. **Honesty:**
   - Every chapter title shown is one she reads aloud in the video.
   - The circled chapter really is chapter 1.
   - Book spines are labelled as illustrations in `content.json`.
   - No promise the video doesn't keep: "it actually stuck" is her claim in the recap.
10. **Avoid:**
    - the banned list in CLAUDE.md
    - arrows, emoji, red circles around faces
    - AI imagery
    - stock photos
    - text over her face
    - more than one accent colour

## 7. Standalone hooks (added 2026-10-01, after her feedback)

Her rule: a stranger who has never heard of her must understand thumbnail + title in a glance. On a small channel almost every impression in Browse and Suggested is a cold viewer.

- **Clarity beats manufactured curiosity in education.** *Opinion, consistent across 2026 guides.* Thumbnails that say clearly what the video covers and who it's for beat vague curiosity in educational and professional niches; exaggerated faces can lower trust there ([vidIQ 2026 tips](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-design-tips/), [Brightbean](https://brightbean.xyz/blog/youtube-thumbnail-best-practices/)). The niche's winners say it plainly: "How to Remember Everything You Read" (Justin Sung, 10M), "How I Remember Everything I Read" (Ali Abdaal).
- **YouTube is starting to serve different thumbnails to different audiences.** *Official, announced.* At Made on YouTube (2026-09-23) YouTube showed "dynamic thumbnails" that match one of three options to audience segments; Paddy Galloway reads it as different packaging for new vs returning viewers ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/09/23/made-on-youtube-2026-ai-personalization-creator-tools/), [Paddy Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/2102774246480982355)). Rollout date not given. Until then, Test & Compare picks one winner for everyone, so every option must work cold.
- **The cold-read test.** *Our rule.* A thumbnail passes only if a stranger, shown the image and the title and nothing else, can say what the video teaches. Inside phrases ("only the vibe", "page 1", "build the shelves", "like it's 1940") fail it, however good they are once you've watched.

**Rules applied in the rewrite**
1. Each thumbnail is one of: the **outcome** ("Remember what you read"), the **problem** ("Stop forgetting books"), the **method stated plainly** ("Read the contents first", with the contents page visible), or a **before/after**.
2. The title carries what the thumbnail leaves out (thumbnail = method, title = outcome, or the reverse), and both read cold.
3. Nothing the video doesn't show: no "5-minute" (she says "a few minutes"), no "remember everything".
