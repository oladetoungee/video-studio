A 4-step system for non-fiction. A few minutes per book.

01 / 10
“So what was that book about?”

Weeks after finishing a book for work or study, you can give the gist and one or two points. Not the argument.

You keep a feeling about the book,
not the book itself.
02 / 10
Ideas stick when they
have a place to go.
Start at page 1
Structure first
A new idea arrives
Nowhere to file it.You know which part it belongs to.
By chapter six
A pile of floating ideas.Each idea has somewhere to go.
What’s left
A feeling about the book.The shape of the argument.
03 / 10
01
Step 1 of 4
Decode the title.

Example: Convivial Toolbox.
Look up the word you don’t know.

Convivial

A term from philosopher Ivan Illich: tools that give power back to the person using them.

My guess before page one: it argues research tools should be usable by ordinary people, not just experts.

04 / 10
02
Step 2 of 4
Map the contents.

Chapters, sub-headings, even page counts. Then group them into blocks.

Same book, pages per part
Part 1
Underlying principles
~80 pp
Part 2
Cases from practice
~50 pp
Part 3
Tools and techniques
140+ pp
A manual masquerading as a textbook: the theory up front is there to make the tools make sense.
05 / 10
03
Step 3 of 4
Memorise it.

Learn the chapter titles by heart. Sub-headings too, if you can.

Skimming the headings helps. Memorising them sticks: it makes the map stronger in my mind.

An old idea: Mortimer Adler’s inspectional reading (How to Read a Book, 1940). Others call it priming.

06 / 10
04
Step 4 of 4
Guess, then read
to check.

From Convivial Toolbox: the last chapter, “Bridging”.

Its sub-headings
Q1Where are the gaps?
Q2Where and when does bridging happen?
Q3Who is bridging the gap?
Q4What are the ways we can bridge the gap?

My guess: a “gap” problem sits at the heart of this book. So when “gap” turns up early, I’ll notice. Read cold, I’d have missed it.

07 / 10
Where it
pays off.
01
Thesis and research
Dense sources you’ll cite or build on.
02
Textbooks and manuals
Anything with a real contents page.
03
Books you’ll have to explain
When someone asks what it was about.

Skip it for fiction. With a novel, not knowing what comes next is the point.
