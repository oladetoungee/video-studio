## 1. Topic, in one line a stranger would recognise

**How to read a non-fiction book so you actually remember it: study the contents page and chapter titles first, guess what the book will argue, then read.** Shown on two real books (Baratta's *How to Read and Write Critically* and Sanders and Stappers' *Convivial Toolbox*).

**What the winners share**
- **Titles name the reader's problem in "you" words**: "Why You Can't Explain What You Read", "...Don't Understand...", "studying is useless if you can't remember it", "WHY YOUR READING IS WASTED". Or the plain promise "How to Remember / Understand ... You Read". The *remember / understand / explain* angle beats *read more books* this year (Ali Abdaal's "read more" title at 0.46x; the forgetting and understanding titles at 6-1,099x).
- A **named method** helps: "Feynman's Method", "2 Skills Nobody Taught You", "The John Locke Rule", "One Habit". Her equivalent: a 1940 method (Adler) she now uses.
- Title length: mostly 35-60 characters.
- **Thumbnails: the book itself is the picture.** Open books, highlighted pages, a coffee and glasses on a desk (Closed Stacks: faceless top-down desk shots with 2-3 plain white words, "Now Explain It", "you're missing this", "Before Page One"); book stacks around the person (Ryan Holiday, Enzo Rendina). Faces are calm, chin-on-hand or looking at the book; no shocked faces except KYNID. Text 2-4 words.
