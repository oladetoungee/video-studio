### 6f. Essay (from her kit, in full; facts re-checked, see 7e)
I have a full-time job as a full-stack software developer here in Estonia, and I'm also doing a full-time master's degree here. I started my master's in September 2025, a month later I got my job, and this month makes it one year of doing both. That's not all I do either, because I make videos, I do church stuff, and I try to hang out with my friends and enjoy life a little bit. Working while studying, I swear, is the real ghetto. Once Monday starts for me, it keeps running until Friday, sometimes until Saturday.

Before any tips, you have to confirm if international students can work where you are studying. In Estonia, Study in Estonia lists working hours for international students as not limited, and the Police and Border Guard Board says you can work alongside your studies as long as it doesn't interfere with them and you keep studying full-time. Full-time study in Estonia means around 30 ECTS credits a semester. At my university, Tallinn University, they check it every two semesters, so after your first year you need at least 45 credits. If you fall below that, you're moved to part-time study, and that can result in your study permit being revoked. That's as of October 2026, and every school sets its own rules.

When I got the job, I set myself a very ambitious goal. I wanted to do work so well that work does not know that school exists, and do school so well that school does not know that work exists. So far so good, I think I've done that in a reasonable manner, even though there have been days of almost running mad. These are the things that help.

The first is to understand your credits at the start of every semester, before it gets busy. Most of my courses explain in the first class how they'll assess you, so on that same day you know the hours and the grading, and you can put every class, deadline and exam date in your calendar. If you're working full-time, use that to choose courses that won't overwhelm you. Then plan your whole degree. I had most of my non-thesis credits cleared early, so next semester is mostly my thesis. Give big things more time than you think, too. In a 1994 study, Roger Buehler, Dale Griffin and Michael Ross asked psychology students how long their senior thesis would take. They said about 34 days on average, it took about 55, and only about 30% finished in the time they predicted.

For the week itself, start with what can't move, like classes, deadlines and work meetings, and then give study its own block. Cal Newport calls this time blocking. Most of my classes are in the evening, so my mornings and afternoons go to work, and study gets a block like "Tuesday, 7 to 9 in the evening, read two papers for Thursday's class." If you only study when you have free time, and you work and study full-time, you won't study, because you rarely have free time. It also helps to get as much as you can in class, since you're already there. When I don't pay attention in class, and I do that sometimes, I end up spending more time on it later.

Keep work and school on the same calendar. If they live in two places, you find the clash when it's already too late. Then every Sunday evening, look at last week and next week, at work and at school, and move your study blocks around releases and deadlines. Right now I'm already planning around December, because I have a big practical course with real clients that month.

Your vacation days will go to school too, and I know that sounds unfair. In Estonia you can also ask your employer for study leave, up to 30 calendar days a year, and for university studies 20 of those days are paid at your average salary. Carry people along. Tell your manager early, write to your lecturer as soon as you see a clash coming, and if you're planning ahead, you'll usually see the clash before it arrives. Be honest about what kind of job you have, too. I don't have to be at the office, which is a privilege, so I can work around the clock. I don't have the work-life balance I want, but I'm trying. A fixed-hours, customer-facing job is a different situation, and your study blocks have to go around it.

The last two are about your body. Meal prepping will save your life. I used to complain about spending four or five hours on it, but it guarantees me food for the rest of the week, and now I don't negotiate with it, especially because I like to eat African food. And protect your sleep. I'm saying that as someone who doesn't sleep well and spends too many nights on a laptop. In 2019, researchers at MIT tracked the sleep of 88 students in a chemistry class, and the ones who slept longer, better and more regularly tended to get better grades. You can't have a perfect work-life balance when you're doing two big things together. It is what it is, and the major goal is to stay on top of things.
