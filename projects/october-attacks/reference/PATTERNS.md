Written 2026-11-01 from the finished files, before the MP4s went to the Bin. The video was posted on 2026-10-26:
https://youtu.be/E5Hj7IHSN2Y. Topic: a news recap of developer security in October 2026 (PHP2Shell, Railway, Zeabur,
OpenCode, eMudhra, one hacker using AI agents) and what to do about it.

**The MP4s in `outputs/` were moved to the Bin on 2026-11-01.** What can still be made again:

## The look: "A · The Month" with B's keys

She chose direction A ("The Month") with the pictures from direction B ("The Key Board") mixed in: "I love that B is so pictorial… the key parts, that storytelling, seeing the key move, drop" (FEEDBACK.md 10-26). Direction critic scores before her pick: A 7.5, B 6, C 5.5.

- **Idea**: the video opens with "October is supposed to be Cybersecurity Awareness Month", so the whole film is that month: an October 2026 wall calendar. Attacked days turn black one by one with their story written in them (14 and 15 Zeabur, 17 eMudhra, 19 OpenCode, 21 PHP2Shell and Railway). The camera dives into a day and the story plays inside it, with the big date number in the corner ("21 WED 21 OCTOBER 2026").
- **Three flat grounds**, one accent per scene:
  - October orange `#F2541B` (the month, punchlines: counts, $50, the pattern)
  - night `#16110D` (inside a day; also the ink colour)
  - bone `#EFE7D8` (explainers, diagrams, the to-do list)
  - ink on orange; orange on night and on bone. No gradients, no glow.
- **Fonts**: Anton (all-caps headlines and big numbers), Manrope 700/800 (sentences), Spline Sans Mono 500/600 (date labels, code, source credits). All OFL 1.1, added to `library/fonts` for this video. Her captions stay Helvetica Neue.
- **Drawn objects** (from B, redrawn in A's palette): keys on a rail, keys swapped / taken / dropping / stamped, a door, a shop shutter, a padlock, a generic browser, a terminal, square-tile grids (1 square = 10 sites, 100 squares = the web).
- **Real sources**: white clippings of the real articles with a light orange highlighter on the line she says, and a mono credit "OUTLET · DATE" under each.

## The motion film

- **Length**: 8:00.2 (480.24 s: 0.3 s intro, 477.4 s of her tightened voice, 2.5 s outro). Her narration had one retake cut and three pauses shortened (2.3 s in all, `film/data/cutlist.json`).
- **Scenes**: 58 (all listed with times in `film/HANDOFF.md`), each drawn for 16:9 and 9:16 (portrait content kept inside y 370-1440). 57 cuts, each a hard cut, whip or camera dive with a written reason. Average scene about 8.3 s; longest `before` (18 s), shortest `login` (3.2 s).
- **How a story is introduced**: back to the orange month, the day is marked (circled, a name label lands, or a black square fills), then the camera dives into that day. Inside, the scene opens with the big orange date number + mono date line + story name in Anton (21 PHP2Shell, 21 Railway, 14 Zeabur, 19 OpenCode, 17 eMudhra). The month comes back six times as the "chapter" screen (`month`, `overflow`, `busy`, `one-more`, `ai-sides`, `end`).
- **Signature moves**:
  - days going black one by one on "one attack after the other"; the 21 circled as "the biggest one"
  - the camera dive into a calendar square
  - keys: old keys drop off a rail and new orange ones swing in (Railway), the one orange admin key that is never swapped (Zeabur), keys taken from a cabinet (OpenCode), one key stamped 4 and grown big (eMudhra), the five "what you can do" key pictures
  - strike-through on what can't help (login, middleware, auth; "update the package: NOT ENOUGH")
  - count-ups on orange (321,810 accounts, 3.2 million records; 9.2 out of 10 meter)
  - tile grids filling (75 of 100 squares = nearly three quarters of the web; 11 of 120 tiles taken = 100+ of 1,200+ sites)
  - the ending: each black day turns into its object (keys, a 4, a jagged hole for PHP), the month steps back and a blank NOVEMBER 2026 page comes in
- **Real source captures**: 58 real pages were captured on 2026-10-26 (`assets/sources/`, 197 MB: a fold PNG, a full-page JPG and a scroll recording per page, highlight boxes in `sources.json`, "Facts to flag" at the top of `SOURCES.md`). The film uses **27 clippings** cut from them (`film/assets/src/`, listed under `images` in `film/film.json`): headline crops and single-line quote crops, never edited, highlighted on her words, credit under each, checked for cut letters and body text under 28 px. A headlines wall of six real headlines (scene `news`) closes the AI part. Fact updates found after recording went on screen as small cards (Railway "Update, 22 Oct: at most 1.85 %"; government sites "taken offline as a precaution: no breach reported"; $50 = "a researcher's estimate").
- **Checks at hand-off**: 0 dead holds, 0 blank cuts, 0 contrast failures, 0 linear moves or dead stops, 0 text-on-text overlaps after her review.

- **Teaser (6.5)**: the weakest video piece. Long stretches are her talking head on its own (about half the sheet frames), the bites are close-ups that look alike, and the film inserts are short. Aim for more variety in her framing and stronger act cards.
- **Shorts (6.9)**: several open on her talking for 4-6 s with no graphic (the shorts check lists "holds" at 0.2-6 s in most of them), sh03 and sh07 used one layout only, and 3 shorts measured slightly over the face-size target (sh06 0.311, sh07 0.312, sh10 0.323 vs 0.30). Real page crops in the top panel were sometimes small, dense body text.

- **Motion film (7.6)**: the round-3 fixes were never re-scored, and the files in outputs were exported before her review fixes (the 9:16 opening collision is visible in this record). Re-score after fixes and before she exports.
