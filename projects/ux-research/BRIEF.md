## Verified facts (checked 2026-10-12)
- **Definition of UX**: "'User experience' encompasses all aspects of the end-user's interaction with the company, its services, and its products." Don Norman and Jakob Nielsen, Nielsen Norman Group (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/definition-user-experience/). She paraphrases this correctly.
- **Don Norman at Apple: early 1990s (1993), NOT the eighties.** NN/g's bio: he joined Apple as an Apple Fellow and was "User Experience Architect" of the "User Experience Office", the first use of the term in a job title (https://www.nngroup.com/people/don-norman/; Wikipedia agrees, with a citation-needed tag). "Popularised" is safe; "coined" is not. **She says "back in the eighties" (re-transcribed at 1:56 with whisper small.en: "eighties").** Fix options for her: cut "back in the eighties" from the film narration (the line still works: "popularised by Don Norman when he was at Apple"), or show "early 1990s" on screen. Never show "1980s".
- **The six category pairs** (generative/evaluative, qualitative/quantitative, attitudinal/behavioural, primary/secondary, remote/in-person, moderated/unmoderated): standard; Maze UX research guide (https://maze.co/guides/ux-research/) and NN/g "When to Use Which UX Research Methods" (Rohrer, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/) cover them.
- **Say vs do**: a core NN/g principle (attitudinal vs behavioural, same Rohrer article). Safe.
- **"Five people"**: echoes Nielsen's "Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users" (2000, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/), which is about qualitative usability tests run in rounds. She uses "five people" as a low-budget starting point, not as Nielsen's claim; don't attribute it to Nielsen in her captions.
- **UX research vs user research vs market research**: Maze treats user research as the parent; IxDF notes user research can cover pricing or delivery (https://ixdf.org/literature/topics/ux-research). Her framing matches.
- **AI in UX research**: IBM Research preprint, Candello et al., arXiv 2512.15944, 17 Dec 2025: UX researchers "expressed limited trust in AI-generated results"; product managers "often overestimated AI capabilities" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15944). Preprint, not peer reviewed. Not in her recording.
- **Job market (description/carousel only, as "one tracker counted")**: AcademyUX, published 15 Sep 2026: US August 2026, **752 UX research postings (−10.2% month on month) vs 17,339 product design postings** (https://blog.academyux.com/ux-job-market-update-august-2026-us-heat-score-at-68/). ITJobsWatch UK: permanent jobs citing UX research 103 → 64 → 59 (six months to Jan 2024 / 2025 / 2026) (https://www.itjobswatch.co.uk/jobs/uk/ux%20research.do). These back up what she says she has noticed; they are not her words.

A Practical Guide for Designers", Medium, 24 Jan 2026. Medium blocked every check on 2026-10-12 (HTTP 403) and a web search did not find it.
