## 04 · Free to marry? That's a yes or no (`moment04-yes-or-no`, kit clips 5 + 6b: access levels close the clip)

**Kit (5):** "is this person free to marry" is one boolean. it doesn't require showing when they married, who they married, or which court granted the divorce. you could build the version that just says yes or no.
**Kit (6b):** an individual needs a yes or no. a court handling an inheritance case might genuinely need more. so why is it one search box returning everything to everyone?

**Ours:**
> data minimisation: expose the minimum amount of data needed to answer the question being asked.
> "is this person currently free to marry?" that's a yes or no. answering it doesn't require when they got married, who they married, when they divorced or which court granted it.
> the answer can be simple: this person currently has an active marriage on record. nothing else. no name of spouse, no dates, no history.
> and maybe access comes in levels: an individual could access one particular level of the data, and certain organisations or institutions could access more 🤨

## 05 · Technology is not neutral (`moment05-not-neutral`, kit clip 6)

**Kit:** in Estonia a divorce record is administrative, nobody's aunties are calling a meeting. now think about a searchable divorce registry somewhere divorce still carries stigma, especially for women. same data, different consequences.

**Ours:**
> technology is not neutral. it takes the form of the place you use it in.
> in estonia a divorce record could just be administrative. nobody cares. in an african context, to a large extent, divorce, especially for women, is still pretty stigmatised.
> so this isn't just a privacy engineering conversation. it's a cultural design one too.
