## 0. What the research says (read this first)

1. **The news itself is a small search.** On YouTube, videos about Marrify got 5-579 views each (the official MarryRight
   channel's launch video: 579; a one-person explainer: 68). The two that broke out were Ghanaian news channels using
   drama framing: Pinax TV "Panic hits Ghanaians abroad... app to catch cheating husbands" (11,932 views, 8.5x the channel's
   usual) and First2watch "Daddy Lumba's marriage issue leads to an app..." (3,256). Google suggests "marrify app ghana",
   "ghana app to check marriage", "ghana marriage checker", "ghana marriage records online". YouTube suggests nothing for
   "marrify" yet. So: **the word "Marrify" alone will not bring strangers**; almost nobody knows the name.
2. **The question behind it is a big, evergreen search.** "How to check if someone is married" is a YouTube suggestion,
   and Google expands it by country: Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Pakistan, India, the UK, the US. Proven demand:
   "How to Find Out If He's Married" (The Real, 826K views, 53x the channel's usual), "No ring? 6 ways to tell if he's
   secretly married" (Family Feud, 101K, August 2026), "How to check marital status in NADRA record" (Pakistan, 69K, 6.6x;
   another one 427K). People want to check a partner. **Our search-led titles use those exact words: "check if someone is
   married", "check marital status online", "marriage records online".**
3. **What the outlier thumbnails have in common** (contact sheet: Pinax, First2watch, Ghana Card, NADRA, SA digital ID,
   Estonia): the topic's real object is in frame and big (a phone showing the app, a Ghana Card held up, a ring or a
   wedding couple), a face reacting to it, a country flag, and 2-5 words that state the stakes ("GHANA CARD AS PAYMENT
   SYSTEM", "IT'S VOLUNTARY... UNTIL IT ISN'T"). The topic words are the plain ones people search. That matches her viral
   samples exactly: the topic named + a literal picture of it.
4. **People react to it as a relationship story first, a privacy story second.** The minister's own line: "Don't just
   believe they're single" (ChannelOne TV). Pulse Nigeria: "can expose if the partner you're dating is already secretly
   married". South African X turned it into a joke (Briefly, 5 Sep: "face of cheaters"; others asked for the same app in
   South Africa). Nobody in the coverage we found, apart from TechCabal, talks about privacy. **That gap is her video's value,
   but the door in is the relationship question.** Title and thumbnail open the door; her privacy take is what they stay for.
5. **Her own channel's proof:** her best video by far is "nobody warned me about studying in Europe as a Nigerian"
   (6,300 views vs about 93 for a typical video: 68x). Thumbnail: her face, chin on hand, "DEAR NIgerian STUDENTS" with
   handwritten labels and an arrow. Identity + Europe works for her audience. But a cold viewer could not place "A Nigerian
   in Estonia reacts" on this topic (scored 2/10), so the Estonia angle goes into the Shorts, the description and a
   possible future video, not the main thumbnail.
6. **Estonia is a big adjacent search**, but a different video: "Most Digital Country in the World: ESTONIA" (650K,
   Nov 2025), "living in estonia as a nigerian" is a YouTube suggestion. Use "Estonia" in the description, tags and the
   Estonia Short (clip 02). This video can't honestly rank for "Africa digital ID" (she doesn't discuss ID systems); left out.
7. **TikTok:** we could not see TikTok search data or Marrify posts from here (TikTok pages don't load without the app).
   Hashtags below come from the YouTube/Google research and what Ghanaian news pages use. If she can, she should type
   "marrify" and "ghana marriage app" into TikTok search and screenshot the suggestions; we'll fold them in.
