**[SEGMENT 1: OPEN]**

Why is it so hard to stop doomscrolling?

Well, maybe because the apps are intentionally designed in a way that makes it so hard to stop.

One of the features that powers doomscrolling is the infinite scroll feature.

This is the feed that never stops, the feature that helps you scroll infinitely on TikTok and Instagram.

And the thing about it is that your brain doesn't get any natural stopping cue.

A stopping cue is a small moment that makes you pause, like the end of a chapter or the end of an episode.

But your Instagram or your TikTok feed doesn't have that.

**[~1:00 BEAT: her own screen time, and the promise of nine habits]**

I do not think of myself as a very big doomscroller.

My screen time is already low for an average person, but I want it even lower, because I see no benefit to doomscrolling.

This video is about the habits I'm putting in place instead, nine things that I do instead of doomscrolling, that I think you can also use as well.

---

**[SEGMENT 2: HOW TO PICK WHAT REPLACES DOOMSCROLLING]**

Now, if you're looking to replace doomscrolling, first of all, you need to find a habit that doesn't take too much effort.

Because for the average person, doomscrolling is something you do when you're tired, or when you don't want to do something more difficult.

You're looking for an easy way out, and doomscrolling is such an easy way out.

So you don't want to replace it with something that is cognitively too demanding.

And anything that you want to replace it with has to have an endpoint, or a checkpoint where you can stop, so that thing doesn't also become addictive.

---

**[SEGMENT 3: NUMBER 1, READING]**

Number one on my list of things that I do instead of doomscrolling is reading.

This is number one because it is something I like.

I try to read almost every day.

If I'm going out somewhere, you'd barely see me without a book.

So wherever and whenever there is a time gap, like I'm waiting for something, or I'm in a queue, or I'm on the bus, the book is there.

Of course my phone is there as much, but I try as much as I can to reach for the book instead.

I even read when I eat.

Because for me, eating feels like a break, and I noticed that whenever I eat, I usually pick up my phone during that period.

So now I read while I eat.

**[~3:00 BEAT: what she reads, and how to pick books you'll actually pick up]**

This year I've read mostly fiction.

Of course I'm in school, so I read academic books as well, but this year, outside of academic books and papers, most of what I've read is fiction.

So regardless of the book type or genre, just find the books that you enjoy, because those are the ones that you would actually pick up instead of your phone.

If you get a book that you don't enjoy, you're just going to end up on your phone anyway.

And you can do it in whatever format you prefer.

For me, physical books work. I don't really enjoy reading on electronics.

That's the reason I haven't gotten a Kindle, because I have an iPad, and I don't even read on my iPad.

But maybe I'll get a Kindle and see how it works out for me.

But if you don't like having books everywhere, you can get a Kindle.

---

**[SEGMENT 4: NUMBER 2, EMBRACING BOREDOM]**

Number two on the list of things that I do instead of doomscrolling is actually just embracing boredom.

Intentionally let yourself be bored, without doing anything, or without finding anything to fill the gap.

Sit with your boredom and your very uncomfortable feelings.

A lot of us pick up our phone the second we feel even a little bit bored.

So try it in small places.

You're waiting for your kettle to boil, or you're standing at the bus stop.

Just let your mind wander. Look around.

It will feel a little bit uncomfortable at first, but as you do it more and more, you get okay with just being bored.

---

**[SEGMENT 5: NUMBER 3, TALKING WITH PEOPLE IN REAL LIFE]**

Number three on my list of things that I do instead of doomscrolling is talking with people in real life.

I feel like we are all not talking with each other enough.

And sometimes talking with people can feel so awkward.

It doesn't have to be something serious, small talk here and there.

They don't even have to be your friend or an acquaintance.

I'd rather replace doomscrolling with real-life things, and talking with people is one of the biggest ones for me.

If you find yourself in places where you can have small talk, please do it.

Hear people's voices, sit with their awkwardness, sit with people and talk.

You can go out and grab a cup of coffee with someone.

Just talk, even if it's awkward.

It's better to talk with someone in real life and feel awkward than to just keep scrolling.

I'll take awkward talking with people over scrolling on social media anytime, any day.

---

**[SEGMENT 6: NUMBER 4, HANGING OUT WITH YOUR FRIENDS]**

Number four on my list is hanging out with your friends.

We complain that it's hard to make friends as adults now.

But I was raised in Nigeria, and interestingly, I noticed that the older generation, the generation before mine, form friendships even in their adult life.

They will become friends out of anything.

Why the hell do you think your mom will throw a party and there are so many people there?

They can make friends easily.

So I don't think it's impossible to make friends in our adult life.

**[~6:00 BEAT: "virtual friends with influencers" is the line that lands, and the bike and thrift store are next]**

It's just that a lot of the time we could use to make friends, we are just making virtual friends.

We're becoming virtual friends with influencers. We're spending it on our screens.

You know, try to make friends, and if you already have a few friends, try to hang out with them more.

---

**[SEGMENT 7: NUMBER 5, PICKING UP SOMETHING NEW]**

Number five on my list of things I do instead of doomscrolling is picking up something new.

One of the things that I did this summer, because I had a little bit more time on my hands and I didn't want to be stuck on my phone, was learning how to ride a bike.

I didn't even know how to ride a bike before then.

I bought a bike, and I would just finish work and ride anywhere.

Sometimes I wouldn't even have a destination in mind. I'd just keep riding.

When I'm biking, I can't press my phone.

I'm just looking around at how beautiful everywhere is, and I'm doing something physical at the same time.

So you can pick up a habit like that. Keep your hands busy.

It could be crochet.

I used to crochet as a kid, but for some reason I don't know how to anymore.

A lot of people say crocheting really helps them. If I still knew how, I think I'd do it a lot.

It could be painting for you, or taking pictures, photography, right?

I've been trying to pick up baking, because I love eating pastries.

But for some reason, I suck at baking as well.

I've been trying to make banana bread for the longest time. I've tried three times now, and I still haven't gotten one single success at it.

So yeah, just try new things. Bake.

**[~8:30 BEAT: the thrift store bag is the payoff here]**

Another thing I've started to enjoy is going to a thrift store and trying to hunt for something.

Going to a thrift store gives you this feeling like you are a hunter.

You're trying to hunt for something, but the interesting thing about going to a thrift store is that you don't know what you are hunting for. You're just hunting for something.

It's so enjoyable these days.

One of the things I hunt for is bags.

I am now growing this reputation for finding really nice bags at thrift stores.

So I am more motivated to walk into a thrift store to hunt for a bag.

It's kind of a little sense of accomplishment when I get a bag and I'm like, "Oh yeah, see what I found."

Weirdly, it is something that I've really started to enjoy.

I found another bag last week. I think for 5 euros or something. Yeah, it was around 5 euros, 6 or 7.

It's just a steal. I cannot wait to show it off, actually.

You know, pick something you like.

It doesn't have to be something that makes money. In fact, do not even make it something that makes money.

I feel like we are so overwhelmed, and because of the situation of things, we usually don't want to do things that don't make us money.

And I think that's also the reason why it's easy for us to settle for cheap habits like doomscrolling.

So follow your curiosity.

If something sounds fun to you, try to do it. Go out to a show. Try something new.

Cook something new. Try a new recipe.

Change the arrangement of your house. Try to decorate it.

I also read somewhere that caring for a pet or a plant, if you don't have kids or maybe you live alone, also helps a little.

---

**[SEGMENT 8: NUMBER 6, LONG-FORM CONTENT]**

Number six on my list of things that I do instead of doomscrolling is watching long-form content, or reading long-form content.

In a way, this one still involves your phone.

But yeah, try to finish watching a very long YouTube video, or read a very long article on Substack.

Do more long-form content.

I feel like a lot of us treat long-form content like background noise.

You're watching a podcast, but you're not really concentrating on it, and it's just playing in the background.

Try to engage more with long-form content. Be more intentional with it, and just stay with it more.

---

**[SEGMENT 9: NUMBER 7, DOING SOMETHING PHYSICAL]**

Number seven on my list of things that I do instead of doomscrolling is doing something physical.

It can be as simple as going for a walk without your phone, or just do a push-up.

Now, this one I don't really do, because I'm very physically lazy.

I do hear that it works. I tried it a few times, and it did.

Yeah, I think it's something that might work, but again, I just don't like physical activities that much. I'm trying.

So if you are someone who likes physical activity, you can do a little bit of exercise very quickly.

You know, give yourself that little bump.

---

**[SEGMENT 10: NUMBER 8, MEDITATION, PRAYER AND JOURNALING]**

Number eight on my list of things that I do instead of doomscrolling is meditation and prayer.

I absolutely recommend meditating and prayer.

Even if it's just a few minutes of it, in that time that you spend scrolling.

I'll say pray, I'll say meditate, all the time.

**[~12:00 BEAT: journaling that doesn't have to be good is the easy win here]**

Another thing is journaling, journaling and writing.

I don't journal as much, but I try to write from time to time.

I find it difficult to write when I'm not relaxed, and I'm usually not relaxed these days because I'm torn between school and work.

But writing doesn't have to be something profound.

There's even something called junk journaling, where people make a journal out of scraps.

If journaling feels like a far-fetched thing to do, just do a brain dump.

Just write stuff down, and dump it somewhere.

It doesn't have to be so polished that you could share it on Substack or Medium.

You can just journal about your life, or write about something completely different.

---

**[SEGMENT 11: IF YOU CAN'T QUIT COLD TURKEY]**

And if you can't simply quit doomscrolling cold turkey, try to reduce it.

One way people do this is that they delete social media apps from their phone and use them on their desktop instead.

You can also get one of those apps that block social media, or set a limit on how long you can use them.

---

**[SEGMENT 12: NUMBER 9, THE PEOPLE IN YOUR LIFE]**

And lastly, talk to the people in your life.

This time I mean your family, your friends.

Call your family. Call your friends.

Have real-time conversations. Try to build genuine relationships and genuine connection.

You cannot get that as much on social media.

It cannot beat the genuine connection that you get from the people in your life.

As much as people comment and give feedback on your posts, you need to also always, always come back to the fact that there are people in your life, real-life people in your life, that care about you.

And you should try your possible best to build genuine connection with them. Talk to them, you know.

## 10. SUBSTACK / MEDIUM ESSAY

### Title:
> What I Do Instead of Doomscrolling

### Subtitle:
> My screen time is already low, but I want it even lower, because I see no benefit to it.

### SEO Title:
> 9 Things to Do Instead of Doomscrolling

### SEO Description:
> Why it's so hard to stop scrolling, how to pick something to replace it with, and nine habits I'm putting in place instead, from reading to hunting for thrift store bags.

---

Why is it so hard to stop doomscrolling? I think part of the answer is that the apps are designed in a way that makes it hard to stop. One of the features that powers it is infinite scroll, the feed that never stops. A book gives you the end of a chapter, and a show gives you the end of an episode, and those small moments make you pause. Your Instagram or TikTok feed doesn't have that, so your brain never gets a natural stopping cue.

I don't think of myself as a very big doomscroller. My screen time is already low for an average person, but I want it even lower, because I see no benefit to it. So I've been thinking about what I'm putting in its place. Whatever replaces it has to be easy. For most of us, doomscrolling is what we do when we're tired, or when we don't want to do something more difficult, so replacing it with something demanding won't work. It also needs an endpoint, somewhere you can stop, so the new thing doesn't become addictive too.

Reading is number one for me. I try to read almost every day, and if I'm going out somewhere, you'd barely see me without a book. When I'm in a queue or on the bus, the book is there. My phone is there as much, but I try to reach for the book. I even read when I eat, because eating feels like a break, and that's when I'd usually pick up my phone. Outside of school books and papers, most of what I've read this year is fiction. Whatever the genre, find books you enjoy, because those are the ones you'll actually pick up. For me that's physical books. I don't even read on my iPad, so I haven't gotten a Kindle yet, but if you don't like having books everywhere, a Kindle is an option.

I'm also trying to let myself be bored. A lot of us pick up our phones the second we feel even a little bit bored, so try it in small places, like waiting for your kettle to boil or standing at the bus stop. It feels uncomfortable at first, but the more you do it, the more okay you get with just being bored.

Then there are people. I feel like we're not talking with each other enough, and sometimes talking feels so awkward. I'd still rather talk with someone in real life and feel awkward than keep scrolling. They don't even have to be your friend or an acquaintance. Friends matter too. We complain that it's hard to make friends as adults, but I was raised in Nigeria, and the generation before mine will become friends out of anything. Why do you think your mom can throw a party and there are so many people there? I don't think it's impossible for us. A lot of the time we could use to make friends, we're becoming virtual friends with influencers instead. If you already have a few friends, hang out with them more.

I'm also picking up new things. This summer I learned to ride a bike, and I would just finish work and ride, sometimes with no destination at all. When I'm biking, I can't touch my phone. I used to crochet as a kid, but I don't know how anymore. I've been trying to bake because I love pastries, but I've tried banana bread three times now and still haven't had one success. And I've started going to thrift stores. The interesting thing about a thrift store is that you don't know what you're hunting for. I hunt for bags, and I'm now growing a reputation for finding really nice ones. I found another last week for around five euros, and I can't wait to show it off. None of this has to make money. I feel like we're so overwhelmed that we don't want to do anything that doesn't pay, and I think that's part of why it's easy to settle for cheap habits like doomscrolling.

The rest of my list is smaller. Long YouTube videos and long Substack articles can help, if you're intentional with them and they aren't just playing in the background. Something physical helps, like a walk without your phone or a push-up. I'm very physically lazy, but I tried it a few times and it worked. I absolutely recommend meditation and prayer, even for a few minutes. Journaling helps too. I find it hard to write when I'm not relaxed, and I'm torn between school and work these days, but it doesn't have to be profound. There's junk journaling, where people make a journal out of scraps, or you can just do a brain dump and write stuff down. If you can't quit cold turkey, delete the apps from your phone and use them on desktop, or set a limit. And lastly, talk to the people in your life. As much as people comment and give feedback on your posts, there are real people in your life who care about you, and I want to try my best to build genuine connection with them. What do you do instead of scrolling?
