Why is it so hard to stop doomscrolling?

Part of it is design. Here are nine easy things I do instead.

01 / 09
Stopping cues
A feed that never ends

In a 2023 study of people's scrolling, infinite feeds left people with the most regret afterwards. As an HCI student, I find it interesting how much one small design decision shapes how we spend our free time.

Source: Rixen et al., 2023
02 / 09
My one rule
Replace it with something easy

For most of us, doomscrolling is what we do when we're tired, or when we don't want to do something more difficult. So the thing that replaces it can't be too demanding, or the phone wins.

04 / 09
No. 1
I read while I eat

Eating always feels like a break to me, and I noticed I picked up my phone every time. So now a book comes to the table.

05 / 09
No. 4
Make friends as an adult

Our parents at the school gate: "My child is going to this school, your child is going to this school." Boom. That's how my parents' generation in Nigeria made friends, and I think we can be more intentional about it too.

06 / 09
No. 5
Not everything has to make money

"I feel like we are not picking up habits that we cannot monetise." I think that's part of why we settle for things like doomscrolling. This summer I learned to ride a bike, and now I hunt for bags at thrift stores.

07 / 09
No. 9
Call your people

As much as people comment and give feedback on your posts, there are real people in your life who care about you. Social media can never beat that connection.
