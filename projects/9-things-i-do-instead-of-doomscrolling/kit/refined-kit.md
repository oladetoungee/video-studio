### 6f. Essay (from her kit, in full)
Why is it so hard to stop doomscrolling? I think part of the answer is that the apps are designed in a way that makes it hard to stop. One of the features that powers it is infinite scroll, the feed that never stops. A book gives you the end of a chapter, and a show gives you the end of an episode, and those small moments make you pause. Your Instagram or TikTok feed doesn't have that, so your brain never gets a natural stopping cue.

I don't think of myself as a very big doomscroller. My screen time is already low for an average person, but I want it even lower, because I see no benefit to it. So I've been thinking about what I'm putting in its place. Whatever replaces it has to be easy. For most of us, doomscrolling is what we do when we're tired, or when we don't want to do something more difficult, so replacing it with something demanding won't work. It also needs an endpoint, somewhere you can stop, so the new thing doesn't become addictive too.

Reading is number one for me. I try to read almost every day, and if I'm going out somewhere, you'd barely see me without a book. When I'm in a queue or on the bus, the book is there. My phone is there as much, but I try to reach for the book. I even read when I eat, because eating feels like a break, and that's when I'd usually pick up my phone. Outside of school books and papers, most of what I've read this year is fiction. Whatever the genre, find books you enjoy, because those are the ones you'll actually pick up. For me that's physical books. I don't even read on my iPad, so I haven't gotten a Kindle yet, but if you don't like having books everywhere, a Kindle is an option.

I'm also trying to let myself be bored. A lot of us pick up our phones the second we feel even a little bit bored, so try it in small places, like waiting for your kettle to boil or standing at the bus stop. It feels uncomfortable at first, but the more you do it, the more okay you get with just being bored.

Then there are people. I feel like we're not talking with each other enough, and sometimes talking feels so awkward. I'd still rather talk with someone in real life and feel awkward than keep scrolling. They don't even have to be your friend or an acquaintance. Friends matter too. We complain that it's hard to make friends as adults, but I was raised in Nigeria, and the generation before mine will become friends out of anything. Why do you think your mom can throw a party and there are so many people there? I don't think it's impossible for us. A lot of the time we could use to make friends, we're becoming virtual friends with influencers instead. If you already have a few friends, hang out with them more.

I'm also picking up new things. This summer I learned to ride a bike, and I would just finish work and ride, sometimes with no destination at all. When I'm biking, I can't touch my phone. I used to crochet as a kid, but I don't know how anymore. I've been trying to bake because I love pastries, but I've tried banana bread three times now and still haven't had one success. And I've started going to thrift stores. The interesting thing about a thrift store is that you don't know what you're hunting for. I hunt for bags, and I'm now growing a reputation for finding really nice ones. I found another last week for around five euros, and I can't wait to show it off. None of this has to make money. I feel like we're so overwhelmed that we don't want to do anything that doesn't pay, and I think that's part of why it's easy to settle for cheap habits like doomscrolling.

The rest of my list is smaller. Long YouTube videos and long Substack articles can help, if you're intentional with them and they aren't just playing in the background. Something physical helps, like a walk without your phone or a push-up. I'm very physically lazy, but I tried it a few times and it worked. I absolutely recommend meditation and prayer, even for a few minutes. Journaling helps too. I find it hard to write when I'm not relaxed, and I'm torn between school and work these days, but it doesn't have to be profound. There's junk journaling, where people make a journal out of scraps, or you can just do a brain dump and write stuff down. If you can't quit cold turkey, delete the apps from your phone and use them on desktop, or set a limit. And lastly, talk to the people in your life. As much as people comment and give feedback on your posts, there are real people in your life who care about you, and I want to try my best to build genuine connection with them. What do you do instead of scrolling?
