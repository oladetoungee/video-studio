# Social playbook: what is working for other creators (built 2026-11-02)

Source: 38 TikToks in `inspiration/tiktok-references/` (34 she sent + 4 top videos from creators she asked us to study).
Per-video notes, numbers and links: `inspiration/tiktok-references/INDEX.md`. Video numbers below (#25) point there.

Every lesson is marked:
- **[her note]** = Gbemi said it when she sent the link.
- **[observed]** = we saw it in the frames, transcripts or numbers.

This file sits next to `SHORTS-PLAYBOOK.md` (how she builds shorts), `TEASER-PLAYBOOK.md` and `HER-VOICE.md`.
House rules still win: no gradients or glows, every text editable in the studio, her face never un-mirrored,
every piece stands alone for a stranger. Where a creator here breaks a house rule, it says so.

> **Her rule (2026-11-02): no faceless videos.** She is always on camera. Faceless formats in this playbook (kodekloud etc.) are for topic ideas and graphics style only: put the graphics beside, behind or between shots of her.

## 1. Hook library

What a hook is made of [observed, and named by #28]: a **verbal hook** (the first sentence), a **written hook** (the
words on screen in frame 1), a **visual hook** (something moving or surprising in frame 1: a push-in, props in her hands,
a grid, a black card) and a **sound hook** (a sound in the first half-second). The strongest videos stack three of them
in the first 2 seconds. #28: "One hook is good. Three hooks are even better."

The checklist #19 shows on screen for why a hook works: sparks curiosity, reframes something familiar, is easy to take
in, hits a nerve, teases the payoff.

Common to almost every hook here [observed]: the topic is said plainly in the first sentence, there is no greeting and
no "hi guys", and the speaker is already mid-energy at frame 1.

| type | real first line (video) | why it works | a version for Gbemi's topics |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Correct a belief** | "Just to make it clear, the literacy crisis we're in isn't that people can't read words." (#15, 766K) | People share it to win arguments. | "Just to be clear, the literacy crisis is about people who can read every word and still miss the point." |
| **Uncomfortable truth about "you"** | "Here's an uncomfortable but honest truth. You do not respect yourself enough." (#29, 5.1M) | Second person + a sting + a promise to help. | "Here's an uncomfortable truth. You can't sit with a hard paragraph anymore, and your phone trained you out of it." |
| **Reflective "you" title, said first** | "You're slowly becoming who you never meant to be, and the scary part is that it didn't happen overnight." (#06) | Slow change everyone fears; the title is the first line. | "You're slowly losing the ability to hold two ideas at once, and you didn't notice it happening." |
| **Plain question = the title** | "What is a DDoS attack?" (#03); "Why is it that when we can't understand a book, we assume the problem is with us?" (#26) | A search question; the viewer already wants the answer. | "Why does every app feel built to keep you scrolling?" / "What is a dark pattern?" |
| **Number puzzle about an everyday act** | "How does one gigabyte folder get compressed to a 400 megabyte zip file? ... So where did those 600 megabytes go?" (#25, 1.1M) | Everyone has done it; the numbers don't add up. | "Your phone shows you 2,000 posts a day. Why do you remember about three?" |
| **News, plainly, with stakes** | "A fake traffic fine is draining people's entire bank accounts in Nigeria this week." | **"X just launched Y and it changes Z"** | "OpenAI just announced something called Dots, and it completely changes how we work with AI." (#38, 1M) / "HTTP just got a new method called QUERY." | **"I recently read..."** | "I recently read a court case between OpenAI, the company that makes ChatGPT, and the parents of a 19-year-old..." (#08) | Credibility through reading + a human stake; explains OpenAI for strangers. | "I recently read a study on what happens to your attention after 20 minutes of short videos." |
| **Direct instruction, one word** | "If you've been using ChatGPT as your friend, your confidant, or someone you use for real life advice, don't." (#36, 1.5M) | Talks to a habit many viewers have; "don't" lands hard. | "If you've been using AI to summarise every book you're 'reading', don't." |
| **"If you're X, learn these 3"** | "If you're using ChatGPT or Claude to build apps, here are the three things you need to actually understand." (#17) | Names the audience in 4 words; a countable promise. | "If you're a junior developer coding with AI, learn these three things first." |
| **Credentials in one breath** | "I lost 30kg, graduated with the highest grade at university, and I went from being homeless to living the life I'm living now, and it's because I trained my brain to crave doing hard things." (#22, 8.3 % saves) | Three proofs, then the method. | "I work full time as a developer and I'm doing a master's in Estonia, and this is how I still read every week." (check the claim is true before using) |
| **Personal story that changed me** | "So I watched a podcast that genuinely altered the course of my life forever, and I'm not exaggerating." (#20, 9.1 % saves) | A testimony; you want the thing that changed them. | "I read one paper [when] that changed how I use my phone." (only if true) |
| **Quote first** | "One of my favourite quotes of all time: 'You'd worry less about what they thought if you realised how little they did.' Let that sink in." (#02) | The quote is the hook; "let that sink in" gives a beat. | For her notes file: "[Quote]. I've been thinking about this all week." |
| **Shout-out to an author** | "Shout out to my guy George Orwell. Listen to this." (#14, 268K) [her note: attention grabbing] | Treats a classic writer like a friend; "wait for this" before the twist. | "Shout out to my girl Chimamanda. Listen to this." |
| **Famous but misunderstood** | "One of the most famous quotes in philosophy is also one of the most misunderstood." (#35, 2.1M) | Promises you'll know something others get wrong. | "One of the most famous rules in design is also one of the most misunderstood." |
| **Promise + 3 concrete lenses** | "I want to explain why the declining literacy is something we need to pay attention to as Kenyans, and I'm going to use my four-year-old niece, the Kenyan corporate scene and a novel called The Secret Life of Bees." (#01) | Three unexpected things you want to see connected. | "I want to explain why doomscrolling matters to Africans in tech, using [someone in my life], [a place I worked] and one HCI paper." |
| **Scenario question ("Say you...")** | "Say you buy something online and you don't receive the goods... What do you do then?" (#32) | Puts the viewer inside the problem. | "Say you open an app to check one message and look up 40 minutes later. What happened?" |
| **Result with a number + props** | "This is how you turn a boring product into an $880 million brand." (#16, props in both hands) | Number + a visual before/after in frame 1. | "This is how a country of about 1.3 million people built one of the most digital governments in the world." |
| **Call-out** | "You overthink your talking head videos, which is exactly why we ain't seen one in a minute." (#13) / "Allow talking head videos to rest. Yes you." (#18) | Feels personal; for a niche audience. | "You save every article and never read it. Yes you." |
| **Confession** | "What I'm most insecure about right now isn't my weight, isn't my stretch marks, it's my career." (#31) | Vulnerable + a reveal at the end of the sentence. | "What I find hardest about studying in Estonia isn't the cold." |
| **"Have you noticed...?"** | "Have you noticed more creators doing these four by four background visuals? Why are they everywhere?" (#19) | Points at something seen but never explained. | "Have you noticed every app now has a streak? Why?" |
| **Cold open mid-thought** | "[A] scenario that we've seen play out over and over again is that whenever companies go public, the founders are very often sidelined." (#21) | Drops you inside the argument. Weak alone: it needs a title box (#21 uses a yellow one). | Use only with a title that states the topic. |
| **Word of the day** | "The word of the day is 'barely functioning'..." (#24) | A dictionary frame for a feeling. | "The word of the day is 'dark pattern'." |

[observed] Videos that opened with a story setup ("Two weeks ago, OpenAI flew some influencers...", #07) or a greeting-ish
continuation ("Okay, so now let's get into part two", #05) did fine only because the title box carried the topic.
Rule for her: if the first sentence doesn't state the topic, the title box must.

**Her note on voice:** her `HER-VOICE.md` bans "It's not X, it's Y" in writing. #15 uses that shape out loud and it worked;
for her, say the correction in one positive sentence (as in the table) so it sounds like her.

**Phrase cards** [her note on #29: "she loves the design of those phrase cards"]: pure black #000000 full frame, the
phrase in **red #FE2802 serif, regular weight, title case, centred, ~55 % of frame width, 4 short lines**. Used 4 times
in 2 minutes: the thesis (1.75 s, at 2.5 s in) and "First" / "second" / "third" (0.5 s flashes). Gbemi version: black
ground, one accent colour per video (her accents: orange #FF4713, blue #1B3FD8, green #22E08A) in Instrument Serif or
a heavy serif, her own key line.

**Countdown timer** [her note on #16: "a timer at the side, idea for teaser countdown"]: white bold sans digits
"00:02" ... "00:54", top-right corner, ~5 % frame height, no box, starts after the series intro and counts the promised
60 seconds. Ours: DM Mono digits in the corner, counting the teaser or "in 60 seconds" shorts.

## 4. Editing devices (with where to see them)

- **Something new on screen every 2-4 s** even in plain talking heads: caption colour change, a card, a cut-in [observed in all edited ones].
- **Two camera angles** cut per point (front + side): cheap variety, no graphics needed. #22 0:25.

## 5. Structure patterns (introduce, turn, land, end)

1. **Truth -> 3 scenarios -> standard** (#29): uncomfortable truth, "let me help you", scenario 1/2/3 each with what to
   stop and what to say instead, closing standard. 2 min.
2. **Question -> one example -> rule** (#25, #03): the puzzle, one tiny worked example carried all the way, the general
   rule in one closing line.
3. **News -> human -> mechanism -> what it means for you** (#08, #30, #37): the claim, a named person, how it happened,
   what you should do.
4. **Promise -> numbered steps -> instruction** (#17, #22): who it's for, 3-5 points, a closing analogy or "do this".
5. **Quote -> what people think -> what it means** (#35, #14, #02).
6. **Personal mistake -> concept -> diagram -> challenge** (#20): "I was doing this wrong", the idea, a drawing, "you have to fully believe it's possible".
7. **Questions -> thinker -> two problems -> question back to you** (#26): ends with "Do you think...? Or do you think...?" (drives comments) + further reading.
8. **Series part** (#01, #05): series label, part number, this part's thesis, "part 2 tomorrow" implied by the label.

Endings seen [observed]: a question to the viewer (#26), an instruction ("remember that the next time...", #02), an
analogy (#17), a mic-drop line (#14), a reading list (#26, #27), a comment-to-get CTA ("Comment 'NetWorth'", #20). None
ends with a long sign-off.

Lengths [observed]: 8-20 s for one line or one feeling (#10, #14, #15, #23, #24); 1-2 min for an explainer (#03, #22,
#25, #29); 5-10 min still works on TikTok for news/analysis when the topic is strong (#20, #32, #36), but #07 at 13 min
had the lowest numbers of that creator's three.

## The 10 lessons that matter most (for every new kit)

1. **Say the topic in the first sentence, in plain words** [observed in 30+ of 38]. If the first line doesn't, the title box must (#21, #05).
2. **Stack three hooks in the first 2 s**: a sentence, words on screen, and something moving or a sound (#28). [observed]
3. **Short and sharp beats long and loose for reach**: the top-liked videos are 12-16 s single thoughts (#14, #15, 28 % like rate) [observed]; long videos only work with a strong topic and receipts (#20, #32, #36).
