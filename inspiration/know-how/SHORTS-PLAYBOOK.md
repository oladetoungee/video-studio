## Her rules for picking and cutting (what the files show)
1. **One idea per short, cut from one continuous stretch of the long video**, in the long video's order. A long idea becomes a
   **numbered series** ("how to read a book (pt 1)" ... "(pt 4)") rather than being squeezed: pt 1 = the method, pt 2 = why
   it works, pt 3 = doing it on book 1, pt 4 = a page-count tip on book 2.
2. **Start on the line that states the topic or the promise**, never on a greeting: "Ghana just launched something called
   Marrify", "So today I want to talk about a reading method that I think will work for you if you want to remember a huge
   chunk of the things that you read", "So why does this method work?", "Also keep in mind the page number, it also tells
   you something", "Technology is non-neutral". When the first spoken line is weak or mid-thought ("I'll start with this
   book...", "that if we are creating open data in Africa"), **the series label box carries the context** so a stranger
   still knows the topic from frame 1.
3. **End on a punch line or a cliffhanger, then the end card**: "Finally." / "no name of spouse, no dates, no history" /
   "question it" / "it's also a cultural design thing" / "So let's actually do this practically." (leads into pt 3) /
   "Who is creative?... why have people expressed any need for creativity now?" (an open question).
4. **Trim hard when she wanders; lightly when she's tight.** Reading-stack shorts lost about half their raw time: asides
   ("By the way, I'm noting this down, I actually want to get that book"), the Justin Sung / priming tangent, retakes
   ("scratch that"), long pauses, and reading out the full 10-chapter list (5:39-6:32) are gone. The ghana shorts kept
   ~90-100 % of the raw stretch because she talked in tight paragraphs. **Jump cuts are hidden under motion inserts or
   label changes**; there are no punch-in zooms (her framing is static in the face parts).
5. **Mix of her face and our motion film.** Reading pt 1: ~60 % face, ~40 % full-screen inserts from our 9:16 film export
   (YOU READ A BOOK / "So what is this book about?" / blue THIS METHOD FIXES THAT. / table-of-contents / INSPECTIONAL
   reading). Ghana: Estonia ~55 % face; "Data Minimisation" is **100 % motion** (a short can be the film scene alone with
   her voice). Inserts last 2-15 s and always land on the line they illustrate. The caption and the series label stay on
   top of the inserts, so the short reads as one piece.
6. **Stands alone**: the series label states the topic in plain words; her opening line states the promise; the short
   never needs the long video, which is only offered at the end ("FULL VIDEO ON YOUTUBE").
7. **End card, 5-7 s**: cream ground, a black rounded phone-style card: camera icon + "FULL VIDEO ON YOUTUBE" (white
   bold caps), the long video's thumbnail, its title, like / dislike counts, a channel row (her avatar, "with Gbemi",
   "Subscribed" pill + bell) and "Like / Comment / Share". A cursor taps Subscribe. The voice has ended; the music runs out.
8. **Lengths**: 22-116 s; most 34-61 s. The longest (pt 3, 116 s) is a full worked example: allowed when every 3-5 s has a
   new caption and an insert every ~15-20 s.
9. **Loudness is uneven** (measured integrated): ghana shorts -11.4 / -11.6 LUFS (loud), reading shorts **-29.1 / -28.3
   LUFS (very quiet)**. When we export shorts: **-14 LUFS, true peak <= -1 dBTP**, voice-first (CHECKS.md loudness).

## Cover frame (her note 2026-11-10: "the real cover should be on the video… it has nothing to do with thumbnails")
Instagram / TikTok / YouTube Shorts let her pick the cover from a frame of the short, so every short CONTAINS one designed cover
frame. Built first on http-status-codes (sh01-13).
- **Where**: drawn OVER the first 0.1 s (3 frames at 30 fps); no time is inserted, so sound and captions stay in sync. In the
  editor, scrub to 0:00 to see it; it is in every export. Manifest per short:
  `"cover": {"on": true, "dur": 0.1, "at": <source s of the still>, "text": "line with ~one word~ in colour", "style": "photo"|"card"}`.
- **One style for the whole series** (so her grid reads as one brand, THUMBNAIL-STUDY §6): her still full frame 9:16 (same framing
  as the full-bleed formats), mid-sentence, eyes open, sharp; a 2-8 word line in heavy white Inter Tight 900 with a soft shadow, centred,
  y 300-700, one word in the code's group colour. 2-3 of 13 are **cards** to break the grid: the code's colour flat with fine same-hue
  diagonal lines (§7), the line in ink with the word in white, her cut-out at the foot.
- **Grid-safe**: every word and her face inside y 240-1680 (the 3:4 grid crop); the line sits above her face.
- **Words**: a stranger understands them with no video (a plain topic or hook: "401 vs 403, explained", "Stop sending 200 OK for
  errors"); no shorthand, no script phrase, no labels. Write all lines first and read each cold.
- **Pick the still**: `python3 talking-head/tools/covers.py --candidates shNN 10` (10 stills across the short ->
  `build/covers/cand/`), choose one, set `"at"`, run `python3 talking-head/tools/covers.py shNN` (cuts `assets/covers/shNN.jpg`, and the
  cut-out for a card), reload the editor. Change the words in the editor (✎ Edit at 0:00, the line is `cover/cover`) or in `"text"`.
  Turn it off with `"on": false`.

## Caption spec (the reading-stack "Helvetica" style; measured at 1080x1920)
Two text elements, both native-app style (Instagram Edits / TikTok text with background):

**A. Series label (top)**
- Font: **Helvetica Neue Regular** (not bold), lowercase as written ("how to read a book (pt 1)"), sentence case for the
  Ghana titles ("Estonia Data Ecosystem").
- Size: **~82 px** (glyph box l-to-p ~76 px); default tracking; black `#000` text.
- Box: white `#FFFFFF`, radius ~8 px, padding ~18 px left/right, ~19 px top/bottom; one line, 950 px wide for a 25-character
  label; centred horizontally; **top edge at y 115-155 px** (6-8 % of the height). Two-line labels wrap with each line in
  its own box (the "Data transparency..." label).
- Optional sub-label under it (pt 3: "priming"): same style at ~46 px, white box, centred, 12 px below.
- On screen for the whole short, including over motion inserts; never animated.

**B. Spoken caption (bottom)**
- Font: **Helvetica Neue Medium** (between Regular and Bold; she calls it bold Helvetica), **lowercase exactly as spoken**
  (whisper text, with "I" capital, no full stops), no emoji.
- Size: **76 px** at 1080 wide (ascender-to-descender ~70 px; that is ~3.6 % of the height, ~7 % of the width).
- **Very tight tracking: about -0.12 em (-9 px)**, so letters nearly touch ("I'm" touches); line height ~0.92.
  (If our renderer can't go that tight, Medium at 76 px with -0.08 em and horizontal scale 0.93 matches the width.)
- Colour: warm off-white **`#DFD7CB`** (not pure white), no stroke, no drop shadow.
- Background: one rounded box behind the whole caption block, **black at ~40 % opacity** (`rgba(0,0,0,0.4)`), radius
  ~10 px, padding ~14 px left/right, ~12 px top/bottom.
- Position: centred; **single line centred at y ~1535 (80 % of the height)**; two lines occupy ~1468-1602. That sits on her
  chest, under the face, above the platform UI zone.
- Line length: **up to ~30 characters (5-7 words) per line, 2 lines max**; a caption is one breath-phrase of 1.5-3 s.
- Timing: phrase blocks swap on the phrase boundary (no word-by-word highlight, no karaoke colour, no pop or bounce; a
  hard swap). Gaps between phrases show no caption.
- Same caption continues over full-screen motion inserts (on dark film scenes the box is barely visible; that's fine).

**Ghana-series variant (her other caption style)**: a handwritten marker font (rounded, uneven, cream `#F2E6C8`) in
**per-line** dark boxes (black ~65 %, radius ~14 px), 3-4 words per line, up to 3 lines, block centred at y ~1380-1680
(72-88 %). Font not identified; nearest open fonts: Gochi Hand / Patrick Hand (OFL). She used it on the ghana shorts and her
16:9 ghana cut, the Helvetica style on reading-stack: **so the caption style is per video too** (each video its own look);
default to the Helvetica spec unless the video's look asks for another.

### Font and licence
- **Helvetica Neue** is a macOS system font: `/System/Library/Fonts/HelveticaNeue.ttc` (index 0 Regular, 1 Bold,
  10 Medium, 7 Light, 4 Condensed Bold, 9 Condensed Black). Apple's licence lets us use it to render text on this Mac,
  so **use it for local renders (burned into the MP4)**; **never copy the font file** into a project, an artifact, a
  published web page or the studio's served files. In the studio editor, load it with
  `font-family: "Helvetica Neue"` + `local()` only (it works because the editor runs on her Mac).
- Closest open fallback if we ever need to ship the font or render elsewhere: **Inter Tight Medium** (OFL, Google Fonts:
  similar width with the tight tracking) or Nimbus Sans (URW; a Helvetica clone, GPL with font exception). Arimo
  (Apache) matches Arial, not Helvetica: avoid.

## Building a short in our studio (spec for the next build)
1. Pick 3-6 stretches per long video with the rules above; a long idea = "(pt N)" series. Each stretch: start on the
   topic/promise line, end on a punch line or cliffhanger; cut asides, retakes and pauses > 0.5 s.
2. Lay the 9:16 film scene for each stretch as inserts on the lines they illustrate (our film is already word-timed);
   her face otherwise. Target 35-60 % inserts.
3. Series label (A) all through; caption (B) all through, phrase blocks from word timings, max 2 lines x 30 characters.
4. End card 5-7 s ("FULL VIDEO ON YOUTUBE", the real thumbnail and title, @WithGbemi / "with Gbemi").
5. Music bed under the voice, sounds per `SOUND-PALETTE.md`; master -14 LUFS, true peak <= -1 dBTP; 1080x1920 30 fps
   H.264 + AAC; copy to her Downloads.
6. Checks: her-words (caption text vs transcript, names spelled: Mortimer Adler, Alex Baratta, Marrify), stands-alone
   (label + first line read cold), text-safe-area (captions own y 1460-1660 at 1080x1920, as in CHECKS.md), loudness, format, delivered.

## Lessons from the 2026-10-15 batch (25 more shorts for database-indexes, phone-distraction, ux-research)
Her rule (10-15): no cap at 6 shorts; cut every stretch that stands alone. Critic rounds on 25 new shorts taught:
- **NO big overlay text (L3 shout) in shorts** (her rule 2026-10-18: she hid it in most shorts, and the ones she could not hide went out with it): the top label is the only top text; a short opens on her talking, with keywords / film inserts later. (Old guidance kept for reference:) Hook text (L3 shout): type all its words inside ~1.2 s (`times` from the first spoken word, 0.22 s apart) and hold it
  >= 1.5 s after the last word; place it below the top label (`y: Math.max(411, TH.labelBottom() + 165)`), or a two-line label
  and the hook collide. A hook cut off by the first insert reads as the opposite ("MAKE YOUR PHONE LESS").
- **Start inserts when the film has something on screen**: a film scene often types its title for 1-2 s; start the insert on the
  next phrase, or pin the film with `film: { at: <film seconds> }` (film/data/words.json) when word sync lands on the previous scene.
- **Full-frame inserts under a two-line label**: film titles sit at y ~100-160 and get covered; prefer a top half
  (`side: 'top', size: 1150`: the panel starts below the label) unless the film's frame is busy all the way down.
- **Big single words (L8 keyword)**: at most one per short, never one that repeats the caption for 4-6 s.
- **Cut the opener that needs the long video** ("for the rest of the types", "in the earlier part", "so again, to reiterate",
  "well, I mean"): start on the topic line, end on the punch line, not on a trailing "actually" / a bare list.
- **Caption hygiene**: whisper times can put a word inside a cut pause (it vanishes from the captions) or leave the next
  sentence's first word in the clip (a stray "and"); use `[a, b, "exact"]` cuts with an RMS check, and `edits/<id>.json`
  `"capShort": {"<cue index - 1>": "text"}` for a doubled word.

## Top labels: her rewrites (october-attacks, 2026-10-26)
She rewrote 13 of 16 labels ("some of them are nice, some just don't feel like they're gonna work"). Kept: "A PHP flaw and 3 hacks in one month", "PHP2Shell: hackers could run commands on your server", "One hacker + AI agents broke into 100+ sites". What her rewrites teach:
1. **Name the thing first.** The company, product or bug is in the label: "Railway got hacked" (ours: "The platform I deploy on got hacked (Railway)"), "OpenCode was Hacked" (ours: "People found out they were hacked from their AI bill"), "Hackers got into Zeabur with admin tokens from 2024" (ours had no Zeabur), "eMudhra's security incident" (ours: "A security key that was the number 4"), "Amazon got a share of the PHP Attack" (ours: "Someone tried this PHP attack 4 days early").
2. **A plain news headline, not a riddle or teaser.** The label says what the story is; the short delivers the surprise. No "a key that was the number 4", no question-mark hooks ("Stolen AI keys used to train another AI?" became "Hackers are using stolen AI keys to train their own AI").
3. **Don't give the answer away in the label.** "Fixing PHP2Shell: update, then restart" became "How to Fix the PHP2Shell Vulnerability" (search wording, the short gives the steps).
4. **No audience call-outs, no first person.** "Laravel devs: …" became "updating Laravel won't fix PHP2Shell"; "The platform I deploy on…" became "Railway got hacked".
5. **Her own words.** "loophole" (what she says on camera) over our "flaw".
6. **Broad topic labels are fine for broad shorts**: "Cybersecurity Attacks in October", "The role of AI in Cyberattacks".
Rule of thumb: would someone typing the company or bug name into search recognise this as the answer? If the name isn't in the label, rewrite it.
