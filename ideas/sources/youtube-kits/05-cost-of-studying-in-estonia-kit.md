The next money you spend to study in Estonia is for your visa and your residence permit.

The residence permit comes later, after you arrive in Estonia.

When you get to Estonia, you apply for your residence permit for study, and the state fee for that is 225 euros.

**[ON SCREEN: D visa = €120 / residence permit for study = €225 when you apply in Estonia (Police and Border Guard Board student guide, July 2026)]**

**[SEGMENT 6: PROOF OF FUNDS FOR STUDYING IN ESTONIA]**

Proof of funds is the money you have to show you have when you apply to study in Estonia.

**[ON SCREEN: proof of funds = €880 a month per applicant (Police and Border Guard Board student guide, July 2026)]**

For the D visa, the guide asks for bank statements from the three months before you apply.

And for the residence permit, you need to have that 880 euros a month for the whole time your permit is valid.

I can't fully remember, but I think I showed a full year when I applied.

So there's always a fee per month, and you multiply it by however many months is required at the time.

Check with the embassy where you're applying, so you know what they want from you.

And have your proof of funds ready even before you apply, because you don't want to be scrambling for your bank statement, right?

### Title:

### Subtitle:
> Every cost of a master's in Estonia for a student from outside the EU, from the application fee to food, using official numbers as of October 2026 and my own experience.

### SEO Title:

---

People ask me how much it costs to study in Estonia almost every time I post a video. So I went through the costs a student from outside the EU meets, in the order you meet them, using official pages as of October 2026, and I'm adding my own experience where I have it. I think the cost of living in Estonia is a little bit expensive compared to other European countries I've been to, so it's worth planning properly.

The first cost is the application fee. Tallinn University, the University of Tartu and TalTech each charge international applicants 100 euros, and at each school that one payment lets you apply to two programmes. The fee is per university, so if you apply to two schools, you pay twice. Think of it as a form fee. You pay it to be considered, and you don't get it back if you aren't admitted.

Tuition is where the real money is. On Tallinn University's fee page, master's programmes starting in 2027 cost about 4,000 to 7,000 euros a year, and a few programmes charge students from outside the EU more. Mine is one of them. I paid 6,000 euros a year for Human-Computer Interaction, and the page now lists 7,000 for non-EU students. At the University of Tartu, International Relations is 4,800 euros a year for non-EU students, Software Engineering is 7,200, and Analytical Chemistry is 9,000. So check the page for your exact programme and your exact intake.

You also usually pay part of it before you arrive. I paid my first semester before coming in. Tallinn University asks non-EU students to pay the first semester's fee to finalise their acceptance. The University of Tartu asks for half of the first semester's fee after the admission decision and sends the official admission letter for the visa after that payment. TalTech wants the full first year before your acceptance is final.

Then come the visa and the permit. Non-EU students usually apply for a D visa first, which is what gets you into the country, and the residence permit comes after you arrive. The Police and Border Guard Board's July 2026 student guide puts the D visa fee at 120 euros and the residence permit for study at 225 euros when you apply in Estonia. If there's no Estonian embassy where you are, you travel to apply. I went to Cairo for mine, so flights and a hotel belong in the budget too. The same guide puts proof of funds at 880 euros a month, asks for bank statements from the three months before a visa application, and says the income has to hold for the whole time your permit is valid. Have your bank statement ready before you apply.

Health insurance is the one people forget. You need it to apply for the visa, and Tallinn University says non-EU students need internationally valid private health insurance. Mine didn't even cost up to 50,000 naira, and it didn't cover much. It was mainly for the visa. Someone I know used a provider called Swisscare and could use theirs a lot more. With private insurance here you usually pay at the hospital first and claim the money back, so read what your policy covers and don't let it run out. Once you start working in Estonia, you can get health insurance through work.

Rent depends on where you sleep. A place in the Tallinn University dorm is about 250 euros a month with utilities included, which is what I paid. I stayed about a month before moving out, and living outside the dorm, my budget was roughly double. The university puts private flats at 400 to 900 euros a month plus utilities, and you pay a deposit, even in the dorm. On a tight budget, I would absolutely advise the dorm, or renting a flat with friends and sharing it. Transport in Tallinn is free once you have your permit and you're registered as a resident. Before mine came, I paid around 30 euros a month. People I know who've lived in Tartu say it's much cheaper to live in, and a monthly bus card there is 12.95 euros if you're under 26.

Food is the cost you control most. Tallinn University puts a comfortable food budget at 300 to 500 euros a month, and I think that's generous. The normal stores are affordable for me. The African store is where my spending skyrockets. Add your phone too, with Telia, Tele2 or Elisa. I'm not going to total all of this for you, because your school, your programme and how you live change the number, so total it yourself. Students on a study permit can work while they study, but I wouldn't plan your first year around a job you don't have yet. Go through the official pages yourself, and do your research as aggressively as you can.
