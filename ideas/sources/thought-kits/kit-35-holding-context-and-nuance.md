## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

People talk about how, oh yeah, people can't read anymore, people are not smart anymore. But I don't think that the problem is that people can't read anymore, that people are not smart anymore, that people are not intellectual, intelligent anymore. I think so many things are going on. One of those things I think is that we are losing the ability to hold context and nuance.

And it is very important to hold context and nuance. It's almost like our ability to hold context when it comes to having conversations has ridiculously reduced. And it's interesting, because conversations that concern us as humans are always so nuanced, because we are very complex. We are not just black and white. And maybe I sound like a conspiracy theorist here, but I do think that social media has a lot to do with our inability to now hold context and nuance whenever we navigate conversations.

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**Her claim.** The problem isn't mainly that people got less intelligent. It's that we're losing two specific skills: holding a statement inside its context, and holding two true things at once. A very smart person can still read one line, react, and never ask what came before it.

**Accuracy on "people can still read."** Some decline is real. The OECD's Survey of Adult Skills (PIAAC, 2023 round, published December 2024) found adult literacy fell or stayed flat in most participating countries. So the script doesn't claim reading is fine. It says some skills are slipping, but that isn't the whole story.

**What holding context means.** Knowing who said it, when, to whom, in reply to what, and in what tone. Examples: a 10-second clip from a two-hour conversation, a screenshot of one tweet without the thread, a quote with the sentence before it cut off. Each can be accurate word for word and still mislead.

**What holding nuance means.** Being able to say "this is true, and that is also true" without feeling you lost. Her "we are not just black and white" is the bridge.

**Not a conspiracy theory.** Alice Marwick and danah boyd (2011) described "context collapse": online, what you say reaches many audiences at once and gets read without the setting it was said in. Platform design (short limits, clips, screenshots, quote-posts, feeds that reward fast reactions) strips context by default. Her hedge stays as a self-aware line, followed by the research.

**The ironic proof.** "Reading comprehension is dead" comments: usually the person read every word correctly and missed the context (the joke, the earlier post, who it was aimed at).

**Honest guard.** "You took it out of context" is sometimes used to dodge something that really was wrong. Holding context means looking at the whole thing, and sometimes the whole thing is still bad.

**Faith, light.** Proverbs 18:13 (KJV), "He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him."

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice, teleprompter format)

**[OPEN]**

People talk about how nobody can read anymore.

Nobody is smart anymore.

But I don't think that's the whole problem.

I think a lot of things are going on.

And one of them is that we're losing the ability to hold context and nuance.

**[SECTION 1: IT'S NOT JUST ABOUT BEING SMART]**

When people say nobody can read anymore, I want to be fair.

Some of it is true.

The OECD measures adult reading skills across many countries, and in its latest survey, reading skills fell or stayed flat in most of them.

So I'm not saying everything is fine.

But I don't think intelligence is the whole story.

Because I see very smart people, people who read very well, still miss the point of a conversation.

And I think what they're missing is context.

**[SECTION 2: WHAT HOLDING CONTEXT MEANS]**

Holding context means you don't just look at what was said.

You look at who said it, when they said it, who they said it to, and what they were replying to.

Think about a ten-second clip from a two-hour conversation.

Or a screenshot of one tweet, without the thread around it.

Or a quote where the sentence before it has been cut off.

Every word in that clip can be true.

And you can still walk away with the wrong meaning.

Holding context is the habit of asking, what's around this, before you decide what it means.

**[RETENTION BEAT, around 1:00: And there's a second thing we're losing, and it's the one that makes conversations about people so hard.]**

**[SECTION 3: WHAT HOLDING NUANCE MEANS]**

The second thing we're losing is nuance.

And it's very important to hold nuance.

Holding nuance means being able to say, this is true, and that is also true.

Without feeling like you've lost the argument.

And conversations about us as humans are always nuanced.

Because we are very complex.

We are not just black and white.

So when we lose the ability to hold nuance, we lose the ability to talk about people properly.

**[SECTION 4: MAYBE I SOUND LIKE A CONSPIRACY THEORIST]**

Now, maybe I sound like a conspiracy theorist here.

But I do think social media has a lot to do with this.

And it turns out there's actually research on it.

Two researchers, Alice Marwick and danah boyd, called it context collapse.

Online, what you say reaches lots of different people at once.

And they read it without the setting you said it in.

The apps are also built in a way that strips context out.

Short posts.

Clips.

Screenshots.

Quote posts.

And feeds that reward whoever reacts fastest.

So it's not that we suddenly became careless.

We spend hours every day in places built to cut things out of context.

**[SECTION 5: THE "PEOPLE CAN'T READ" COMMENTS]**

Here's something funny.

You've seen it in the comments.

Someone says, reading comprehension is dead.

But look at most of those arguments.

The person usually read every single word correctly.

What they missed was the context.

The joke.

The post before it.

Who it was actually talking to.

So even the complaint that people can't read is often a context problem.

**[RETENTION BEAT, around 3:00: But I don't want to be unfair here, because context can be misused too.]**

**[SECTION 6: CONTEXT IS NOT AN EXCUSE]**

Holding context doesn't mean everything is fine once you know the background.

Sometimes people say, you took it out of context, just to avoid owning something that really was wrong.

Holding context means looking at the whole thing.

And sometimes, when you look at the whole thing, it's still bad.

That's fine.

At least now you're judging what actually happened.

**[SECTION 7: WHAT THE BIBLE SAYS]**

There's a verse that says all of this in one line.

Proverbs 18:13.

He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.

Answering before you've heard the whole thing.

That's what reacting to a clip is.

**[SECTION 8: WHAT I'M TRYING TO DO]**

So this is what I'm trying to do.

Before I react, I ask, what's around this?

I watch past the clip.

I read the thread.

And I try to ask, what did they mean, before I ask, what did they say?

Do you find yourself reacting before you know the full story?

Tell me in the comments.
