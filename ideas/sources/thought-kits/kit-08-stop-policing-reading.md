# KIT 08 — Stop Policing Reading

**Premise:** We are too much of a non-reading people to be policing reading this much. The gatekeeping almost never comes from the big readers — it comes from people performing a relationship with reading rather than doing it. Just read. (Bite kept in; no faith thread.)

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

I absolutely don't like it when people police reading. Oh, I don't like people that read fiction, or people that read self-help are not serious, or people that read this are not… we don't even read that much for us to police reading this much. Just read. Let people read — ebook, physical book, whichever one you prefer, just freaking read, man. I want to read cooking books. I don't care. Just read. We are too much of non-readers for us to be policing reading this much.

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**The spine — the hypocrisy of scarcity.** "We are too much of non-readers to be policing reading this much." The reading police are almost never the big readers. Actual heavy readers are omnivores — they'll read a cereal box; they're not ranking your genres. Policing usually comes from people performing a *relationship* to reading rather than doing much of it. It's identity cosplay: "I'm the kind of person who looks down on self-help" is a cheap sentence to say; reading forty books a year is expensive. Gatekeeping is often inversely correlated with actual reading.

**The data (so it's more than an opinion).** Depending on survey, between a quarter and nearly half of US adults read no book in the past year. YouGov Dec 2025: 40% read zero, median 2 books among all respondents. Reading for pleasure on an average day fell ~28% (early 2000s) → ~16% (2023). The town-where-nobody-exercises-and-three-people-argue-about-treadmills analogy.

**The taxonomy of policing (and it self-contradicts).** Genre (fiction = escapism / non-fiction = joyless), format (ebooks/audiobooks "don't count"), difficulty (not literary = beneath you), purpose (fun = frivolous / improvement = try-hard). The rules contradict each other — every choice is wrong to someone — which proves it was never about the books. It's about having something to feel superior about.

**Audiobook myth, killed with science.** Comprehension between reading and listening is broadly comparable for most material (Willingham et al.) once decoding is accounted for. "Audiobooks don't count" isn't just snobbish, it's wrong.

**Defend the bullied genres.** Self-help = someone quietly trying to get better/heal/manage money/be less anxious — nothing embarrassing about improving your own life. Romance = most mocked and most commercially dominant, mostly women reading about being wanted/safe/chosen — worth noticing why *that* genre draws *that* mockery. Cooking books = so obviously harmless it exposes the absurdity; someone will still have an opinion.

**The landing.** Reading is one of the few cheap, quiet, good habits left, under attack from a thousand louder things fighting for the same minutes. Gatekeeping a dying practice is self-sabotage for the whole culture of reading — every time you shame someone's format or genre, you manufacture one more non-reader. Whatever gets somebody turning pages, protect it.

---

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

I absolutely cannot stand it when people police reading.

Oh, I don't respect people who read fiction. Oh, people who read self-help are not serious. Oh, if you read romance you're not really reading. Oh, ebooks don't count. Oh, audiobooks? That's not reading, you're just listening. Everybody has become a reading referee all of a sudden. And I'm looking at all of you like, since when? Since when did we, of all people, earn the right to police reading this much?

Because let's be real about the actual numbers here. In the US, depending on which survey you check, somewhere between a quarter and nearly half of adults did not read a single book in the past year. Not one. A YouGov survey at the end of 2025 found forty percent of Americans didn't finish one book all year, and of the people who did read, the median was two books. Two. For the whole year. Reading for pleasure on an average day has been falling for two decades. So this is the field we are standing on. Most people are not reading at all. And in the middle of that, a small handful of people who do read have somehow decided that their real job is to stand at the door and rank everybody else's choices.

It's like being in a town where almost nobody exercises, and the three people who go to the gym are standing in the corner arguing about whether the treadmill is "real" cardio. My brother. My sister. Nobody is exercising. Sit down.

And have you noticed it's never actually the big readers doing this? The people who genuinely read, like read a lot, read wide, they are the most relaxed people about it. They'll read a novel, then a biography, then a book about mushrooms, then the back of a cereal box. They're not out here gatekeeping, because they actually love the thing. The policing almost always comes from people performing a relationship with reading rather than actually doing much of it. Because "I'm the type of person who looks down on self-help" is a very cheap sentence to say. It costs you nothing. It's a personality you can wear without reading a single page. Actually reading forty books a year is expensive, it costs time and attention, so people buy the aesthetic of the reader instead, and the aesthetic comes with a nose you can look down.

And here's the thing that gives the whole game away. The rules contradict each other completely. If you read fiction, they say you're escaping reality, you're not reading anything useful. Okay, so you read non-fiction instead, and now they say you're joyless, you only read to optimise yourself, you can't just enjoy a story. You read something literary and difficult, you're a snob. You read something fun and easy, you're not a serious reader. There is literally no book you can pick up that somebody won't have a problem with. And when every possible choice is wrong, that tells you the choice was never the point. It was never about the books. It was about having something, anything, to feel superior about.

Let me defend the ones that get bullied the most. Self-help gets the most sneering, right, but what is self-help usually? It's a person quietly trying to be better. Trying to manage their money, trying to be less anxious, trying to heal something, trying to fix a habit that's hurting them. Since when is a human being trying to improve their own life something to be embarrassed about? Romance is the most mocked genre on earth and also one of the best-selling, which is already funny, and it's mostly women reading about being wanted and safe and chosen, and I'll let you sit with why that specific genre attracts that specific amount of mockery.

And the audiobook thing, let me just kill it, because people say it with so much confidence. "Listening isn't really reading." Actually, the research says comprehension between reading text and listening to it is broadly the same for most material. Your brain gets to the meaning either way. So the single mother listening to a book on her commute because that's the only twenty minutes she has, she is reading. Full stop. She's doing better than the person who read zero books this year but has strong opinions about her format.

Me, personally? I want to read cooking books. I want to read about food. And that is the most harmless thing in the entire world, and I promise you somebody would still find a way to have an opinion about it. That's how you know it's a sickness.

So here is my whole point. Reading is one of the few genuinely good, cheap, quiet habits we have left, and it is under attack from a thousand louder, faster things fighting for the same little pockets of your time. It is barely surviving. And instead of protecting it, instead of just being glad that anybody at all is still turning pages, some readers spend their energy making the door smaller. That is self-sabotage for the entire culture of reading. Because every time you make somebody feel stupid for how they read or what they read, you don't create a better reader. You create one more person who quietly decides reading is not for them. You manufacture a non-reader.

So please. Physical book, ebook, audiobook, whichever one you prefer. Fiction, non-fiction, literary, trashy, self-help, romance, cookbooks, whatever gets you turning the page. We are too much of a non-reading people to be policing reading this much. Just read. Just freaking read, man.

## REFERENCES
- **Non-readers:** Pew (2021) ~23% of US adults read no book in whole/part in the past year. YouGov (Dec 2025, 2,203 adults): 40% read zero, median 2 books among respondents. YouGov (Dec 2023): 46% hadn't read/listened to a book that year. Surveys differ by method; all point the same direction. Script uses the honest range ("between a quarter and nearly half") so no single stat is overstated.
- **Reading-for-pleasure decline:** American Time Use Survey — reading for enjoyment on an average day fell from ~28% (early 2000s) to ~16% (2023).
- **Audiobook comprehension:** research (e.g. Daniel Willingham) finds listening vs reading comprehension broadly comparable for most material once decoding is accounted for. Script says "broadly the same for most material" — accurate; edge cases (dense technical text, re-reading) favour print.
