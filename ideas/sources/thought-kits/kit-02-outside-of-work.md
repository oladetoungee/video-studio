# KIT 02 — Who Are You Outside of Work

**Premise:** Your company can separate you from your work overnight. If your work is your whole identity, they can separate you from yourself overnight. Told through two cultural extremes I've personally lived.

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

Who are you outside of work? If they take you out of your work boundaries, out of the office, the company you work in, the industry we work in, if everything gets taken away from you, then what's left? I think we're currently in an age where productivity is ridiculously at a high rate. Like you need to be productive. You need to work, work, work, work, work. You need to get it, you need to make it, you need to this and that. That we all just exist in work. You do nine to five and that's it.

I was talking to someone the other day and they were like — they get back from work and they are just dead because they are tired, and I know that feeling, like you're just done. And with the situation of things with the labour matter today and how much the value of human labour is being played around with following the entry of AI, I think it's time for every individual that works to sit themselves down. Because whether you do that or not, your company could choose to do that for you tomorrow morning. People are getting laid off. Like you go to work on Wednesday, you do your work, you have plans of what to do on Thursday, and then you wake up to a layoff email and your access to Slack is blocked before the next morning.

All of this, of course, and many more, even before all of this, is reason why I think you need to sit yourself down and ask yourself, outside of work, what is my life? I moved to Europe like about ten months ago, started working fully about eight to nine-ish months ago, and I think that living in Europe, working in Europe, has completely changed my view of work in relation to my life. Like work is important, but my view of it in relation to my life has completely turned around. I come from Nigeria. I've lived my own life in Nigeria. Nigeria is an also place. You also work...

*(...trailed off here; picked up in the exploration below)*

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**The hook is the Wednesday image.** You work Wednesday, make plans for Thursday, wake to a layoff email with Slack already revoked. It's the coldest, most concrete version of the whole argument, and it's widely reported from the tech layoff waves. Everything hangs off it: they can separate you from your work overnight, so if work is your whole identity, they can separate you from *yourself* overnight. It's not a self-care question, it's a security question.

**Enmeshment.** Psychologists' term for when the boundary between you and your job dissolves so completely that losing the job feels like losing yourself. HBR (Janna Koretz, 2019) documents people falling into anxiety and depression on job loss — not just lost income, a lost self. Opens with a partner at a law firm curled on his bathroom floor asking "who am I if not this?"

**Brooks' territory too.** *Success addiction* — achievement works like a substance, tolerance builds, you need bigger hits, and high performers often use work as a substitute for love and connection. His Atlantic piece on professional decline arriving sooner than you think says basically the thesis: build the parts of you that survive the job, because the job will leave you before you leave it.

**The cultural comparison is what makes it MINE.** I've lived the Nigerian version (hustle as survival + culture, sometimes baptised by church, multiple streams or you're not serious) and now I watch Estonians close laptops and just go. Same humans, completely different settlement between work and life. So: if the relationship changes depending on which country you're standing in, it was never a law of nature. It was always a *setting*. And settings can be changed.

**Gravity / the sun.** In one culture work is the sun — you don't decide to orbit it, the whole environment pulls you into orbit. In Nigeria "wetin you dey do?" is almost the first identity question; respect and seriousness are calibrated to work. And that's not shallowness — it's rational. No safety net, one salary carrying a whole extended family: work being central is *load-bearing*, not a lifestyle choice. Lagos hustle and San Francisco hustle are spelled the same and mean opposite things — survival vs aesthetic. Honour that or the Nigeria half becomes a caricature.

**Sabbath** *(PARKED as its own future essay — do not fold in here).* The original protest against defining people by output; Heschel, "human beings not human doings."

**Identity diversification** *(PARKED as its own future essay).* People diversify income so one source can't sink them; nobody diversifies identity. Most people run a single-asset identity portfolio, 95% in job title, then act shocked when one email crashes the account.

**Two essays were hiding inside the original** — "who are you outside work" (meaning) and "AI/layoffs mean find out fast" (urgency). Decision: identity is the *spine*, the layoff era is the *why now*.

**The landing = an audit.** List what's actually yours — the things that don't need an employer's permission to exist. For me that list is long; for the friend who comes home dead every evening it might be frighteningly short. The essay is for them.

---

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

Who are you outside of work? If they take you out of your work boundaries, out of the office, out of the company you work in, out of the industry entirely, if everything work gets taken away from you, what is left?

I've been thinking about this a lot because we are currently in an age where productivity is ridiculously high. Like you need to be productive, you need to work, work, work, work, work. You need to get it, you need to make it, you need to do this and that. And somewhere inside all of that, a lot of us have quietly started to just exist in work. You do your nine to five and that's it, that is the day. I was talking to someone the other day and they were telling me that when they get back from work they are just dead. Just tired, just done, nothing left in the tank for anything else. And I know that feeling. I'm not judging anybody, I know that feeling very well.

But then look at what is happening with labour right now. Look at how the value of human work is being played with since AI entered the room. People are getting laid off in ways that would have sounded like fiction a few years ago. You go to work on Wednesday, you do your work, you make plans for Thursday, and then you wake up to a layoff email and your Slack access is already blocked before the next morning. Before the next morning! Your whole professional existence, gone, before you even brushed your teeth.

Now imagine that email lands and your work was your entire identity. It means a stranger in HR just deleted you. Psychologists actually have a word for this, they call it enmeshment, when the boundary between you and your job disappears so completely that losing the job feels like losing yourself, and people in that position genuinely fall into anxiety and depression because there is nothing underneath to land on. So this "who are you outside work" question is no longer some soft journal prompt. It's a security question. Because whether you sit yourself down and answer it or not, your company can decide to ask it for you tomorrow morning.

For me this question became loud because of the move I made. I moved to Europe about ten months ago, started working full time here about nine months ago, and living and working here has completely changed my view of work in relation to my life. Not that work stopped being important. Work is important. But its position in the arrangement of my life has turned around completely.

Because I come from Nigeria, and I've lived my full life in Nigeria, so let me explain what I mean carefully, because I'm not about to sit here and caricature my own country. It's not that people in Nigeria don't have lives. We have lives o, rich ones, loud ones, full of family and church and food and people. But the way work sits inside that life, it is so central, so integral, that even when you are not intentionally making it central, it becomes this defining thing that every other part of your life has to adjust itself around. Your rest adjusts to work. Your friendships adjust to work. Your church schedule adjusts to work. Work itself adjusts to nothing. And honestly, a lot of that is not madness, it's mathematics. When there is no safety net, when one salary might be carrying a whole family, work being central is not a lifestyle choice, it is load-bearing. The hustle is survival before it is culture. Even the way we greet, one of the first questions anybody will ask you is what do you do. Your seriousness as a person is measured through it.

Then I moved here, and I'm watching people close their laptops at five and just go. Actually go. Nobody is messaging anybody at 9pm. Summer comes and colleagues disappear on holiday for weeks and somehow the company does not collapse. The same work, the same human beings, but work is sitting in a completely different position in their lives. Here it feels like work is one room in the house. Where I come from, work can quietly become the foundation the whole house is standing on.

And living on both extremes taught me the thing I actually want to say, which is this. If the relationship between work and life can be this different in two places I have personally lived, then that relationship was never a law of nature. It was always a setting. And a setting can be examined. A setting can be changed, small small, even if your environment is still shouting at you to hustle.

So the sitting down I'm talking about is really an audit. Sit yourself down and list what is actually yours. The things that do not need an employer's permission to exist. Your faith. Your people, the ones who love you with no job title attached. The things your hands know how to do, the cooking, the creating, whatever it is for you. The person you are to your family. The things you are learning just because. That list is who you are outside of work, and nobody can block your access to it before the next morning.

And if you write that list and it's frighteningly short, if the honest answer is that outside of work there is almost nothing there, please don't let that crush you. Let it inform you. Because the email might never come, I pray it never comes for you. But the question is coming regardless, one way or another, retirement, change, life itself will ask it eventually. Better you answer it on your own terms, at your own table, than in front of a locked Slack screen on a Thursday morning you had other plans for.
