# KIT 26 — Human Beings Need Other Human Beings

**Premise:** Prolonged isolation is used as punishment because it works, even on the hardest people. We are built to need each other, and you cannot "I don't care" your way out of it. Alone time is healthy; isolation is not. And the first thing God ever called "not good" was a man being alone.

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**The prison point is real and has a number.** The UN's Nelson Mandela Rules (2015) define solitary confinement as 22+ hours a day without meaningful human contact, and ban "prolonged" solitary, meaning more than 15 consecutive days, as cruel treatment. People held alone for long stretches report losing track of time, anxiety, paranoia, and hallucinations. Nothing physical is done to them. Removing people is enough.

**It hurts outside prison too.** The US Surgeon General's 2023 advisory on loneliness said lacking social connection raises the risk of early death about as much as smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day. Useful because everyone already accepts that smoking harms you.

**Her phrase is the spine: "you cannot 'I don't care' your way out of it."** Aimed at the online hyper-independence culture (cut them off, protect your peace, I don't need anybody). Honest about why people pull back (hurt, being used) without letting it stand as the answer.

**Validation, de-shamed.** Everyone wants to be seen. A baby taking its first step looks back to check someone saw. Adults don't lose that; they hide it.

**Honesty guard.** Solitude and isolation are different. Introverts (she included) love their own company, and alone time is where people rest, think, pray. Alone time is a room you go into and come out of; isolation is when you stop coming out. Introverts still need people, maybe fewer and quieter.

**Faith.** Genesis 1: God calls each thing he makes good. Genesis 2:18: in a perfect garden, before sin, the first "not good" is a man being alone. So needing people was part of the design, not a flaw that came later.

**Landing.** Plain action, no kicker. Stop being ashamed of needing people; reply the message, call your people, say yes to the invite, go where people are. Ends on an echo of her own "it is being human."

## 3. THE FINAL (my voice)

There's a reason why prolonged isolation is a punishment. You will hear it about the craziest prisons, that if they really want to punish someone, they isolate them. And there's a reason that works as a punishment, even for the craziest of people. Because we are social creatures. Human beings.

We are innately social creatures. We crave each other. We crave other people in different forms. Validation. Love. Company. All kinds. We crave it. It is inbuilt. You cannot "I don't care" your way out of it. You are stuck in it. It is who you are. It is being human.

And the prison thing is not me exaggerating. The United Nations has a set of rules for how prisoners should be treated, they're called the Mandela Rules, and they say that keeping someone in solitary confinement for more than fifteen days in a row counts as cruel treatment. Fifteen days. People who are kept alone for long stretches start to lose track of time. They get anxious and paranoid, and some of them start seeing and hearing things that aren't there. Nobody laid a hand on them. The only thing taken from them was other people, and that alone is enough to break a person.

And you don't have to be in prison for it to hurt you. In 2023, the US Surgeon General put out a warning about loneliness, and one of the things it said was that not having enough social connection raises your risk of dying early about as much as smoking up to fifteen cigarettes a day. We all know smoking is bad for us. Somehow we don't talk about being alone that way.

That's why this whole "I don't need anybody" energy bothers me. You see it everywhere online. Cut them off. Protect your peace. I'm my own best friend. I don't need nobody. And I understand where some of it comes from. People have been hurt. People have been used. Pulling back feels safer. But needing people is not a weakness you can train out of yourself. It's how you were made. You can post the quotes and act like you're fine on your own, but at 2am, when something goes wrong, you will want somebody to call. Everybody does.

Even validation. We talk about wanting validation like it's something embarrassing, like only insecure people want it. Everybody wants to be seen. Everybody wants someone to look at what they did and say, well done. Watch a baby take its first step. The first thing it does is look back at its mother, to check if somebody saw. That doesn't go away when you grow up. You just get better at hiding it.

Now, being alone and being isolated are two different things, and I want to say this properly, because some of us are introverts and we love our own company. I love my own company. Time alone can be good for you. It's where a lot of people rest and think and pray. But alone time is a room you go into and come out of. Isolation is when the door closes and you stop coming out. Even the most introverted person still needs a few people. Maybe fewer than the extrovert. Maybe quieter ones. But they still need them.

And this goes all the way back to the beginning. In Genesis, God makes the light, the land, the sea, the animals, and after each one he says it is good. Good, good, good. Then he looks at Adam, in a perfect garden, with nothing broken yet, no sin, no pain, and for the first time he says something is not good. It is not good for man to be alone. Before anything went wrong in the world, being alone was already the thing God called not good. So needing people was never a flaw that came in later. It was part of the design from the start.

So stop being ashamed of needing people. Stop acting like wanting love, wanting company, wanting someone to notice you, is something you should grow out of. You won't grow out of it. None of us will. Reply to the message you've been ignoring. Call your people. Say yes to the invite. Go where people are, even when it feels awkward at first. You're a human being, and human beings need other human beings.

## REFERENCES
- **UN Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners (Nelson Mandela Rules, 2015), Rules 43–44** — solitary confinement = 22+ hours/day without meaningful human contact; "prolonged" = more than 15 consecutive days; indefinite and prolonged solitary are prohibited.
- **US Surgeon General's Advisory, *Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation* (2023)** — lacking social connection carries a mortality risk comparable to smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day.
- **Effects of prolonged solitary** (anxiety, paranoia, time distortion, hallucinations) — well documented in the psychiatric literature on solitary confinement (e.g. Stuart Grassian's work). Kept general and non-graphic.
- **Genesis 1** (God calls each part of creation good) and **Genesis 2:18** ("It is not good that the man should be alone") — the first "not good" in the Bible. Accurate.
- **Infant "looking back"** — social referencing in infants (checking a caregiver's reaction) is well established; used as an everyday observation.
