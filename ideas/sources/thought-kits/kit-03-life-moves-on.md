# KIT 03 — Life Moves On (Fresh Deliveries)

**Premise:** "Life moves on" is usually comfort. Read literally it's almost a threat — life doesn't move on *from* your troubles, it just moves, and today arrives with its own fresh deliveries whether you unpacked yesterday's or not. Observation voice.

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

Life moves on is such a literal phrase in all ramifications. Because life actually moves on. You think you're overwhelmed by the trials and demands and remnants of yesterday, and before you blink your eyes to take that in, today pops in with its own fresh deliveries o.

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**The flip.** "Life moves on" is normally offered as consolation — the pain will pass, time heals. Read literally it's a threat: life does not wait, it doesn't move on *from* your troubles, it just moves. You're still standing in yesterday's rubble and today has already arrived with its own full load. Same sentence: comfort in one mouth, pressure in another. The essay lives in that double face.

**Jesus made the exact point.** Matthew 6:34 — "do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own." *Sufficient unto the day.* That's the original thought in King James clothing. The instruction isn't denial, it's *rationing*: one day's trouble per day is the design spec. Most overwhelm is us violating the spec, carrying three days at once — yesterday's remnants + today's fresh + tomorrow's imagined — on a back built for one.

**The beautiful tension right next door.** Lamentations 3 — "his mercies are new every morning." The mornings that keep relentlessly arriving with fresh demands are the same mornings arriving with fresh mercy. Same courier, two packages. The door that keeps opening to admit new trouble is the same door admitting new grace.

**Grief is where it's sharpest.** The strange offence of the world continuing — buses run, people laugh in cafés, your inbox fills. You want one minute of silence and the world won't give it. Also true in the small everyday version: you didn't even finish being tired from yesterday.

**Work-brain framing.** Yesterday's unresolved stuff is an unclosed sprint; today opens its own tickets regardless of your backlog. Unprocessed life compounds — you service interest on days you never closed.

**Possible landing.** If you can't stop the conveyor, the skill is learning to *close* days — actually end them, not just fall asleep mid-carry.

**Register decision:** OBSERVATION voice (standing a little outside it, pointing), not confession from inside the pile-up. Her call, confirmed.

**The metaphor came from her own mouth:** "fresh deliveries o." Every morning is a delivery you never ordered and cannot refuse — no "sorry we missed you" slip, no reschedule, no return address. Life is the only courier with a perfect delivery record. And mercy comes by the same courier.

---

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

Life moves on is such a literal phrase, in all ramifications.

Because life actually moves on. You think you're still overwhelmed by the trials and the demands and the remnants of yesterday, and before you even blink your eyes properly to take that in, today pops in with its own fresh deliveries o. Fresh ones. Nobody asked you if you were ready. Nobody checked whether you finished unpacking yesterday's own.

That's the part we don't hear when we say the phrase. Normally we offer it as comfort. Somebody is going through something and we tell them, don't worry, life moves on, and what we mean is the pain will pass, time will do its healing thing. Which is true. But take the phrase literally for one second and you'll see its other face. Life moves on means life does not wait. It doesn't move on from your problems, it just moves. Full stop. Whether you have processed anything or not. You're still standing in the middle of yesterday's scattered boxes and the doorbell is ringing again. Today has arrived, and today came with its own full load.

I started thinking of every morning like a delivery you never ordered and cannot refuse. There's no "sorry we missed you" slip. No rescheduling. No return address to send it back to. You open your eyes and the package is already on the doorstep. Life is honestly the only courier with a perfect delivery record. Rain, grief, public holiday, it does not matter, the van still comes.

Anyone who has lost something big knows the strange offence of this. The world just continues. Buses still run. People are laughing in cafes like nothing happened. Your inbox is still filling up. You almost want the whole world to observe one minute of silence for your situation and the world simply will not do it. But it's also true in the small everyday version. You didn't even finish being tired from yesterday, you haven't concluded yesterday's matter in your head, and today is already presenting its own agenda.

Now, this could easily become a depressing observation, so let me tell you why it isn't, at least not for me. Jesus made this exact point, and I find it so funny that we quote it all the time without noticing how literal he was being. Matthew 6, do not worry about tomorrow, tomorrow will worry about itself, each day has enough trouble of its own. Each day has enough trouble of its own! That is scripture agreeing with the courier. The days come pre-loaded, that is simply the design. And that is exactly why the instruction is one day at a time. It's not motivation talk, it's rationing. One day's delivery per day, that is what your back was built to carry. Most of our overwhelm, if you check it, is us carrying three days at once. Yesterday's remnants, today's fresh one, plus tomorrow's own that hasn't even shipped yet, we've already collected it in our imagination.

And there's one more thing about this courier that changes everything for me. The book of Lamentations, of all places, a book written from inside actual rubble, says his mercies are new every morning. New every morning. Same delivery schedule as the trouble. So it's two packages on the same doorstep, every single day, and both of them are fresh. The relentlessness that won't let you rest is the same relentlessness that won't let you stay locked inside yesterday. The door that keeps opening to admit new demands is the same door admitting new grace. Life refusing to pause is annoying until you realize it's also life refusing to leave you behind.

So no, you cannot stop the conveyor. The deliveries will keep coming, that one is settled, it's not in your hands. What is in your hands is which package you sign for first each morning, and whether you learn to actually close a day. Finish it. Unpack what you can, thank God for what went well, hand over what you couldn't resolve, and let the day end. Because the van is coming again tomorrow. It always comes. And apparently, so does the mercy.
