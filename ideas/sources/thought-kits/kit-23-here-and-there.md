# KIT 23 — Here, and There, and Here Again

**Premise:** Life isn't a line, it's an oscillation — here and there, well then not well, things fall into place then fall out. The wave isn't the malfunction, it's the design. And the same motion that means nothing bad lasts also means nothing good does — so hold the good loosely and the hard lightly. Short and soft, almost a psalm.

**Register:** short, soft, poem-adjacent (like the one-size-fits-all kit, not the essays). Making peace, gently. Faith woven in.

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

Life is so here and there. Things work and things don't work. Always well and then things are not well. Feel happy and then you feel sad. Things fall into places and things fall out of places. So here and there. I…

*(trailed off at "I"; steer: short and soft, faith in, observational / making-peace register)*

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**The shape she landed on: life is an oscillation, not a trajectory.** We're sold life as up-and-to-the-right; we live it and it's here and there. The disappointment people carry isn't the hard parts themselves — it's being promised a line and getting a wave, so every downswing feels like failure/doing-something-wrong when it was always part of the pattern. The wave isn't the malfunction; the wave is the design.

**Protect her sharpest phrasing: "fall into place / fall out of place."** It names what people forget — falling into place was never permanent either. We treat the good arrangement as the arrived-state and the bad as the intrusion, but they're the same motion. What fell in will fall out; what fell out will fall back in; neither is the final word. Life doesn't break sometimes — it moves, always; stillness was never on the menu.

**The old version (scriptural + universal): Ecclesiastes 3.** "A time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to break down and a time to build up." Her thought, ~3,000 years old — and notably NOT depressing there; presented as wisdom, almost relief. The relief is in the acknowledgment: when someone says plainly "it's both, it's always been both," something unclenches, because you stop waiting for the day it becomes only the good half.

**The gentle two-way turn (the comfort of the piece).** The oscillation cuts both ways. Bad isn't permanent → hold on. But good isn't permanent either → actually receive it while it's here; don't stand in it waiting for it to fail or rush past it. The wave that won't let the bad settle also won't let the good settle — same mercy pointed two directions.

**Landing (soft, not preachy).** Used to fight the here-and-there, wanted it to pick a side. Making peace: the same movement that means nothing good lasts means nothing bad does. The work isn't to escape the oscillation — it's to hold the good more loosely and the hard more lightly, because both are only passing through.

**Second scripture for the turn:** Psalm 30:5 — "weeping may endure for a night, but joy comes in the morning" — same shape, adds the turn that "there" is no more permanent than "here." Extended honestly in the final (another night comes, then another morning) so it's not a false "and then it's all good forever."

**Decisions:** short & soft; faith IN; observational/making-peace (her trailed-off "I" resolved as gentle acceptance, not weariness — safest reading of "peace with it").

---

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

Life is so here and there.

Things work, and then things don't work. Things are well, and then things are not well. You feel happy, and then you feel sad. Things fall into place, and then they fall right back out of place.

Here and there. That's just what it is.

And I used to fight that. I think a lot of us do. We want life to just pick a side and stay there. We treat the good stretch like it's the real life finally arriving, and the hard stretch like it's an interruption, like something went wrong, like we did something wrong. But I'm slowly learning that the wave isn't the malfunction. The wave is just how it moves. It was never going to be a straight line up. It was always going to be here, and then there, and then here again.

And the thing I keep forgetting is that falling into place was never permanent either. I always remember that the bad doesn't last. I forget that the good doesn't sit still either. It's the same motion carrying both of them. The things that fell into place will shift, and the things that fell apart will come back together, and round it goes.

There's that old passage, a time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to break down and a time to build up. And it's funny, it's not written like a sad thing. It's written like wisdom. Almost like relief. Because I think something in you unclenches when somebody finally just says it plainly. Yes. It's both. It has always been both. You can stop waiting for the day it becomes only the good half, because that day was never coming, and that's okay.

So here's where I'm landing, softly. The same movement that means nothing bad lasts forever also means nothing good does. And instead of fighting that, I'm trying to hold the good a little more loosely, and the hard a little more lightly. Taste the good while it's here, because here doesn't stay. And breathe through the there, because there doesn't stay either.

Weeping may last for the night. But joy comes in the morning. And then, honestly, another night comes. And then another morning. Here, and there, and here again.

I think I'm finally okay with that.

## REFERENCES
- **Ecclesiastes 3:1–8** — "a time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to break down and a time to build up." Quoted accurately, paraphrased in delivery.
- **Psalm 30:5** — "weeping may endure for a night, but joy comes in the morning." Quoted accurately; the script honestly extends it (another night, another morning) rather than implying permanent arrival — keeps the oscillation truthful.
- No empirical claims — a reflective/comfort piece.

## PRODUCTION NOTES
- Register: SHORT and SOFT — closest to the one-size-fits-all kit, not the essays. Resist expanding it; the brevity is the tone. Deliver slow, low, almost to yourself.
- Keep her exact phrasings: "here and there," "fall into place / fall out of place," "things work and things don't work."
- The two-way turn is the heart — deliver gently: **"the same movement that means nothing bad lasts forever also means nothing good does."**
- The Psalm ending is deliberately NOT a tidy happy-ever-after ("and then another night comes") — that honesty is what keeps it from feeling like a cliché. Don't cut the extension.
- Closer: **"Here, and there, and here again. I think I'm finally okay with that."** — let the last line sit; don't add anything after it.
- Pull-quotes: "The wave isn't the malfunction. The wave is the design." · "Hold the good a little more loosely, and the hard a little more lightly." · "Here, and there, and here again."
- If she ever wants the weary version instead of the at-peace version (the trailed-off "I" could have gone either way), the ending flips easily — ask before assuming.
