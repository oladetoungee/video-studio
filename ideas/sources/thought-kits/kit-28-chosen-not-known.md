# KIT 28: Chosen by God, Not Known by People

**Premise:** You can be chosen by God and your name can stay unknown to people. Somewhere along the line success got redefined to include being known, so people with real impact on the ten people around them still feel like they're doing nothing with their lives. Popularity is not a criteria for God using someone, and it's not the evidence of it either. In Exodus 18 the leader over ten was chosen by the same standard as the leader over a thousand.

**Register:** her dictated version is the base (minimum effective edit). Reflective, confessional ("I'm saying this to myself"), faith-centered. Triggered by a sermon: *When God Chooses You*, Apostle Stephanie Ike Okafor.

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**Her real subject is a redefinition.** Success quietly picked up a new ingredient, being known. Once that happens, impact that has no audience stops registering as impact, even to the person doing it. Her sharpest evidence is the sad person who is visibly doing a lot for the people around them and still says "I'm not doing anything with my life." Nothing in their life changed. Only the measuring stick did.

**The sermon excerpt, and whose sons these are.** Her transcription says the fourth son ended up executed and the fifth to the eighth are only names. That lines up exactly with David's sons. By birth order across 2 Samuel 3:2-5 and 2 Samuel 5:14, the fourth is Adonijah, whom Solomon had executed (1 Kings 2:25). The fifth to eighth are Shephatiah, Ithream, Shammua and Shobab, and scripture gives us their names and nothing else. **Confirm against the sermon before saying "David" on camera**, because the excerpt she captured doesn't name him. The final keeps her wording and doesn't name the family.
- *Bonus if it is David:* the ninth son, Nathan, is also only a name in the story of David's reign, yet Luke 3:31 traces Jesus's genealogy through Nathan, not Solomon. An unknown son carried the line. Not used in the final, because it rests on the unconfirmed identification. Good material for a follow-up.

**Popularity is not a criteria, and it's not evidence.** She says both, and they're different claims. God doesn't choose people *because* they're known (criteria). And being known doesn't prove God is using someone (evidence). The ministry-size point hits the second one. A big church can be proof of many things. It isn't automatically proof of a calling.

**Isaiah 55:8-9.** "As the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways." Her dictation had "far from the head," fixed.

**The Jethro story is her strongest image, and she ran out of breath before finishing it.** In Exodus 18:21 Jethro tells Moses to choose capable men who fear God, trustworthy men who hate dishonest gain, and set them over thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens. The standard for the leader over ten is word for word the same as the standard for the leader over a thousand. Nobody was put over ten because they were less. The ten needed a leader, and their matters were as real to them as anyone else's. This is the completion her ramble was reaching for, so the final finishes it plainly. (She said "10, 5, 20, 50, 100"; the verse is thousands, hundreds, fifties, tens.)

**The "global" usernames point** is a small, sharp, relatable observation and stays in her wording. One dictation fragment ("global like baja") was unclear and is left out.

**The sex trafficking line** is part of the sermon excerpt and cuts off mid-sentence, so it doesn't go in the final.

**Honesty guard, already in her words.** "It is okay to be global." She's not against reach or big ministries. She's against making reach the proof. Kept.

**Decisions:** her dictated version is the base; minimum effective edit; sermon credited by name with a short paraphrase rather than a long quote; Jethro thought completed; ending kept as her own "I'm rambling now."

Publicity, and fame, and popularity have become a crucial part of it.

So if you are not known, it's easy to not see any impact or success in what you're doing.


Even the popularity of the word global.

Have you noticed how many Instagram usernames are the global this, the global that?

And it's okay to be global.

But at the heart of it, for so many people, consciously or unconsciously, wanting to be global comes from a belief that being known is a key ingredient of success.

So if you take that away, and all of a sudden you're not global, and your impact is just within your close community, it's easy to think of yourself as unsuccessful.


You see people doing pretty well.

They have impact on the people around them, on the community around them.

And they're still so sad.

Oh my God, I'm not doing anything with my life.

And you're looking at them like, oh my goodness, you're doing so much with your life.

The fact that you don't have ten thousand people, or a hundred thousand people, following you doesn't mean you're not.


And this sermon just beats it down.

Please go and watch it.


When we talk about God choosing people, God calling people, the first thing that comes to our minds is that God chooses you, then a lot of people know you, then you're popular.

And I want you to always keep at the back of your mind, and I'm saying this to myself, that popularity is not a criteria for God using someone.

And it's not the evidence of God using someone.


Even with pastors.

You'll notice people tend to believe God truly called someone because they have a lot of people in their ministry.

The size of the ministry, the number of people, becomes the proof that the ministry is successful.

And that is not the way of God.

The way of man is not the way of God.

Scripture says as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are his ways higher than our ways.


You don't need a million and one people to hear you.

You don't need a million and one people to listen to you, or greet you, or welcome you, or talk about how much of an impact you've made in their life.

Not for you to be used by God.

And not for you to know that God is using you, or that what you're doing matters.


Don't forget.

Keep at the heart of everything you do that you're performing for an audience of one.

And he chooses to use you however he wants.

He can use you within a community of ten people.


There's this series I've been watching on YouTube, about the Israelites in the wilderness.

Jethro watches Moses trying to judge everybody's matter by himself, from morning till evening.

And he tells him to choose leaders over the people.

Leaders over thousands, over hundreds, over fifties, and over tens.

Same standard.

Capable people, people who fear God, trustworthy people, people who hate dishonest gain.

Nobody was put over ten people because they were less.
