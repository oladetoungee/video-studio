# KIT 34: Technology Is Taking More Than It Gives

**Premise:** We need to be much more careful with technology, with how it's built and how much of our lives we hand over to it. This isn't doom-saying. But I believe it now takes more from us than it gives. Algorithms decide what we eat, what we wear, who we meet and who we love. When the AI tokens run out, it can feel like life has stopped. And one question cuts through it: if I couldn't post this, would I still do it?

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**Replacing "stewarding."** She asked for something other than "stewarding," ideally a phrase. Using: "we need to be much more careful with technology, with how it's built and how much of our lives we hand over to it." Plainer, and it covers both the builders and the users.

**The accuracy bar on "outrageously outweighs."** That's a strong claim. It stays, but as her belief ("I believe," "for me"), not as a measured fact. That keeps it honest and still lets her say it with her chest.

**Not a doomsayer, and the script proves it.** She says she isn't one, so the script has to leave room for what technology does give. One short beat: she works in tech, she teaches tech, it has given her a lot. That makes the "it takes more" line land as considered, not as a rant.

**The dating story trailed off.** "I'm so blown away at how..." never finished. The final leaves a clearly marked line for her to say what blew her away, with a suggested version that fits her next point (how much the algorithm decided before she saw a single face). Don't record the suggestion if it isn't true to what happened.

**"Five, seven years."** Her dictation said "57 years," which is a slip. The final says "years" so she can say the real number on camera.

**The dating statistic, checked.** A Stanford study (Rosenfeld, Thomas and Hausen, published 2019) found that by 2017 about 39% of heterosexual couples in the US had met online, more than any other way. So "in the Western world, you'd probably meet them on an app" is close but too broad. The final says "in the US, more couples now meet online than any other way," which is what the study supports.

**The algorithm list.** Her list (what you eat, what you wear, who you meet, how you treat your husband...) is spoken anaphora and stays in the script, because that's how she talks. Captions turn it into full sentences.

**The tokens line is the best line in the piece.** Funny, specific, and it makes the dependence point without a lecture. It gets its own section and its own clip.

**"Emotionally, cognitively, financially."** One plain example for each so a stranger isn't just hearing three words: emotionally (asking an app before asking a friend), cognitively (not writing a paragraph without help), financially (how much of what we buy we found through a feed).

**The Twitter question.** Can't find the original author, so she credits it the way she remembered it ("I saw this somewhere, I think on Twitter"). It becomes the close, because it's the one practical thing a viewer can do today.

**[RETENTION BEAT, around 1:00: And it's not only love. Look at how many other decisions it's quietly making for us.]**

**[SECTION 3: ALGORITHMS DECIDE SO MUCH]**

Your mentality.

Financially, when so much of what we buy, we found through a feed.

I'm not saying any one of these is wrong on its own.
