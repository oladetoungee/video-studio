# KIT 05 — Things That Should Be Crimes · EP 01: Fake School Fees

**Premise:** Series opener. Some things aren't legally crimes but should be. Episode one: the child of struggling parents who invents fake school fees. Twist — it *is* already a crime (obtaining by false pretences), just the only one with a 0% prosecution rate, because the victims are the two people who'd never report you.

**Format:** Episode one of a series. Ends with a call for nominations → comments become the episode pipeline.

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

I think that there are some things that are not explicitly crimes. They're not crimes, but I genuinely think they qualify to be crimes. There should be like some reviews where things happen and it should actually qualify to be a criminal activity.

Because why are you a child of struggling parents that were sent to university by your struggling parents — which is not a cheap thing to do if you're in a place like Nigeria, it is not easy to send a child to university — but a lot of times, your struggling parents who are still struggling to meet up with your fees, your dormitory rent, your dues, and your genuine school financial obligations, your feeding, they are still struggling to meet up with that. And then you, the child of struggling parents, decides that you need more things. Of course, you need clothes and cool stuff and all of those things. So you take it upon yourself to create imaginary fees and bills that do not exist for your struggling parent. You build more struggles onto the original struggles, the original expenses of you being in school — you'll choose out of your own will to add to that. How is that not even a crime?

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**The legal technicality (sharper than the premise).** What she's describing is *already* a crime on paper — obtaining money by false pretences, fraud in basically every legal system including Nigeria's. It's literally the same **419** (Section 419, Nigerian Criminal Code — that's where the slang comes from). So the real observation isn't "this should be a crime," it's: it IS a crime with a **0% prosecution rate**, because the victims are the only two people on earth who'd never report you. The perfect crime — perfection isn't in the execution, it's in the choice of victim. Their love for you is the security flaw.

**The economics (where the comedy dies).** A struggling parent has no "miscellaneous" budget line. Every fake ₦15,000 levy doesn't come from savings, it comes from somewhere in their *body* — mother re-tying the same two wrappers another year, father postponing the hospital check, meat leaving the pot at home while you buy shawarma on campus. Money was converted sacrifice. The offense isn't the amount, it's the source: you taxed people already taxing themselves for you.

**Why students do it (keeps it honest, not just a dragging).** Campus runs on a *shame economy*. Nobody wants to be the visibly poor one. Most of the time it isn't greed — it's shame outsourcing its bill to the people least able to pay it. Hiding the family's struggle at school by increasing the family's struggle at home. The math never made sense.

**Faith layer.** Proverbs 28:24 — whoever robs father or mother and says "it is not a sin" is partner to a destroyer. The self-justification ("it's still family money") is quoted *inside* the verse. Bible saw you coming.

**Two candidate endings.**
- *Restitution:* you're grown and earning now, they still don't know about the "lab coat maintenance levy of 2014" — pay the fraud back in kindness, with interest.
- *"They knew" (heavier, likely truer):* poverty makes people excellent accountants. The fee sounded strange, the amounts kept coming, they suspected — and paid anyway, because what if this one is real and you suffer for their doubt. They chose to be defrauded rather than risk failing you. The crime was **pardoned in advance**.
- **Decision:** stack both. Restitution, then the gut-punch.

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

I think there are some things that are not explicitly crimes. They're not crimes, nobody will arrest you for them, but I genuinely think they qualify. There should be some kind of review board where certain behaviours are examined and upgraded to criminal activity. So I'm starting a list. Things that should be crimes. And episode one is for my Nigerian students, past and present, because you people know what you did.

Why are you, a child of struggling parents, parents that are sending you to university by force by fire, and if you know Nigeria you know that is not a cheap thing, it is not easy to send a child to university, why are you, that same child, sitting down in your dormitory inventing fees?

Because that's what it is. Your parents are already struggling to meet up with the real obligations. The actual school fees. The dormitory rent. The dues that genuinely exist. Your feeding. They are stretching themselves to cover the legitimate cost of you being in that school. And then you, the beneficiary of all this stretching, you decide that you need more things. Clothes, cool stuff, enjoyment, belonging. Which, fine, you're young, you want things, that part is human. But instead of wanting them honestly, you take it upon yourself to create imaginary bills. Faculty handbook fee. Departmental levy. Practical manual money. Course rep said we're contributing for the lecturer's birthday. You are manufacturing expenses that do not exist and submitting them to two people who cannot afford the ones that do exist. You are adding struggle on top of the original struggle, voluntarily, with your full chest. How is that not a crime?

And here's the part that actually made me laugh when I thought about it properly. It IS a crime. Technically, legally, what I just described is obtaining money by false pretences. That is fraud. That is literally the same 419 we shout about in this country. Inventing a fee that doesn't exist and collecting money for it is the textbook definition. So this behaviour is not missing from the law books. It's sitting right there. It is just the only crime in the world with a zero percent prosecution rate, because the victims are the only two people on this earth who would never report you. Think about that. The scheme doesn't work because you're a criminal mastermind. The scheme works because their love for you is the security flaw. You found the perfect crime, and the perfection is not in your execution, it's in your choice of victim.

Now let me spoil the joke, because the joke deserves to be spoiled. That money you collected was never just money. A struggling parent does not have a miscellaneous section in their budget. So your fake 15,000 naira levy did not come from savings, it came from somewhere in their body. It's your mother re-tying the same two wrappers for another year. It's your father postponing that hospital check he has been mentioning. It's meat quietly leaving the pot at home while you're buying shawarma on campus with the proceeds of your fraud. The money was converted sacrifice. That's why your conscience is doing you somehow right now if you ever did this. The offense was never the amount. The offense is the source. You taxed people who were already taxing themselves for you.

I know why it happens though, and I'll be honest about it because I want this to be true, not just a dragging session. Campus runs on a shame economy. Nobody wants to be the visibly poor one. The pressure to have the clothes, to contribute when the group is contributing, to belong, it's real, and let's be honest, teenagers are not famous for withstanding social pressure. So most of the time it wasn't even greed. It was shame, outsourcing its bill to the people least able to pay it. Trying to hide the family's struggle at school by increasing the family's struggle at home. When you say it like that you can see the mathematics doesn't even make sense. It never made sense.

And in case anybody wants to do the classic self-justification, it's not really stealing, it's my parents, it's still family money, the Bible saw you coming from thousands of years away. Proverbs 28 says whoever robs their father or their mother and says "it is not a sin" is partner to a destroyer. Partner to a destroyer o. The excuse itself is quoted in the verse. There is nothing new under the sun, including your faculty handbook fee.

So where does this leave us. Two places, and I'll give you both. If you're grown now, earning now, and your parents still don't know about the lab coat maintenance levy of 2014, the case file is still open in heaven's records, but the restitution process is simple, it's called taking care of them now, extravagantly, with interest. Pay back the fraud in kindness.

But before you go, let me tell you the part that will actually touch you. Some of those parents knew. Poverty makes people excellent accountants. The fee sounded strange, the amounts kept coming, they looked at the numbers and something didn't add up, and they paid anyway. Because what if this one is real? What if they doubt you and you suffer at school because of their doubt? They would rather be defrauded than risk failing you. Which means for some of you, the crime was never undetected. It was pardoned in advance. Before you even collected the money, you had already been forgiven. Now go and sit with what you want to do with that.

Episode one, closed. The list continues. If you have a nomination for something that should be a crime, drop it, because I know you people are holding evidence.
