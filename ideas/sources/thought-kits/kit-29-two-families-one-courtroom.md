# KIT 29: Two Families in One Courtroom

**Premise:** Caleb Flynn, a former American Idol contestant and music pastor in Tipp City, Ohio, was found guilty of killing his wife Ashley so he could be with the woman he was having an affair with. In a courtroom video, it was just his father and mother standing right behind him, while Ashley's family sat in the same room. Two sets of parents, one afternoon. Parenting has no exit. You can resign from a job and end a marriage, but you can't stop being someone's mother or father, whatever your child does.

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**Confirming the case.** Caleb Flynn, a former American Idol contestant and music pastor from Tipp City, Ohio. Prosecutors said he shot Ashley twice in the back of the head while she was in bed so he could be with his mistress, and staged it to look like an intruder came in through the garage. In the prosecution's telling he'd keep his mistress, his daughters, his job at Ashley's family business, and his position as a worship leader. Ashley was 37, a seventh-grade volleyball coach at Tippecanoe Middle School and a substitute teacher, mother of two elementary-aged daughters. The jury deliberated about two hours and found him guilty on all nine counts on September 29, 2026, including aggravated murder. Sentencing is October 5; he could face life without parole. His lawyer says they'll appeal.

**Correction 1: the King David detail.** It didn't go the way she remembered. One writer who followed the trial (Substack, not a news outlet) says the texts show Flynn claiming God told him to "do it just like King David," and the mistress took that to mean divorce and marry her. So it wasn't the mistress saying he planned to kill his wife "the David way." Not confirmed in mainstream reporting, so the final uses David only as her own association ("the story of David came to my mind"), which is true and needs no source.

**Correction 2: the room wasn't empty.** Ashley's dad, mom, sister and brother-in-law were in the courtroom for the verdict. His parents (reported as Mike and Kim Flynn) were the ones behind him. After the verdict they told a local station they still believe he's innocent, and they declined to comment outside a hearing two days later.

**That correction makes her thought stronger.** The real picture is two sets of parents in one room. One mother and father who lost a daughter. Another mother and father standing behind the son convicted of taking her. Parenthood in both its hardest shapes, the same afternoon.

**Ashley comes first.** A piece about how heavy parenting is could slide into making his parents the sad heroes. Ashley's parents carry the heaviest version. Order in the final: Ashley, her family, her girls, then the parents behind him.

**Her actual thought: parenting has no exit.** You can resign from a job. You can end a marriage, which is the bitter part here, because divorce was right there and he didn't take it. His parents don't get that option.

**Standing there isn't the same as approving.** His parents say he's innocent, so the piece doesn't guess what they believe. Her point holds either way: whether a parent agrees with what their child did or not, they're often the last two people standing in the second row when the consequences arrive.

**The David story that fits better.** Not David and Bathsheba, but David and Absalom. Absalom had his brother Amnon killed and later tried to take his father's throne. When he died, David wept: "O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! would God I had died for thee" (2 Samuel 18:33, KJV). That's what scripture sounds like when it's the parent of someone who did something terrible. You can grieve your child and still know what he did was wrong.

**Care on making this content.** Public case, fine to name. But sentencing is days away and an appeal is coming. Post after October 5, lead with the observation not the crime, keep the mistress and the texts out, mention the girls gently, and don't use Ashley's photos or courtroom images of real people in the thumbnail.

It's such a ridiculously sad story.

What on earth is wrong with you, that you would kill someone you had committed your life to, instead of just divorcing them, man?

But then I saw a video of him in court.

That's what it sounds like to be the parent of someone who did something terrible.

You know what they did was wrong, and you still grieve them as your child.

Both at the same time.

**[CLOSE]**

I keep thinking about Ashley's parents.

I keep thinking about her two little girls.

And I keep thinking about the two people standing behind him.

It's just so sad, and so heavy.
