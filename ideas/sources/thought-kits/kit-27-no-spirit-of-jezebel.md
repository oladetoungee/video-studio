# KIT 27: There Is No Spirit of Jezebel

**Premise:** "The spirit of Jezebel" is a label churches throw at women who are strong-willed or "rough on the edges." It isn't in scripture, it's applied almost only to women, it shuts down the conversation, and the Bible actually honours insistent women. Name the actual thing instead.

**Register:** her dictated version is the base (minimum effective edit). Faith-centered, firm but not combative ("I'm not trying to start an argument").

---

If you are a woman and you've been in the church in church communities long enough, you probably have heard of something called the spirit of Jezebel. Now, just for context, Jezebel was a queen, not an Israelite, that doesn't like the Israelites and doesn't like their gods and she does what she can do in her power to oppose them.

And now you wonder why people refer to a certain spirit of this character, this person. Actually in the Bible there isn't a spirit that is affiliated with Jezebel per se. I think it's just something that we've come to use more and more over the years and it has just become a thing to have the spirit of Jezebel.

First of all, with all the sincerity in my heart, I'm not trying to start an argument. There is nothing like the spirit of Jezebel. Several spirits were talked about in the Bible and none of them were accorded to Jezebel. Technically it is not spiritual. Secondly, in many cases, it is some language that has been formulated against women, a classic case of misogyny.

Because if you think about it, there are many people in the scriptures that positioned the same way Jezebel positioned, that opposed the people of Israel. I can't think of an example here, but please write it in. But you never hear spirit of XYZ or spirit of ABC. Jezebel was pretty much like those people. But I think what is unique in her case is that she's a woman and so it is convenient to coin a spirit. So when you're a woman that is a little bit rough on the edges, you usually will be tagged that you have a spirit of Jezebel.

So case number one, it is biblically incorrect. Case number two, it is a classic case of misogyny. You can make more cases. I'm going to leave that to you.

But my point is that it is so unhelpful to tag someone and say that they have the spirit of Jezebel. It is unfair and it is not sincere, on both parties. Usually, at least in the cases I've had people use it, I have even had it used specifically for someone. I think in this case it's just the person being difficult in the matter, which is very normal. Everyone can be very resistant to their own point of view and opinion, but as Christians we are called to engage with each other.

It is insincere to just pull it out of thin air and say someone has the spirit of Jezebel, and also it is unhelpful because it is so ambiguous. If you had the spirit of... what are the spirits that were named in the Bible? I can't remember but you can note for me. Say the spirit of maybe uncontentment or something. It is specific. You can say perhaps this spirit is disturbing this person, and they have to do this, they have to learn to be more contented. But in the case of someone being accused of having the spirit of Jezebel, it is insincere and unhelpful because what exactly is the spirit of Jezebel? In many cases that I've seen it used, it's usually used for a woman that is insistent on her own opinion. I'm not saying the women are angels and they have no side to them as well, but I find it's very unhelpful to have people being referred to as having the spirit of Jezebel.

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**Case one holds.** "Spirit of Jezebel" appears nowhere in scripture. The idea grew mostly in charismatic/deliverance teaching over recent decades.

**The Revelation objection must be answered inside the piece.** Revelation 2:20 names "that woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess" in Thyatira. It helps her argument: the charge is specific (false teaching, sexual immorality, food sacrificed to idols), it's a name for one false teacher in one church, and it still never says "spirit of Jezebel."

**Case two gets stronger with the men lined up.** Pharaoh, Haman, Jeroboam, Herod, and Ahab, whom scripture describes as unmatched in evil. None of them became a casual church label for a stubborn man. Where an "Ahab spirit" is taught, it usually means a man too weak to control a woman, so the blame still returns to her.

**Named spirits (her request).** "Uncontentment" isn't one. Real ones: spirit of fear (2 Tim 1:7), heaviness (Isa 61:3), infirmity (Luke 13:11), jealousy (Num 5:14), and a lying spirit (1 Kings 22:22), which sits inside Ahab's own story. They're descriptive and specific, which is exactly her point.

**Case three (added): it ends the conversation.** Once tagged, disagreement and self-defence both become proof. It turns a disagreement into a diagnosis and can't be answered.

**Case four (added): scripture honours insistent women.** Abigail (1 Sam 25), the persistent widow (Luke 18), the Syrophoenician woman who argued with Jesus and was commended (Mark 7 / Matt 15). She was from the region of Tyre and Sidon, Jezebel's homeland (1 Kings 16:31).

**Honesty guard (already hers).** "I'm not saying women are angels." Keep it. The piece isn't saying women can't be difficult; it's saying name the actual thing.

**Landing (added, short).** If she's difficult, say so. If she's wrong, show her. Speak the truth in love (Eph 4:15). Don't reach for a label nobody can define or answer.

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

If you are a woman and you've been in the church, in church communities, long enough, you've probably heard of something called the spirit of Jezebel.

Now, just for context. Jezebel was a queen. She wasn't an Israelite by birth. She was a Phoenician princess who married Ahab, the king of Israel. She didn't like the God of Israel or his prophets, and she did everything she could, with all the power she had, to oppose them.

And now you wonder why people refer to a certain spirit of this character, this person. Actually, in the Bible, there isn't a spirit that is affiliated with Jezebel per se. I think it's just something we've come to use more and more over the years, and it has just become a thing, to have the spirit of Jezebel.

First of all, with all the sincerity in my heart, I'm not trying to start an argument. But there is nothing like the spirit of Jezebel. Several spirits were talked about in the Bible, and none of them were accorded to Jezebel. Technically, it is not scriptural.

And I know someone will bring Revelation 2 at me. That's where Jesus talks to the church in Thyatira, and he says they're tolerating "that woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess." But look at it closely. Even there, it's specific. She was teaching people falsely and leading them into sexual immorality and into eating food sacrificed to idols. Those are named sins. You can point at them. It's not "she's insistent on her opinion." It's not "she's a bit rough on the edges." And it still doesn't say the spirit of Jezebel. It's a name given to one false teacher in one church.

Secondly, in many cases, it is language that has been formulated against women. It is a classic case of misogyny.

Because if you think about it, there are many people in the scriptures who positioned themselves the same way Jezebel did, who opposed the people of Israel. Think about Pharaoh, who enslaved the Israelites and ordered their baby boys thrown into the river. Think about Haman, who plotted to wipe out every Jew in the kingdom. Think about Jeroboam, who set up golden calves and led Israel into idol worship for generations. Think about Herod, who had the baby boys of Bethlehem killed. And think about Ahab, Jezebel's own husband, the man who sat on the throne beside her. The Bible says there was nobody like Ahab, who sold himself to do evil.

But you never hear the spirit of Pharaoh. You never hear the spirit of Haman thrown at a difficult man in a church meeting. Jezebel was pretty much like those people. And where you do hear people talk about an Ahab spirit, it's usually describing a man who is too weak to control a woman. So even there, somehow, the problem goes back to the woman.

What is unique in her case is that she's a woman. And so it is convenient to coin a spirit. So when you're a woman who is a little bit rough on the edges, you'll usually be tagged. You have the spirit of Jezebel.

So, case number one, it is biblically incorrect. Case number two, it is a classic case of misogyny.

Case number three, it ends the conversation. Once someone says you have the spirit of Jezebel, anything you say after that becomes proof. If you disagree, that's the spirit. If you defend yourself, that's the spirit. There is no way to argue your way out of it. It takes a disagreement and turns it into a diagnosis.

And case number four, the Bible actually honours insistent women. Abigail went against her own husband, Nabal, and rode out to stop David, and David blessed her for it. The persistent widow kept going back to the judge until he gave her justice, and Jesus used her as the example of how we should pray. And the Syrophoenician woman argued with Jesus himself. She would not take no for an answer, and Jesus commended her. And here's the part that gets me. She came from the region of Tyre and Sidon. That's Jezebel's homeland. A woman from Jezebel's own country stood her ground in front of Jesus, and he praised her for it.

But my point with all of this is that it is so unhelpful to tag someone and say they have the spirit of Jezebel. It is unfair, and it is not sincere. It is not sincere on both parties. At least in the cases where I've seen people use it, and I've even had it used around me, specifically about someone, I think it was just the person being difficult in the matter. Which is very normal. Everyone can be very resistant, holding on to their own point of view and opinion. But as Christians, we are called to engage with each other.

It is insincere to just pull it out of thin air and say someone has the spirit of Jezebel. And it is also unhelpful, because it is so ambiguous. It can be used however anybody likes.

Think about the spirits that are actually named in the Bible. The spirit of fear, in 2 Timothy. The spirit of heaviness, in Isaiah. A spirit of infirmity, in Luke. A spirit of jealousy, in Numbers. And a lying spirit, and this one is almost funny, because it shows up in 1 Kings 22, in Ahab's own story. So in the very chapters where Jezebel lived, the Bible names a spirit, and it's a lying spirit. Not a Jezebel spirit.

And notice that they're specific. If you say perhaps a spirit of fear is troubling this person, you know what you're dealing with. They have to learn to trust God. They have to do this, they have to do that. There's somewhere to go.

But in the case of someone being accused of having the spirit of Jezebel, it is insincere and unhelpful, because what exactly is the spirit of Jezebel? In many cases where I've seen it used, it's usually for a woman who is insistent on her own opinion. I'm not saying women are angels, that they have no fault and no side to them as well. But I find it's very unhelpful to have people referred to as having the spirit of Jezebel.

If she's being difficult, say she's being difficult. If she's wrong, show her where she's wrong. Name the actual thing. Sit with her like a sister and speak the truth in love. That's what we're called to do. Not reach for a label that nobody can define and nobody can answer.

- **Speak the truth in love** — Ephesians 4:15.
- **Origin of the teaching** — popularised largely in charismatic/deliverance circles in recent decades; kept general in the script rather than naming specific authors.
