# KIT 19 — A Pet With a Human Face

**Premise:** The (mostly unadmitted) craving for a permanent higher-power position over a partner isn't sustainable — not because hierarchy is evil, but because a human being grows, and you'd have to actively suppress that growth forever to hold the gap. That's not love, it's the textbook definition of abuse. Chosen submission still proves the will is intact. We're too strong-willed — and that's the design.

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

A lot of people I know, men especially, would crave this kind of relationship with a power dynamic where they don't have equal status in the relationship. This is not to say that craving is bad or good — all the best to you and your creator if you crave this. But a lot of people don't admit it, and when they talk, there's this need to be in a relationship where you have a higher power dynamic than the other person. It's there. People don't admit it, but it is there.

The problem I have with it is that it's not really sustainable. You either have to be an extremely wicked tyrant to make it perfect, or you have to go get an animal — get a dog or a cat. Now that is an equal power dynamic situation. But the problem with wanting that with another human being is that we are too strong-willed for that. People have tried, willingly, to assume this position of being subservient or acknowledging that the other person is better or more powerful than they are, but I always think it's just pretense. Genuinely, we are not cut out for that kind of shit.

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**The honest naming (she's right the craving is real and unadmitted).** A genuine desire — not only in men — for a clear hierarchy where one leads and one follows and nothing's negotiated. Dressed up as "someone has to be the head," complementarity, "I just want peace in my home." Her instinct not to moralize up front is strategically right: open with "this is evil" and everyone who feels it stops listening. "Okay, that desire exists — can it actually work?" disarms them before the cut.

**Core argument + mechanism.** "Not sustainable" is a claim; the mechanism makes it land. A human is not a fixed object — they grow, learn, gain confidence, money, friends, their own thoughts. So even if you start with a gap, the person keeps developing and keeps pressing against the ceiling. You can't set the hierarchy once and relax — you must actively hold them down, continuously, forever, against their nature. Not a state you achieve, a war you keep fighting — and it breaks, because you're fighting the tide.

**Why "wicked tyrant" is an argument, not a jab.** The only way to stop a human growing into their power is active suppression: isolate (no outside input), control the money (no independence), undermine confidence (so they don't believe they could leave), keep them small on purpose. That's the textbook definition of abuse. So the uncomfortable landing: a permanent, stable power imbalance between two adults is unachievable through love — only through control, because the imbalance isn't natural and must be enforced. The tyranny isn't a bug; it IS the system.

**The dog line, sharpened not cut.** A pet can hold that position sustainably because it has no competing will that grows. Not about pets being lovable — about no autonomous will straining upward. Sharp version: if you want a creature permanently beneath you, you don't want a partner, you want **a pet with a human face**. A human can pretend to be that pet for a while, but the real person is always still in there.

**"It's just pretense," refined so it doesn't insult genuine choosers.** Real submission is chosen; anything chosen can be un-chosen. So even the willing submitter submits *by their own will* — their will is still in charge. A choice made daily is a form of power, not its absence. This dismantles the fantasy: you can never get a will-less partner from someone who *chose* to submit, because the choosing proves the will is intact. The only way to get truly no will is to break it → back to the tyrant. No exit: either submission is chosen (will still there, fantasy fails) or the will is gone (not a relationship, a slow crime).

**Honesty guard (prevents overreach).** A permanent power imbalance ≠ a division of labour. Two equals can say "you lead here, I lead there" — that's teamwork: chosen, flexing, renegotiable. Same with consensual bedroom dynamics — a game two equals agree to play and can stop. She's not against roles, leadership, or preferring to follow — only the fantasy of fixed, total, permanent superiority the other can never grow out of. Naming this stops the "so nobody can lead in a marriage?" derail: lead all you want, by agreement, today — they can renegotiate tomorrow, because they're a person, and that renegotiability is the exact thing the fantasy can't tolerate and can't remove without tyranny.

**Faith turn (cuts against how the craving is usually justified).** People quote "wives submit" (Ephesians 5) and stop. The passage turns immediately to command the "head" to love sacrificially, give himself up entirely, as Christ did — i.e. died first. Biblical leadership = serves most, dies first, not rules hardest. The text guts the tyrant fantasy from inside the very scripture used to justify it. Takes the weapon out of the hand of anyone who'd quote scripture at her.

**The landing.** It fails not because hierarchy is morally wrong but because a human won't hold still for it. We're too strong-willed — not a flaw, the design. The thing that makes someone worth being with (a real, growing, willed person) is the same thing that makes them impossible to keep permanently beneath you. So choose: a real person (an equal, negotiating forever) or total control (destroy the person to get it, then you're alone with something you broke). No third door.

**Decisions:** faith IN; keep all her bites ("get a dog," "we are not cut out for that kind of shit"); preserve her original distinctness.

---

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

A lot of people I know, men especially, crave this kind of relationship. The kind with a power dynamic where they don't have equal status with the other person. Where they're up here and the partner is down there. And let me say from the start, this is not me telling you that craving is good or bad. If that's what you want, all the best to you and your creator. Genuinely.

And most people won't admit to it out loud. But when they talk, when they really talk, it's there. This need to be in a relationship where they hold the higher power. It leaks out in how they describe what they want. It's there. People don't say it plainly, but it is absolutely there.

My problem with it is not moral. My problem with it is simpler than that. It's just not sustainable. It cannot last. And I want to explain why, because I've thought about this a lot.

The reason it can't last is that a human being is not a fixed object. A person grows. They learn things. They gain confidence. They get their own money, their own friends, their own thoughts in their own head. They change. So even if you manage to start the relationship with a nice big gap between you, the person underneath you does not stay the same size. They keep developing. And a developing person keeps pressing up against whatever ceiling you built over them.

Which means you cannot just set the hierarchy once and then relax into it. You have to actively hold the other person down. Continuously. Forever. Against their own natural growth. It's not a position you arrive at and enjoy. It's a war you have to keep fighting every single day, against the basic nature of the person you supposedly love. And that breaks. It has to break, because you're fighting the tide.

And this is exactly why I say you'd have to be an extremely wicked tyrant to make it work. That's not me being dramatic, follow the logic. If the only way to stop a human from growing into their own power is to suppress them, then what do you actually have to do? You have to isolate them, so they don't get outside input. You have to control the money, so they can't build any independence. You have to chip at their confidence, so they never quite believe they could leave. You have to keep them small on purpose.

And that's not a strict-but-loving home. That is the textbook definition of abuse. So here's the uncomfortable place this argument arrives at. A permanent, stable power imbalance between two grown adults is not something you can achieve through love. It's only something you can achieve through control. Because the imbalance is not natural, so it has to be forced. The tyranny is not a side effect of the system. The tyranny is the system. It's the only thing holding it up.

So this is where I say, if that's really what you want, go and get a dog. Get a cat. I'm not even being rude. Get a pet.

Because a pet can actually hold that position for you, sustainably, for life. And you know why? Not because pets are sweet. Because a dog does not have a competing will that grows over time. A dog is not secretly developing, year by year, into your equal. It's not going to wake up one day with its own ambitions and its own money and its own idea of how things should go. So the reason the dynamic works with a pet and does not work with a person is not about love at all. It's that a pet has no autonomous will straining upward. And here's the sharp version of that. If what you truly want is a creature that stays permanently beneath you, calm and content down there forever, then you don't actually want a partner. You want a pet with a human face.

And a human being can pretend to be that pet. For a while. But there's a real person in there. And the real person is always, always still in there.

Which brings me to the thing I used to say, that when people willingly take the lower position, it's mostly pretense. Now let me be fair and sharpen that, because some people genuinely do choose it, and I don't want to call all of them liars. So here's the precise version. Submission that is real is submission that is chosen. And anything that can be chosen can be un-chosen. So even the person who truly, willingly submits is submitting by their own will. Which means their will is still the thing running the show. They are choosing, every single day, to defer to you. And a choice you make every day is not the absence of power. That is power.

You see what that does to the whole fantasy? The person who wants a partner with no will of their own can never actually get it from someone who chose to submit, because the choosing is the proof that the will is right there, fully intact, in charge. The only way to get a human with truly no will left is to break their will. And now we are right back at the tyrant again. So there is no way out. Either the submission is chosen, and then the will is still there and the fantasy fails. Or the will is genuinely gone, and then you didn't build a relationship, you committed a slow crime.

Now let me be careful here, because I'm not against everything that looks like this. There's a difference between a permanent power imbalance and just dividing the labour. Two equal people can absolutely say, you handle this, I'll handle that, you take the lead in this area, I'll take the lead in that one. That's not hierarchy. That's teamwork. It's chosen, it flexes, it can be renegotiated. Same with whatever two consenting equals agree to do in their own bedroom, that's a game two people agree to play and can stop playing whenever. That's the opposite of a fixed, permanent gap. So I'm not talking about roles, or leadership, or even someone who simply prefers to follow. I'm talking about the fantasy of a total, permanent superiority that the other person is never allowed to grow out of. That specific thing. That's the thing that can't be done without cruelty.

And I'll say one thing to the people who like to reach for the Bible to justify wanting to be the head. Because they always quote the "wives submit" part and stop reading right there. But keep reading. The same passage turns straight around and tells the head to love sacrificially. To give himself up completely. The way Christ did, which, let me remind you, ended with him dying for the people he was leading. So even in the scripture people use to grab for power, the actual model of the one in charge is the one who serves the most and dies first. Not the one who rules the hardest. The text itself guts the tyrant fantasy. You just have to read past the one line you liked.

So here's where I land. The reason this whole thing fails is not that hierarchy is morally evil. It's that a human being will not hold still for it. We are too strong-willed. And that is not a defect. That is the design. The very thing that makes a person worth being with, that they are real, that they are growing, that they have a will of their own, is the exact same thing that makes them impossible to keep permanently underneath you.

So you actually have to choose. You can have a real person, which means an equal, which means negotiating with them for the rest of your life. Or you can have total control, which means you have to damage the person to get it, and then you're just alone in a house with something you broke. There is no third door. There is no version where you get a whole, complete human being who happily stays smaller than you forever, by choice. Because the choice is the will. And the will is the exact thing you were trying to get rid of.

We are just not cut out for that kind of shit. And thank God for that.
