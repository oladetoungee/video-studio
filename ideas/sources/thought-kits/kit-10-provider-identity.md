# KIT 10 — You Were Just the Equipment

**Premise:** When someone has to keep announcing that they're the provider, the protector, the providing isn't the end — it's a means. The real goal is identity, the feeling, the social value. The person being "provided for" just happens to be standing inside the circle of what that identity requires.

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

I find it weird when people say that they are providers, or "as a provider," "as a protector," because I've seen people hold on to this kind of thing. I used to know this couple, they were neighbours, and whenever they had a disagreement, one of the things the husband would say is how much work he's doing to provide for this woman, to feed her, to do this and do that. And it's correct, because this woman doesn't make money — even though I don't think that position is a fair position to her — she doesn't work to make money, and well, she doesn't starve either and she's not homeless, even though I think she wouldn't be homeless and starving if this person wasn't in the picture either. But yeah, it's correct in that way. But then you would do really counterproductive stuff — you claim you are working hard to provide for them, and then you beat them. And I'm like, what the fuck? Isn't that counterproductive?

This is why I genuinely think that if someone has to go on and on about being a provider, bro, it is just control. And maybe control is a harsh word. I think in many cases the goal is not doing that stuff for the other person. You would think that providing for that person is the end, but when I observe these situations, at least the little I have seen, providing for the other person is usually not the end — it is usually a means to another end. There's always another goal in mind, and when I observe, the goal is usually identity validation. I'm a provider. You want to feel that feeling. That is usually the goal.

I saw this video, a Christian man, and the conversation was about danger and sexual abuse and rape of women, and he said basically — I'm paraphrasing — that women need to be with good men, and if you refuse to be with a good man then you put yourself in danger.

So at the end of the day, I think when people hold on to this "I'm a this to you" — and it doesn't even have to be gender related, it could be some other thing — when they hold on to that idea so much, the providing or caring or whatever it is is not the goal for them. You just happen to be the one in the circle of them trying to achieve their main goal, which is the feeling and the satisfaction and the social value that is obtained from being that thing that they claim to be.

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**Her method is the essay.** She argued from contradiction, not feeling: he claims the goal is providing, but he also beats her, and both cannot serve the same goal. When actions contradict the stated aim, the stated aim isn't the aim. That test yields the thesis honestly — provision was the means; the end was identity.

**Ambivalent Sexism Theory (Glick & Fiske).** Sexism splits into *hostile* (contempt, aggression) and *benevolent* (protective paternalism, chivalry, "I cherish and provide for her"). The 1996 paper literally writes the self-justifying sentence: *"I am not exploiting women; I love, protect, and provide for them"* — the neighbour's argument, published thirty years ago as the textbook illustration.

**Why it isn't actually counterproductive.** Across 19 nations, ~15,000 people, hostile and benevolent sexism are positively correlated (~.40–.50). Not two men — one man, one system. Provision and punishment are both expressions of ownership. It only looks contradictory if you assume her wellbeing was the goal; if the goal is authority, both behaviours are productive. Yours to feed, yours to discipline.

**The video guy has a name too — Iris Marion Young, "The Logic of Masculinist Protection" (Signs, 2003).** The protector role elevates the protector to superior authority and demotes the protected into *grateful dependency*; in exchange for protection the woman concedes critical distance from decision-making autonomy. Young calls the arrangement a **protection racket**. And the structure of his claim: protection from men, sold by a man — protection from his own category. It also relocates responsibility for male violence off perpetrators and onto women for failing to get properly assigned to an owner.

**The darkest implication (from her own logic).** If the identity is the goal, the provider has a *structural interest in your continued need*. Your independence threatens his self-concept — hence discouragement, reasons the job/business won't work, reasons to stay home. The dependency isn't an accident of the relationship, it's the infrastructure.

**The load-bearing lie.** "You'd be nothing without me" must stay inflated because the value of the ledger depends on it. She punctured it in passing: the woman wouldn't be homeless and starving without him.

**The ledger / itemisation tell.** Real giving keeps no receipts. A line-by-line account produced mid-argument as a weapon means it was never a gift — it was an invoice with long payment terms, collecting compliance, deference, silence.

**De-gendering (her explicit instinct, and what makes it bigger).** Parent who reminds you about school fees; boss who "puts food on your table"; the friend who's always helping and needs you to know; the church person whose whole personality is service; the martyr sibling. Good deed → debt → leverage. Diagnostic: what happens when you no longer need them? Glad you outgrew them = giving. Cold, or new ways to make you need them = buying.

**Three tests for the audience.** Does the provision survive (a) no audience, (b) your independence, (c) your disagreement?

**Faith thread — Matthew 6:1–4.** Do good to be seen and "you have received your reward in full" — the applause *was* the payment, transaction closed, nothing owed after. And "don't let your left hand know what your right hand is doing" = the original anti-receipt policy. Critiquing from inside the faith lands harder, especially since the video was a Christian man.

**Fairness guard so it doesn't overreach.** Provision is real, often sacrificial labour, and finding meaning in caring for people is normal and healthy. Pride isn't the problem. The line is what happens when the role and the person's wellbeing conflict — if their flourishing threatens your identity and you choose the identity, that's the reveal. Without this, the accusation gets too broad to stick and lets the actual culprits off.

**Decisions made:** faith thread IN; video guy gets one solid takedown paragraph, not the whole essay; couple stays anonymous; violence gets one line, evidence not spectacle.

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

I find it so weird when people announce themselves as providers. As protectors. When somebody has to keep telling you, I am the provider, I am the protector, something is off, and I want to explain what I think it is.

I used to have neighbours, a married couple, and every time they had a disagreement, one of the things the husband would say, loudly, is how hard he is working to provide for this woman. To feed her. To do this for her, to do that for her. And technically, factually, he's correct. She doesn't earn money. I don't think that arrangement is fair to her, by the way, but that's a separate matter. She doesn't work for money, and she is not starving and she is not homeless, so yes, on paper, the man has a point.

But then the same man who is providing for her would turn around and beat her. And I'm just there like, what the fuck? Isn't that counterproductive? You are working so hard to take care of somebody and then you're hurting them? Those two things cannot be serving the same goal.

And that right there is the thing that cracked this whole matter open for me. Because when somebody's actions contradict the goal they claim to have, it means the goal they claim to have is not the real goal. There's another goal. So I started looking for it.

And here is what I've come to think, from the little I've observed. When a person is holding on that tightly to being the provider, the providing is not the end. It is a means. There's another goal behind it. And in most of the cases I've seen, that goal is identity. The feeling. The social value. The satisfaction of being seen as that thing they say they are. The person they claim to be providing for is not really the destination. She just happens to be standing inside the circle of what they need in order to feel like who they want to be.

And I actually looked into this, because I thought maybe I'm just being cynical. I'm not. Psychologists have a whole framework for it. They separate sexism into two types, hostile and benevolent. Hostile is the obvious one, the contempt, the aggression. Benevolent is the sweet-sounding one, the protective, the chivalrous, the "I cherish my woman, I provide for her, I would never let anything happen to her." And in the original paper from 1996, the researchers literally write out the sentence a man uses to justify himself, and it is, I am not exploiting women, I love, protect, and provide for them. That is my neighbour's argument, word for word, written down by researchers thirty years ago as the textbook example.

And here's what killed my "isn't that counterproductive" question. They tested these two types across nineteen countries, about fifteen thousand people, and the two are positively correlated. The men highest on the sweet protective one also tend to be higher on the hostile one. They are not two different men. It's one man running one system. Because provision and punishment are both just expressions of ownership. It only looks contradictory if you assume her wellbeing was the goal. If the goal is authority over her, then both behaviours are working perfectly. She is yours to feed and yours to discipline. Same premise. Nothing counterproductive about it at all.

Now let me talk about that video, because this is what really got me going. I saw a Christian man talking about sexual violence against women, and his message was basically, women need to attach themselves to good men, and if you refuse to do that, you have put yourself in danger.

Sir. Danger from who?

You're offering women protection from men. So what you're actually saying is, buy protection from me, from a threat that is also me, or people from my exact category. That is not safety, that is a protection racket. There's a philosopher, Iris Marion Young, who wrote about this and called it exactly that. She says the logic of the protector elevates the protector to a position of superior authority and puts the protected person into a position of what she called grateful dependency, and that in exchange for that protection, the woman gives up her own decision-making autonomy. That's the invoice. That's the price of the protection. Not money, obedience. And notice what that man's framing also does, very quietly, it takes responsibility for male violence off men who commit it and puts it on women for not having gotten themselves properly assigned to an owner.

And this brings me to the darkest part of this whole thing. If the identity is the actual goal, then your independence is a threat to it. Think about it. If someone needs to be the provider in order to feel like themselves, then you becoming self-sufficient is you taking their identity away from them. Which is why so often you see that the person is not actually happy when the one they're "providing for" starts to stand on their own. Suddenly there's discouragement. Suddenly there are reasons why you shouldn't take that job, why that business won't work, why you should just stay home. The dependency is not an accident of the relationship. It's the foundation of it.

And that's the lie sitting at the centre of the whole thing, that "you would be nothing without me." Because that woman, my neighbour, if that man was not in the picture, I don't believe she would be homeless and starving. I don't. But that claim has to stay inflated, because the value of the ledger depends on it.

The ledger, that's the other tell. Real giving does not keep receipts. When somebody can produce a line-by-line account of everything they've done for you, in the middle of an argument, as a weapon, then it was never a gift. It was an invoice with long payment terms. And what it's collecting is not money, it's compliance. Your agreement. Your silence.

And this thing is not even only about gender, that's just where it's loudest. It's the parent who reminds you they paid your school fees every time you disagree with them. It's the boss who says he puts food on your table. It's the friend who is always the one helping and needs everybody to know it. It's the church person whose entire personality is service. Same structure every time. The good deed becomes a debt, and the debt becomes leverage.

And the Bible calls this thing out so directly, which is why the video annoyed me extra. Matthew 6 says when you do your good deeds to be seen by people, you have already received your reward in full. In full. Meaning the applause was the payment. You wanted to be seen, you got seen, transaction closed, nobody owes you anything after that. And the same chapter says when you give, don't let your left hand know what your right hand is doing. That is the original anti-receipt policy. God Himself said don't itemise.

Let me be fair though, because I don't want to throw everybody inside one basket. Providing is real work. It's often genuinely sacrificial. And it is completely normal and healthy for a human being to find meaning in taking care of the people they love. Pride in that is not the problem. The problem is what happens when the role and the person's actual wellbeing come into conflict. Because that's the moment of truth. If somebody's flourishing threatens your identity, and you choose the identity, you've told on yourself. That's the whole reveal right there.

So here are the three questions I've started asking. Does the provision survive with no audience? Does it survive the person becoming independent? And does it survive the person disagreeing with you?

Because if it only appears when it can be announced, and it disappears the moment you stop complying, then you were never the point. You were just the equipment.

Benevolent sexism = protective paternalism, idealisation of women, subjectively positive attitudes toward women in traditional roles. Script says "positively correlated" with the range, which is the honest version.
- **Protection racket** — Iris Marion Young, "The Logic of Masculinist Protection: Reflections on the Current Security State," *Signs* 29(1), Autumn 2003. Faithfully represented.
