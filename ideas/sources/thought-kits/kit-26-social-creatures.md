# KIT 26 — You Cannot "I Don't Care" Your Way Out of It

**Premise:** There's a reason prolonged isolation is used as punishment, even on the hardest people in the hardest prisons. We are social creatures. We crave each other (validation, love, company) and it's inbuilt. The "I don't need anybody" culture is pretending, and pretending leaves you thirsty and calling it strength.

**Register:** her emphatic, a-little-fierce voice from the opening ("I don't care... you are stuck in it"), warming toward the end. Faith woven in (Genesis 2:18).

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

There's a reason why prolonged isolation is a punishment. You will hear in crazy prisons that if they actually want to punish people, they will isolate them. There's a reason why that is a valid punishment even for the craziest of people. Because we are social creatures, human beings.

We are innately social creatures. We crave ourselves. We crave other people in different forms. Validation, love, all kinds, we crave it. It is inbuilt. You cannot "I don't care" your way out of it. You are stuck in it. It is who you are. It is being human... let's keep writing this piece.

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**Her evidence is the strongest possible kind.** Prisons can do almost anything to a person, and the thing they reach for when they want punishment to really hurt is removing other people. It's codified: the UN Nelson Mandela Rules (Rule 44) define solitary confinement as 22+ hours a day without meaningful human contact, and prolonged solitary as more than 15 consecutive days; Rule 43 prohibits prolonged solitary as cruel treatment. The world looked at people convicted of the worst crimes and still decided two weeks alone was too cruel. The least sentimental institution confirms her point.

**The body agrees.** US Surgeon General's 2023 advisory: lacking social connection can raise premature-death risk as much as smoking *up to* 15 cigarettes a day (the "up to" is a qualified upper bound for mortality risk, not a general equivalence; kept in the script). Murthy described loneliness as like hunger or thirst, a signal the body sends when something needed for survival is missing. This lets her word "crave" carry real weight.

**Where it goes after her opening.**
- Target 1: the "I don't need anybody" culture (cut everybody off, protect your peace, hyper-independence). Understand where it comes from (people got hurt), then show you can't opt out of needing people any more than needing water. You can only pretend, and you end up thirsty and calling it strength.
- Her best dictated line, "you cannot I-don't-care your way out of it," kept almost verbatim.
- Target 2: validation, the need people are most ashamed of. If needing people is hunger, the question was never whether you're hungry but what you feed it with. A starved hunger gets less picky (likes, strangers' comments, a relationship you know is bad for you).

**Guard.** Solitude and isolation are different. Chosen alone time you come back from is healthy. Isolation is being cut off, or cutting yourself off and calling it peace. Without this the piece reads as anti-alone-time.

**Faith.** Genesis: everything created is called good, and the first "not good" is the man being alone (Genesis 2:18). It comes before anything goes wrong in the story, so needing people is the design, not a flaw.

**Landing.** Plain next actions, no mic-drop: text your friend back, go to the thing you were invited to, tell someone you miss them. You're allowed to need people.

---

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

There's a reason why prolonged isolation is used as a punishment.

You'll hear it about prisons, even the craziest prisons in the world. If they actually want to punish somebody, they isolate them. And there's a reason that works as a punishment even on the toughest people in there. It's because we are social creatures. Human beings are innately social creatures.

We crave each other. We crave other people in so many different forms. Validation. Love. Company. All kinds. We crave it, and it is inbuilt. You cannot "I don't care" your way out of it. You are stuck with it. It is who you are. It's part of being human.

And the prison part is not me exaggerating. The United Nations has a set of rules for how prisoners should be treated, called the Nelson Mandela Rules, named after a man who spent years in isolation himself. They say solitary confinement means being locked up for 22 hours or more a day with no meaningful human contact. And they say that once it goes past 15 days in a row, it counts as torture, and they ban it.

Fifteen days. The world looked at people who have done some of the worst things a person can do, and still decided that two weeks without real human contact is too cruel to allow. The punishment considered too much for even the worst offenders is being left alone.

Your body knows this too. In 2023 the US Surgeon General said that lacking social connection can raise your risk of dying early as much as smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day. And he described loneliness like hunger or thirst. It's a signal your body sends when something it needs to survive is missing.

I love that, because nobody is ashamed of being hungry. Nobody says, I'm too strong to need water.

But we say it about people all the time. I don't need anybody. I'm my own best friend. Protect your peace. Cut everybody off. And I understand where it comes from. A lot of people have been hurt. Leaning on people has disappointed them, so they decide to need nobody, because then nobody can let them down again.

But you can't stop needing people any more than you can stop needing water. You can only pretend you don't. And the pretending costs you. You end up thirsty, and you call it strength.

The validation part is the one people are most embarrassed about. "I don't need validation." You do. Everybody does. Wanting to be seen, wanting somebody to say well done, wanting to matter to somebody, that's the hunger. The question was never whether you're hungry. The question is what you're feeding it with. Because when you starve the real hunger, it goes looking for whatever it can find. Likes from strangers. Comments. A relationship you know is bad for you. Hunger doesn't go away when you deny it. It just gets less picky about what it eats.

Now, I love my alone time, so I'm not saying you should never be by yourself. Solitude, time you choose, time you come back from, is good for you. Isolation is something else. Isolation is when you're cut off with no way back. Or when you cut yourself off and call it peace.

In the very beginning, in Genesis, God makes everything and keeps calling it good. The light is good. The land is good. The animals are good. And then the first thing he looks at and says is not good is a man being alone. It is not good for man to be alone. And that was before anything went wrong in the story. So needing people is not something broken in us. God built us that way on purpose.

So text your friend back. Go to the thing you were invited to. Tell somebody you miss them, out loud, it won't kill you. You're human, and humans need people. You're allowed to need them too.

## REFERENCES
- **Nelson Mandela Rules** (UN Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners, 2015 revision). Rule 44: solitary confinement = 22+ hours a day without meaningful human contact; prolonged = more than 15 consecutive days. Rule 43: prolonged solitary confinement prohibited as cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment. The Rules are "soft law" (not legally binding) but widely used by courts and monitors. Named after Mandela, who spent long periods in isolated confinement during his 27 years in prison.
- **US Surgeon General's Advisory, "Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation"** (Vivek Murthy, May 2023). Lacking social connection can increase premature-death risk as much as smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day (drawing on Holt-Lunstad et al.'s 2010 meta-analysis). Murthy compared loneliness to hunger or thirst. **Accuracy guard:** "up to" is a qualified upper bound for mortality risk; script keeps "up to" and frames it as risk of dying early, not an across-the-board equivalence.
- **Genesis 2:18** — "It is not good that the man should be alone." Preceded by the repeated "it was good" in Genesis 1. Accurate.
