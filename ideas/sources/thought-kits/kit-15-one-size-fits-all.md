# KIT 15 — One Size Fits All

**Premise:** A spoken-word / psalm-style piece. "One size fits all" is usually the lie on the label — fits nobody. The gospel is the one place it's true: it fits each person exactly, as they are, and then changes their size. Form matters here as much as content — short lines, the list as a drumbeat.

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

The gospel of Christ is a one size fits all.

Whatever your size is… tired, broken, loved, unloved, shamed, timid, guilty, bruised, beaten, abused, offended, unbroken, overwhelmed, scared,

Come and step into it, it fits all and fills all in all.

*(Follow-up: extend the list, and extend the addressing of the list — "clothes the tired, covers the shamed, lifts the fallen…" — into a fuller call-and-response.)*

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**Different genre from the other kits.** Not an argument or observation — a spoken-word piece, almost a psalm. It already has cadence. The job is to protect the cadence, not bury it in research and references. Lighter machinery, more attention to shape.

**The metaphor has teeth: gospel as garment.** "One size fits all" in the world is usually a lie — it means it fits nobody, baggy on one, tight on another. The flip: this is the one place the phrase is actually true. It fits all, and it fits each person exactly, which no earthly garment can do. That tension is the whole engine; it doesn't need much more.

**Her list is the heart, and it's already right.** Crucially she put "loved" AND "unloved," "broken" AND "unbroken." That's the quiet genius — it's not only for the wounded. The put-together, confident, seemingly self-sufficient person is a size too. If the list were only pain-words it becomes an altar call for the broken and tells the steady person this isn't for them. Keeping "unbroken" in is what makes it genuinely *all*.

**Scriptural spine (light touch, real).** "Fills all in all" is straight from Ephesians 1:23 — Christ "who fills all in all." Two images sit right underneath hers and deepen it without clutter: Matthew 11:28 ("come to me, all you who are weary and heavy laden") = the invitation shape; and the biblical garment language, "put on Christ" / "clothed with Christ" (Romans 13:14, Galatians 3:27) = the step-into-it image is one scripture already gave her.

**The one honest tension — decided.** "One size fits all" can accidentally sound like "come as you are and stay exactly as you are." The gospel's actual claim is bigger: it fits you as you are but doesn't leave you as it found you. Chose to add ONE turn near the end — it fits you as you are, then begins to change your size — so the piece is a door AND a promise, not only a door.

**Form — decided.** Kept compact and poem-like, not a long explainer. Short lines, repetition, the list as a drumbeat, one small breath in the middle before returning. Built to be spoken slowly to camera or over music.

---

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

The gospel of Christ is one size fits all.

And you know how "one size fits all" usually goes. It's the lie on the label. It means it fits nobody. Too tight on one, hanging off another, made for no one in particular so it belongs to no one at all.

But this one is different. This is the one place the phrase is finally telling the truth. It fits all. And it fits you. Not the general shape of you. You.

So whatever your size is.

Tired. Come.
Broken. Come.
Weary, and running on empty. Come.
Loved, and somehow still not full. Come.
Unloved, and starving for it. Come.
Shamed. Come.
Timid. Guilty. Ashamed of things nobody knows. Come.
Bruised. Beaten. Abused.
Betrayed by the ones who should have covered you. Come.
Offended, and still holding it. Come.
Angry. Bitter. Tired of pretending you're fine. Come.
Anxious. Overwhelmed. Scared of what tomorrow is carrying. Come.
Grieving. Lonely. Forgotten in a room full of people. Come.
Restless. Searching. Tired of searching. Come.
Doubting, and not sure you're even allowed to be here. Come.

And the ones we always forget to call.

Whole. Steady. Unbroken.
Strong. Capable. Together.
The one who looks like they need nothing.
The one everyone else leans on.
There is a size here for you too. Come.

Because it was never only cut for the wounded. That's the part people miss. It fits the one who's falling apart, and it fits the one who's holding it together, and it turns out those are the same person on different days anyway.

So step into it.

Not clean yourself up and then step into it. Not become the right size and then step into it. Step in as you are, at the size you are, on the day you're having.

It fits. And then it goes to work.

Because it doesn't just fit you. It does something to you.

It clothes the tired, and then it carries the tiredness.
It covers the shamed, and then it lifts the shame off.
It holds the broken, and then it starts to mend what broke.
It reaches the fallen, and then it raises them up.
It meets the guilty, and then it washes the guilt away.
It finds the lonely, and then it stays.
It sits with the grieving, and then it wipes the eyes.
It receives the bitter, and then it softens the heart.
It welcomes the doubting, and then it steadies the ground under them.
It takes you exactly as you came, and then it refuses to leave you that way.

It fills. Every empty place you've been carrying, it fills. Him who fills all, in all.

I won't lie to you and tell you it leaves you the way it found you. It fits you as you are. And then, quietly, it begins to change your size. It doesn't wait for you to change before it fits. It fits first. And the fitting is what changes you.

One size. Every size. Your size.

Come and step into it. It fits all, and it fills all in all.

## REFERENCES
- **Ephesians 1:22–23** — the church as his body, "the fullness of him who fills all in all." Her closing line is this scripture; represented accurately.
- **Matthew 11:28** — "Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest." The "come / step in" invitation shape.
- **Romans 13:14 / Galatians 3:27** — "clothe yourselves with the Lord Jesus Christ" / "clothed yourselves with Christ." The garment image is scriptural, not invented — the piece just makes it plain.

## PRODUCTION NOTES
- This is spoken-word, not an essay. Keep it compact; resist padding. Best delivered slowly, to camera or over a soft bed of music.
- The list is a drumbeat — the repeated "Come." is the pulse. Don't rush it; let the one-word lines land individually.
- The load-bearing move is including the *unbroken*: "Whole. Steady. Unbroken. The one who looks like they need nothing." Without it the piece is only for the hurting. Keep it.
- The one theological turn at the end ("it begins to change your size") is deliberate — it makes it a door AND a promise. If you ever want the purely-invitational cut, that final paragraph lifts out cleanly.
- Pull-quotes: "It fits you as you are. And the fitting is what changes you." · "One size. Every size. Your size."
- Bruised/beaten/abused and betrayed/abused lines: deliver gently and keep moving — same principle as the sensitive beats in other kits, don't dwell.
- **Two-movement structure (extended version):** the piece now has two litanies that mirror each other. First litany = the sizes ("Tired. Come."), a summons. Second litany = what it does ("It clothes the tired, and then it carries the tiredness"), a promise. The first calls people in as they are; the second shows what happens once they're in. Deliver the first building/gathering, the second slower and settling. The "fits first, then changes" turn is the hinge between call and transformation — don't rush past it.
- Each fits/then-does line is a two-beat: the fit, a tiny pause, then the work. That pause is the whole effect. On camera, let it breathe.
