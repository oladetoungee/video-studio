# KIT 36: Should Couples Build an Online Presence Around Their Marriage?

**Premise:** I genuinely don't think it's healthy for couples to build a shared digital presence out of their marriage. I've never been married, so this is from observation, not experience. But I believe marriage is already fragile and sacred, even with no audience. Add content, an audience, and your relationship as material, and I don't think people think it through. People underestimate how much pressure the digital world puts on us as individuals, talk less of relationships.

When it comes to marriage and digital presence, I genuinely don't think it's healthy for couples to have a couple digital presence. It's just too fragile a position, even without an audience.

Even without an audience, marriage, I believe... I do not know. I do not know this out of experience, but from observation, I believe marriage in itself is a fragile relationship. ## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**What she's actually against.** Not couples posting a photo together, or mentioning their spouse. She's against building a *presence* out of the marriage: a shared brand, a couple channel, the relationship itself as the content. The script makes this distinction early so it doesn't read as "never post your partner."

**Her honesty is her strength.** "I've never been married, I know this from observation." Kept near the top. It earns trust and invites married viewers to answer rather than dismiss her.

**Why it adds pressure (plain mechanisms, no studies needed):**
- **Fights become material.** A normal argument now has an audience, or gets hidden to keep the content going. Either way, the couple stops working things out just between themselves.
- **Strangers get a vote.** Comments weigh in on your husband's tone or your wife's choices. Thousands of opinions in a space meant for two.
- **Money tied to the marriage.** If the income depends on the couple, changing anything about the relationship, even taking a break from filming, affects the business. That makes it harder to be honest about how you're really doing.
- **Performing happiness.** On a bad week, the camera still expects the good version. Living one marriage and posting another is a strain.
- **The ending is public too.** If it falls apart, the breakup becomes content as well. We've all seen couple accounts announce a split. Kept general, no names.
- **Kids, if they come.** They didn't choose an audience.

**Her bigger point.** The digital world already pressures individuals (comparison, performing, opinions). A marriage has two people's pressure plus the relationship itself.

**Honest guard.** Some couples share their lives online and seem to handle it well, often by keeping clear limits on what's private. Her point is her belief about the risk, not a verdict on every couple who does it. The close turns this into questions to ask before starting.

**Faith.** Not added. Her word "sacred" carries the weight without a verse. Can add one later if wanted.

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice, teleprompter format)

**[OPEN]**

When it comes to marriage and being online, I'm going to say something some people won't like.

I genuinely don't think it's healthy for couples to build an online presence out of their marriage.

And let me be honest from the start.

I've never been married.

So I don't know this from experience.

I know it from watching.

**[SECTION 1: WHAT I DON'T MEAN]**

When I say couples shouldn't build a presence out of their marriage, I don't mean you can't post your husband.

Or put up a picture with your wife on your anniversary.

I mean making the marriage itself the content.

A couple channel.

A shared brand.

Your relationship as the thing you post about every week.

That's what I'm talking about.

**[SECTION 2: MARRIAGE IS ALREADY FRAGILE]**

From what I've seen, marriage is already a fragile relationship.

Even without an audience.

Even with nobody watching.

Two people, learning to live with each other, every day, for the rest of their lives.

And I think marriage is so fragile and so sacred.

Which is a weird thing for me to say, because I've never been married.

But that's how I see it.

**[RETENTION BEAT, around 1:00: Now add an audience to that, and here's what I think actually happens.]**

**[SECTION 3: FIGHTS BECOME CONTENT]**

When a marriage becomes content, even normal fights change.

Every couple argues.

But now, either the argument has an audience.

Or you have to hide it so the content can keep going.

Either way, you're no longer working it out just between the two of you.

**[SECTION 4: STRANGERS GET A VOTE]**

When you put your marriage online, strangers start having opinions about it.

Someone in the comments thinks your husband's tone was rude.

Someone else thinks you should have handled it differently.

Thousands of opinions, in something that was meant for two people.

**[SECTION 5: WHEN THE MONEY DEPENDS ON THE MARRIAGE]**

And when a couple channel starts making money, it gets even heavier.

Now the income depends on the relationship looking a certain way.

So if you need a break, or something is wrong, it's not just your marriage that's affected.

It's the business too.

That makes it so much harder to be honest about how you're really doing.

**[SECTION 6: PERFORMING HAPPINESS]**

There will be bad weeks.

Every marriage has them.

But the camera still expects the good version.

So you live one marriage, and you post another one.

And I think that slowly wears people down.

**[RETENTION BEAT, around 3:00: And there's one more part people really don't think about.]**

**[SECTION 7: IF IT ENDS, THAT'S PUBLIC TOO]**

If a marriage that's built on content falls apart, the ending becomes content too.

We've all seen couple accounts post that they're separating.

And then everyone has something to say about that as well.

And if there are children, they're part of it, even though they never chose an audience.

**[SECTION 8: THE DIGITAL WORLD IS ALREADY HEAVY]**

I think people underestimate how much pressure the digital world puts on us.

Even as individuals.

The comparison.

The opinions.

The feeling that you always have to show up.

That's heavy for one person.

Talk less of a relationship, where it's two people's pressure, plus the marriage itself.

**[SECTION 9: I COULD BE WRONG]**

Now, I know some couples share their lives online and seem to handle it well.

Usually, they keep very clear limits on what stays private.

So I'm not saying every couple who does this is doomed.

I'm saying I believe it's a very risky place to put something this fragile.

And I don't think people think it through.

So if you're thinking about it, ask yourselves a few things first.

What stays just between us?

What happens to the channel if we need a break?

And would we be okay if this all became public?

If you're married and you've thought about this, I'd really like to hear from you in the comments.
