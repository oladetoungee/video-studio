**Premise:** You cannot be a teacher without being a learner, and it isn't a season you graduate from. If you are to keep teaching, you have to keep learning. ## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**Her line has a two-thousand-year-old twin.** Seneca, in his seventh letter to Lucilius, wrote "homines dum docent discunt," people learn while they teach. Usually shortened to *docendo discimus*. Her thought arrived at the same place on its own, which is worth saying out loud.

**Reason one: teaching exposes the holes.** You don't really know something until you have to explain it. Psychologists Rozenblit and Keil named the "illusion of explanatory depth" (2002): people rate their understanding of everyday things (a zipper, a flush toilet) highly, then rate it much lower after trying to explain how they work step by step. Teaching forces that step-by-step explanation constantly, so it keeps finding gaps, and filling gaps is learning.

**Reason two: the subject moves.** She teaches tech, where what was best practice a few years ago is already old. A teacher who stops learning keeps teaching, but teaches the past. True beyond tech too: medicine, language, even how children learn.

**Reason three: the students move, and they teach back.** New learners bring new questions and angles you've never stood in. That's the humbling part, and the most human.

**"Teacher" is bigger than classrooms.** Parents, managers, mentors, older siblings, pastors, team leads, creators. Somebody learns from how you live whether you planned it or not. This widens the audience without changing the point.

**Honest guard.** This isn't "don't teach until you know everything." You teach what you know today and stay a learner while you do it. The practical form is being able to say "I don't know that one, let me find out," and then actually finding out.

**Faith, light.** Luke 2:52, Jesus "increased in wisdom." If even Jesus grew, nobody has arrived. Proverbs 9:9, "teach a just man, and he will increase in learning." And the New Testament word for disciple, *mathētēs*, means learner. The people who went on to teach the world were called learners first.

**Standalone sections.** Each section re-anchors to "you can't teach without learning" in its first lines, so any cut makes sense to a stranger.

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice, teleprompter format)

**[OPEN]**

To teach is to learn.

You cannot be a teacher without being a learner.

And it's not the kind of learning that you do for a certain period of time and then you graduate from.

No.

If you are to continuously teach, you have to continuously learn.

**[SECTION 1: YOU DON'T KNOW IT UNTIL YOU EXPLAIN IT]**

One reason you can't teach without learning is very simple.

You don't really know something until you have to explain it to someone else.

In your head, everything makes sense.

Then somebody asks you one small question, why, and you realise you only knew the outside of it.

There's actually a name for this.

Researchers call it the illusion of explanatory depth.

People think they understand how ordinary things work, like a zipper or a toilet, until someone asks them to explain it step by step.

And then they find out they don't.

So every time you teach, you find the holes in what you know.

And then you have to go and fill them.

That's learning.

**[SECTION 2: THE THING YOU TEACH KEEPS MOVING]**

Another reason teachers have to keep learning is that the thing you teach doesn't stand still.

I teach tech on my channel, and I can tell you, what was true a few years ago is already old.

Tools change.

Best practices change.

If I stopped learning, I would still be teaching.

But I'd be teaching the past.

And it's not only tech.

Medicine changes.

Language changes.

Even the way children learn changes.

The teacher who stops learning keeps giving the same answers, even when the questions have changed.

**[SECTION 3: YOUR STUDENTS TEACH YOU]**

This next one is my favourite reason why to teach is to learn.

Your students teach you.

If you've ever taught anyone anything, you know this.

Somebody asks a question you never thought to ask.

Somebody sees it from an angle you have never stood in.

And suddenly, the teacher is the one learning.

There's a line from Seneca, a Roman writer, from about two thousand years ago.

He wrote that while people teach, they learn.

Two thousand years, and it's still true.

**[SECTION 4: YOU ARE PROBABLY A TEACHER]**

When I say you can't be a teacher without being a learner, I don't only mean classroom teachers.

If you're a parent, you're teaching.

If you're a manager, a mentor, an older sister, a pastor, or a team lead, you're teaching.

Somebody is learning from how you live, whether you planned it or not.

So this is for all of us.

**[SECTION 5: YOU DON'T HAVE TO KNOW EVERYTHING FIRST]**

Now, I don't want anyone to hear "keep learning" and think they can't teach until they know everything.

You can teach what you know today.

Just stay a learner while you do it.

Be the kind of teacher who can say, I don't know that one, let me find out.

And then actually go and find out.

**[SECTION 6: EVEN JESUS GREW]**

This part really humbles me.

The Bible says that Jesus increased in wisdom.

That's Luke chapter 2, verse 52.

If even Jesus grew, who am I to think I've arrived?

Proverbs 9:9 says, teach a just man, and he will increase in learning.

And the word the Bible uses for disciple, in Greek, is mathētēs.

It means learner.

The people who went on to teach the whole world were called learners first.

And they never stopped.

**[CLOSE]**

So if you teach anything, in any form, at all, keep learning.

Not for a season.

Not until you graduate.

For as long as you teach.

To teach is to learn.
