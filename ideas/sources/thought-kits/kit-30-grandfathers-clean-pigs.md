# KIT 30: My Grandfather Had Clean Pigs

**Premise:** A video about pigs actually being clean brought back a memory of her late grandfather, who raised pigs and proudly showed them to her and her cousins on his seventieth birthday. His pigs were clean. Pigs are mostly as clean as the care they get, so clean pigs were a quiet record of a man who took care of things every day. A short, soft remembrance that ends on her own line.

**Register:** her dictated version is the base (minimum effective edit). Short, tender, a little funny. One small addition (why clean pigs mean care) and the two verses she asked for, Proverbs 12:10 and Proverbs 10:7. Nothing after her ending.

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

I saw a video not so long talking about how pigs are actually clean and how the only reason they get dirty is because they are hot and all of that, and then I remembered my grandfather. You know, my grandfather raised pigs. He raised pigs, and I remember the day we went to visit.

It was his seventieth birthday and we had all gone to celebrate this birthday with him. And he had taken some of us, I think it was me and a few of my cousins, to the backyard of his house. And he showed us his pigs. My grandfather's pigs were clean.

My grandfather had clean pigs. Today I remember my grandfather. May his soul rest in peace. But most importantly, today I remember that my grandfather had clean pigs.

*(Follow-up: yes to building it; add the two scriptures that come to mind, the one about taking care of animals and the one about the sweet remembrance of the righteous.)*

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**The ending is the whole piece.** "Today I remember my grandfather. May his soul rest in peace. But most importantly, today I remember that my grandfather had clean pigs." Funny and tender at once, which is how real grief often sounds. The joke carries the love. Don't explain it and don't add anything after it.

**The video is right, and it makes the memory mean more.** Pigs can barely sweat, so they roll in mud mainly to cool down when they're hot. Given shade, water and enough room, they keep their sleeping and eating places clean and go to the toilet away from them. A pig is mostly as clean as the care it gets. So his clean pigs weren't luck. They were a record of daily work: cleaning, water, space, attention, long before anyone came for a birthday.

**One small addition.** A few lines that let the listener see what she might only have felt as a child, that clean pigs meant a man who took care of things. No lesson, then straight back to her ending.

**The two verses she asked for.** Both are in Proverbs. Proverbs 12:10 (NKJV), "A righteous man regards the life of his animal," goes right after the lines about him caring for the pigs every day, so it names what the listener just saw. Proverbs 10:7 (NKJV), "The memory of the righteous is blessed," goes right before "Today I remember my grandfather," so it leads into her remembrance. She remembered right that the word "righteous" links them: 12:10 says "a righteous man" in the KJV and NKJV, and 10:7 says "the righteous" in the NKJV (the KJV has "the just"). Using NKJV for both keeps the shared word.

**Keep her details.** The seventieth birthday, everyone gathered, and him taking her and a few cousins to the backyard on purpose to show them the pigs. That last part says he was proud of them.

## REFERENCES
- **Proverbs 12:10 (NKJV):** "A righteous man regards the life of his animal." KJV: "A righteous man regardeth the life of his beast."
- **Proverbs 10:7 (NKJV):** "The memory of the righteous is blessed." KJV: "The memory of the just is blessed"; ESV: "The memory of the righteous is a blessing." Close alternative: Psalm 112:6, "the righteous will be remembered forever."
- **Pigs and mud:** pigs have very few functional sweat glands and wallow mainly to cool down (mud also protects their skin from sun and insects). Given space, they separate their dunging area from where they sleep and eat. General animal-science knowledge; the script says "mostly" so it doesn't overclaim.
