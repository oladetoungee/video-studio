# KIT 14 — You Might Just Be Comfortable

**Premise:** "People who aren't even doing as much as me are getting ahead" hides an assumption nobody checks: that you know how much they're doing. You don't. You're comparing your full backstage to their highlight reel, self-assessing in your own favour, and underrating how brutal execution actually is. Blunt, secular.

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

When people say things like, oh yeah, I need to put myself out there because people that are not even doing as much as me are getting ahead — I find that kind of… because what makes you think they are not doing better than you? What makes you think they are not putting in as much work? I feel like that's a level of self-assurance that I don't think is very advisable. If you see people doing things and the first thing that comes to your mind is "people that are not even doing as well as me" — what makes you think they're not as good as you? You are the one self-assessing in this case.

"Oh, they're getting more opportunity" — but what makes you think so? And I'm not saying this as an attack. But you don't want to start approaching things like that, because when we assess ourselves we tend to be a little biased towards ourselves. So are you sure? Maybe they're just there because they like to use social media, or they talk about themselves, or maybe they're actually doing real good work — and maybe you need to do better work. It might not be the case, but it could be.

*(Follow-up steer: fully secular, street-level, reasonably blunt, sprinkle only a little. Core point to centre: people don't understand how much effort it takes to execute. Thinking you *could* do the thing — or know how to do it better than someone already doing it — is not the same as doing it. Check that again, honestly.)*

---

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**The buried assumption.** The complaint rests on a measurement nobody verified: that you know how much they're doing. You see their *output* (posts, wins, visibility) and compare it to your *input* (effort, hours, unseen grind). You know your own backstage and only their front stage — so of course you win. The comparison is rigged by construction.

**The self-assessment tax.** She named the spine: when we assess ourselves, we're biased toward ourselves. Documented — most people rate themselves above average, statistically impossible for most people. So "they're not even as good as me" comes from the least reliable narrator on the subject of you: you. Not necessarily wrong — but the last person who can be trusted to make that call, held far too confidently.

**The centre she asked for — execution.** People don't grasp how much effort execution takes. Knowing how to do a thing and doing it are separated by a huge, ugly gap full of everything nobody enjoys: consistency, showing up unmotivated, finishing, the unsexy 90% after the idea. Most never cross it, and from the wrong side they look at someone who did and think "that's not even hard." Response: then do it — the lane's wide open.

**The reasons people give are secretly descriptions of work.** "They just like social media" → showing up publicly and consistently is an uncomfortable skill most people won't build, which is why it's valuable — not luck. "They just talk about themselves" → that's distribution, half the job now. Great work in silence doesn't lose because it's worse; it loses because you skipped half the game and called the other person a show-off for playing it.

**The honesty guard (don't overcorrect).** Sometimes it *is* luck — connections, timing, head start, algorithm. Pretending all success is pure merit is its own delusion. But: the "they're just lucky/undeserving" story feels good and teaches nothing (if it's luck, you can only wait). The "they're doing something I can't see" story feels worse but is the only one with a door — it hands you an action. So pick the useful story, not because it's always true, but because self-flattery is a dead end and curiosity is a map.

**The landing.** When "they're not even doing as much as me" appears, that's not information, it's comfort. Comfort is fine; don't mistake it for analysis. The ones who catch up got suspicious of the thought that felt too good. Check it again, honestly — you might be right, or you might just be comfortable.

---

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

When people say things like, I need to put myself out there, because people who aren't even doing as much as me are getting ahead, I always want to stop them right there. Because there's a hidden move inside that sentence and I don't think they notice they're making it.

You said people who are not even doing as much as you. Okay. How do you know?

How do you actually know how much they're doing? You don't. You can't. What you're looking at is their output. The posts, the wins, the opportunities, the visibility. And you're comparing that to your input. Your effort, your hours, your struggle that nobody sees. So you're weighing everything you know about your own work against the small visible tip of theirs, and then announcing that you're doing more. Of course you came out on top. You rigged the comparison without noticing. You know your whole backstage. You only see their stage.

And there's a second thing hiding in there. You are the one doing the assessment. You're the one deciding they're not as good as you. And here's the uncomfortable fact: when human beings assess themselves, they lean in their own favour. Almost everybody thinks they're above average, which obviously cannot be true for almost everybody. So the judgement "they're not even as good as me" is coming from the single most biased source available on the subject of you, which is you. That doesn't automatically make you wrong. It just means you are the last person who should be trusted to make that call, and you're making it very confidently.

But here's the part I actually want to get to, because I think this is the real thing.

I don't think people understand how much effort it takes to execute.

You think that because you can picture the thing, because you understand how it's done, because you could theoretically do it, that means you're already better at it than the person actually doing it. And those are not the same thing. Not even close. Knowing how to do something and doing it are separated by a massive, brutal gap, and that gap is full of everything nobody enjoys. Consistency. Showing up when you don't feel like it. Finishing. Doing the unsexy ninety percent after the exciting idea. Most people never cross that gap, and from the wrong side of it, they look at someone who did and go, that's not even hard, I could do that.

Then do it. Genuinely. If it's that easy and they're that mediocre, the lane is wide open, go.

And look at the reasons people give for why the other person is ahead, because they always give the same ones, and they're always secretly describing work. Oh, they just like using social media. Okay, so they show up online consistently and talk about their work in public, which is uncomfortable and most people can't do it, and that's exactly why it's valuable. That's not luck, that's a skill you're refusing to build and then resenting. Oh, they just talk about themselves a lot. Yeah, that's called distribution, and it's half the job now. You might be doing genuinely great work in total silence, and great work that nobody hears about doesn't lose because it's worse. It loses because you skipped a whole half of the game and called the other person a show-off for playing it.

Now let me be fair, because it's not always earned. Sometimes it really is luck. Sometimes it's a connection, timing, a head start, an algorithm that decided to like them that week. That's real, and pretending everything is pure merit is its own delusion. But notice something. The version where they're just lucky and undeserving, that story feels good and it teaches you absolutely nothing. If they're only ahead because of luck, then there's nothing for you to do but sit and wait for your own turn. The version where they're doing something you're not seeing feels worse, but it's the only one that hands you a door. It gives you something to actually do.

So even if you're not sure which one is true, pick the useful one. Not because it's always accurate, but because flattering yourself is a dead end and getting curious is a map.

The moment "they're not even doing as much as me" shows up in your head, be honest about what that is. That's not information. That's comfort. And comfort is fine, we all need it sometimes, but do not confuse it for analysis. The people who actually catch up are the ones who heard that thought, noticed how nice it felt, and got suspicious of it.

So next time you catch yourself sizing somebody up and deciding you're the one who's really doing the work, just check that again. Honestly. You might be right. But you might just be comfortable.
