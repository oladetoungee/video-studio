# KIT 24 — Come Back to Your First Love

**Premise:** A plea, not an argument. Please, open a book today. Confessional (she's drifted from it too and wants back to her "first love"). The freshest reframe: reading gives your overwhelmed mind ONE single thing to hold — it's the rest, not another task. And it's the one kind of consuming that's actually creating.

**Register:** soft but urgent — aggressive in *meaning*, not volume. Keep the pleading intensity ("I'm begging you"), the repetition, the confession, the "I'd arrest you people." Faith: one light touch on "first love."

**Relationship to Kit 08:** Kit 08 ("Stop Policing Reading") is the ARGUMENT against gatekeepers. Kit 24 is the PLEA to the person who stopped reading; policing demoted to a short closing jab. Different pieces — keep both. Fusable into one flagship reading video on request.

---

## 1. THE ORIGINAL (what I said first)

I'm begging you with everything that I have. Please open a book and read. Today, get yourself a book, read a few pages. Buy a new book, get it from a library, borrow it from a friend, thrift a secondhand book. Just pick a book and read. I don't want it to sound aggressive — I mean it aggressively. Please read a book. You need to force yourself to read a book.

There's so much that reading gives you. It forces your mind to work. Reading is such an active activity for your mind. It opens up your mind in a way that's so beautiful. I don't do it as much as I used to, and I want to get to the point where I can do it as much as I used to. I want to get back to my first love.

If you think you're overwhelmed by things in your life, pick a book and read. Bury yourself in a book — academic, fiction, self-help, whatever, genuinely doesn't matter. Reading takes away the noise in my head and gives me one single thing to grab, one single thing to hold, enough for my mind to actually settle down.

Another thing I love is interacting with the author. You can argue with them and not voice it out. Paint pictures in your head. Critique the writing. Agree, disagree. Write and imagine. Mark your books. That's one thing I don't like about the library — I always get a book from the library, and I just want to mark it but I can't; I do that for my own books. Put a question mark next to something that doesn't sit right. Get a quote from a fiction book that just speaks to you.

We're all running to wherever it is we don't know these days. Everybody's productive, catching up with the news, what's going on, what do I learn, how do I do this. But please take some minutes out of your day — ten pages, twenty, five. Don't know what to read? Doesn't matter, pick anything. If you can't focus — honestly sometimes I can't either — that's exactly the reason to read, not to avoid it. Reading helps you build focus. And for people policing reading — "if you read fiction you're not serious, self-help you're not this" — come on, I'd arrest you guys one of these days. We're not reading enough as a society; we have a reading deficiency; we read so little it doesn't even make sense to police the activity.

## 2. THE EXPLORATION (before the final)

**Emotional core: this is CONFESSION, not correction.** What keeps it from preachy is that she's not above anyone — she's stopped too, wants back to her first love. The plea is her talking to herself as much as to them. That's the warmth: not "read, you philistines," but "come back with me." A hand held out, not a lecture.

**"First love" is the phrase to build around (light scriptural echo).** Revelation 2:4–5 — "you have forsaken the love you had at first… remember, and do the things you did at first." Exactly her arc: a first love, drifted from, and the way back isn't guilt — it's just returning. One light touch, not forced.

**Best original insight = the spine: reading gives your mind ONE single thing to hold.** The opposite of the usual pitch (reading makes you smarter/productive). Truer and rarer: reading quiets the noise by giving an overloaded mind exactly one thing to grab. It's the antidote to the overwhelm she circles in her other kits. Not more input — the relief of single-tasking your own head. Connects reading to REST, not work — which is what reaches the person too tired to read: reading IS the rest.

**Active-mind point, sharpened.** A screen hands you the picture, voice, pacing — you receive. A book hands you only symbols; your mind builds the whole world (faces, rooms, voices — "paint pictures in your head"). Reading is the one "consuming" that's actually CREATING. Everything else pours into you; reading makes you build. That's why it feels fuller and quieter afterward.

**Interacting-with-the-author — keep almost verbatim, it's very her.** Arguing silently, agreeing, disagreeing, marking the book, the question mark by the line that doesn't sit right, the quote that grabs you. Proof reading is a conversation, not passive absorption. The library-marking detail (wants to mark, can't) is a charming true-reader tell — keep it.

**Focus point needs her honest twist (it's everyone's objection).** "I can't focus long enough to read." Her right answer: the not-focusing is the REASON to read, not to avoid it — reading rebuilds the focus you lost. Like being unfit and refusing to exercise: the inability is the symptom, reading is the cure; push through the early slipping — that's the muscle firing again. Her admission that she sometimes can't either IS the permission — keep it.

**Policing note = short closing jab only** (Kit 08 carries the full argument). One or two warm/affectionate lines: doesn't matter what you read; we read so little that policing the remainder makes no sense; "I'd arrest you people." Then back to the plea — don't let it take over or the piece loses its softness and becomes Kit 08.

**Landing = back to the outstretched hand.** Not "be better" — "come back to this with me." Five pages, today, any book. Because it gave her one quiet thing to hold, and she wants that back, and wants it for them. End on the PLEA, not the argument — the plea is the true register.

**Decisions:** faith = one light "first love"/Revelation touch; keep raw pleading intensity, repetition, "I'm begging you," "I'd arrest you"; confessional warmth throughout.

## 3. THE FINAL (spoken, in my voice)

I'm begging you. With everything I have, I'm begging you. Please, open a book and read.

And I know how that sounds. I don't want it to come across as aggressive. I'm not being aggressive. But I do mean it aggressively, if that makes sense. It's coming from a soft place, but I mean it with my whole chest. Please. Read a book.

Today. Get yourself a book today. Buy a new one. Borrow one from a friend. Get one from the library. Thrift a secondhand one, they cost almost nothing. It genuinely does not matter how you get it. Just pick a book, and read a few pages.

And let me be honest with you first, because I'm not standing above anybody here. I don't read as much as I used to. I don't. And I miss it. I want to get back to the point where I read the way I used to read, and this is me trying to walk myself back too. I want to return to my first love. Because that's what it was for me, a first love. And you know how it is with a first love, you don't leave because you stopped loving it, you just drift, life gets loud, and one day you look up and you're just, not there anymore. And the way back is not to feel guilty about it. The way back is just to return. Remember what it gave you, and go do the thing you used to do.

So let me tell you what it gives, in case you forgot too.

Here's the one I think about the most. If you feel overwhelmed, if your head is full and pulled in a hundred directions, pick up a book and read. Because reading gives your mind one single thing to hold. That's the thing nobody tells you. Your whole day, your mind is being dragged everywhere at once, this notification, that worry, this task, that noise. And a book just says, no. Just this. Just this one line. Just this one page. It takes all the noise in your head and hands you one thing to grab onto, and it's enough for your mind to finally settle down. It's not another task. It's the rest. If you're too tired to read, that's actually the tiredness reading was made for.

And it's an active thing, reading, that's the other part. Because a screen hands you everything. The picture, the voice, the pace, all of it, you just sit there and receive it. But a book only gives you symbols on a page, and your own mind has to build the entire world out of them. The faces. The rooms. The voices. You paint the pictures yourself, in your own head. So reading is the one kind of consuming that is actually creating. You're not watching someone else's images. You're making your own. That's why you feel different after a book than after three hours of scrolling. One pours into you. The other one makes you build.

And this is the part I love the most, honestly. When you read, you're interacting with the author. You can argue with them, in your head, without saying a word out loud. You can agree with them. You can completely disagree. You can critique the writing, this line is beautiful, this part doesn't work. You can put a little question mark next to something and go, mm, no, this one doesn't sit right with me. You can find one quote in a novel that just, ahh, that somehow says the exact thing you never had words for, and you mark it. You can write in the margins. You can imagine. It's a whole conversation, and you're both people in it.

That's actually my one problem with library books, and I always get books from the library, but I want to mark them so badly and I can't. So I do all my marking in my own books instead. Underlining, question marks, little notes. If you've never done it, try it, it changes how you read. It stops being something happening to you and becomes something you're doing.

And I know. I know we're all running somewhere these days, and half of us don't even know where. Everybody's productive, everybody's catching up on the news, what's happening, what's happening, what do I need to learn, how do I do this, how do I keep up. I get it. But please. Take a few minutes out of that. Ten pages. Twenty. Five, even. You don't know what to read? Doesn't matter. Pick anything. Literally anything.

And if you're thinking, but I can't focus long enough to read, listen. Sometimes I can't either. That's not the reason to avoid it. That is the exact reason to do it. The not being able to focus is the symptom, and the reading is the cure. It's like being out of shape and using that as your reason not to exercise, when being out of shape is the whole reason to start. Reading is how you build the focus back. So when your attention keeps slipping off the page in the beginning, that's not you failing. That's the muscle firing again for the first time in a while. Push through that part. It comes back.

And quickly, to the people who like to police it, oh, if you read fiction you're not serious, if you read self-help you're not deep, come on. Come on. Academic, fiction, self-help, romance, whatever it is, it does not matter, just read the thing. We are reading so little as a whole society, we have an actual reading deficiency, that it makes no sense to stand around policing the tiny bit of reading that's left. I'll deal with you people another day.

But you, right now, this is my plea. Just pick a book. Today. Any book, any way you can get it. Five pages. Because it will give you back something the world keeps quietly taking from you, one quiet thing to hold, and your own mind, built by your own hands, settling down at last.

Please. Go and read. I'm begging you.

## REFERENCES
- **Revelation 2:4–5** — "you have forsaken the love you had at first… remember therefore from where you have fallen… and do the things you did at first." The "first love" / return arc. One light touch; not quoted verbatim in delivery.
- **Reading as active world-building vs passive reception** — a well-established idea in reading/media psychology (readers generate imagery, infer, simulate; the "picture in your head" is the reader's own construction). Used as accurate general framing, not tied to one study.
- **Focus is rebuilt by reading** — consistent with attention-as-trainable; the "symptom is the reason, not the excuse" framing is her argument, presented as encouragement not clinical claim.
- **Reading deficiency / we read very little** — see Kit 08 for the hard stats (Pew/YouGov: a large share of adults read no book in a year). Not cited in this piece's body (it's a plea, not a data argument); the numbers live in Kit 08.
